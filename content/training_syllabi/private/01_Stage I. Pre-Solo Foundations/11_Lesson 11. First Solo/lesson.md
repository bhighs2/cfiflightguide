# Lesson 11. First Solo

**Lesson:** 11  
**Stage:** Stage I — Pre-Solo Foundations  
**Type:** SOLO  
**Target Proficiency:** Proficient — solo

## Objective

Conduct the learner's first solo flight in the C172S at KDWH. The lesson has two parts: first, the learner demonstrates **3–5 good touch-and-go landings with the instructor aboard** to confirm the day's readiness; then, with all pre-solo endorsements complete, the instructor steps out — monitoring by radio — and the learner flies **3 full-stop solo landings** in the pattern.

## Completion Standards

By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:

- Complete a thorough preflight and surface operation as pilot in command.
- Demonstrate 3–5 good touch-and-go landings with the instructor aboard, confirming readiness for solo.
- Fly the KDWH pattern and land, solo, as the sole occupant — 3 full-stop landings.
- Apply all Stage I skills without anyone in the right seat: preflight, taxi, radio, takeoff, pattern, landing, and go-around judgment.
- Operate within the student-pilot limitations and the instructor's solo-endorsement boundaries.
- Make the go/no-go and go-around decisions alone.

## Why This Matters

The first solo is the milestone the entire stage has built toward. Every prior lesson — preflight, surface operations, the pattern, airspace, slow flight and stalls, ground reference, emergencies, crosswind — converges here, in a cockpit with one seat occupied. The two-part structure protects the milestone: the instructor first confirms the day's readiness with supervised touch-and-gos, then steps out and lets the learner fly.

Solo does not mean unsupported. The instructor monitors by radio, and the learner knows that the same discipline that governed every dual lesson — preflight thoroughly, fly the airplane first, go around when it is not right — governs now. The only difference is that the decisions are the learner's alone.

## Knowledge

### 1. The Two Parts of the First Solo

The lesson is deliberately structured in two parts:

1. **Supervised confirmation** — 3–5 touch-and-go landings with the instructor aboard. This confirms the day's readiness: the wind, the weather, the learner's state, and the landings themselves.
2. **The solo** — with all endorsements complete, the instructor steps out, monitors by radio, and the learner flies 3 full-stop landings in the pattern.

The touch-and-gos are the gate. Only when they are good does the instructor step out.

**Source:**

[[pdf: /knowledge/14_CFR_Part_61.pdf | start=68 | end=73]]

### 2. Solo Authority and Limitations

On the solo, the learner is the pilot in command and the sole occupant. The student-pilot limitations of § 61.89 and the instructor's endorsement boundaries govern the flight:

- No passengers, no flight for compensation.
- Only within the endorsed area and any limitations the instructor has set.
- No night or cross-country without the specific endorsement.
- The visibility and visual-reference requirements apply.

**Source:**

[[pdf: /knowledge/14_CFR_Part_61.pdf | start=68 | end=73]]

[[pdf: /knowledge/AC_61-65K_Certification.pdf | start=40 | end=41]]

### 3. Solo Discipline

The discipline of every dual lesson applies unchanged:

- Preflight thoroughly — no one else will catch what you miss.
- Fly the airplane first — aviate, navigate, communicate.
- The go-around is always available and always correct when the approach is not right.
- The instructor is on the radio, but does not fly the airplane.

## Risk Management

### Readiness Assumed, Not Confirmed

#### Hazard

The solo proceeds on schedule or expectation rather than on the day's actual readiness.

#### Consequence

A learner soloing on a day when the wind, weather, or their own state is not right.

#### Recognition

Poor touch-and-go landings, gusty or strong crosswind, a nervous or fatigued learner, or a sense of "we're doing it today."

#### Mitigation

The 3–5 touch-and-gos are a genuine readiness gate. If they are not clean — or conditions or the learner's state are off — the solo waits. There is no schedule pressure on a first solo.

### Startle at the Empty Seat

#### Hazard

The reality of being alone in the airplane startles the learner on the first solo takeoff or landing.

#### Consequence

A momentary freeze or a rushed, degraded action at a critical moment.

#### Recognition

Hesitation, a missed call, or a rushed action on the first solo circuit.

#### Mitigation

Brief the feeling in advance. The first circuit is flown exactly as trained. If anything is not right, the learner goes around and talks to the instructor on the radio.

### External Pressure and Celebration Distraction

#### Hazard

Family watching, a camera, or the significance of the day distracts from the flying.

#### Consequence

Attention drawn away from preflight, the pattern, or the landing at the moment it matters most.

#### Recognition

Head out of the cockpit at the wrong time, a missed checklist item, or a distraction on final.

#### Mitigation

Brief that the flying comes first and the celebration comes after. The pattern is flown with the same outside-dominant scan as every lesson.

### Solo Judgment — Forcing the Landing

#### Hazard

Alone on final, the learner feels pressure to complete the landing rather than go around.

#### Consequence

A forced, unstable landing — the exact accident the training has worked to prevent.

#### Recognition

High, fast, off centerline, or drifting on final, continued instead of abandoned.

#### Mitigation

A solo go-around is always available and always correct. The instructor would rather talk through three go-arounds than watch one forced landing. Go around, rejoin, and land when it is right.

## Conduct of the First Solo

### Purpose

Conduct the first solo safely: confirm readiness with supervised touch-and-gos, then fly 3 full-stop solo landings with the instructor monitoring by radio.

### Procedure

1. Thorough preflight and a supervised pattern warm-up.
2. Fly 3–5 touch-and-go landings with the instructor aboard. Confirm they are good — stable approaches, controlled touchdowns, correct pattern work.
3. Confirm the A.3 and A.4 endorsements are in the logbook; sign the A.6 solo endorsement with any limitations.
4. The instructor steps out with the handheld radio and takes a monitoring position.
5. The learner starts, taxis, and flies the pattern — 3 full-stop solo landings, taxiing back for each.
6. The instructor monitors each circuit and is available on the radio.
7. Full debrief of the first solo.

### Common Errors

- Proceeding to solo when the touch-and-gos were not clean.
- Skipping the endorsement check before the learner solos.
- The instructor without a working radio or a monitoring position.
- The learner forcing a landing rather than going around.
- Celebration or onlookers distracting from the flying.

### Completion Standard

The learner flies 3 full-stop solo landings to the Stage I standard, goes around whenever an approach is not right, and operates within the endorsement boundaries. The A.6 endorsement is signed.

## Scenario Application

### Scenario 1 — The Touch-and-Gos Are Not Clean

**Situation:**

On the supervised warm-up, two of the touch-and-go landings are unstable, and the wind is gustier than forecast.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What does the readiness gate require before the instructor steps out?
- Who decides whether the solo happens today?
- What is the right call if the warm-up is not good?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

The solo waits. The touch-and-gos are a real readiness gate — if they are not clean, or the conditions or the learner's state are off, the solo is postponed. There is no schedule on a first solo.

**Expected Performance:** Proficient.

### Scenario 2 — Alone on Final, Not Stabilized

**Situation:**

On your second solo circuit, you turn final high and fast, and it does not settle.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What is the correct action, alone on final?
- Is a solo go-around a failure?
- Who do you tell, and when?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Go around. A solo go-around is always available and always correct. Fly the go-around, then make the radio call, then rejoin and land when it is right. The instructor would rather hear about a go-around than watch a forced landing.

**Expected Performance:** Proficient.

### Scenario 3 — The Empty Seat on Takeoff

**Situation:**

On the first solo takeoff, the airplane feels lighter and climbs faster, and the empty right seat catches your attention.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What has changed, and what has not?
- Where does your attention belong?
- What do you do if something does not feel right?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

The airplane performs differently with less weight — expect it — but the procedures are unchanged. Attention belongs outside, on the flying. If anything is not right, fly the airplane, go around or land, and talk to the instructor on the radio.

**Expected Performance:** Proficient.

### Scenario 4 — The Crowd at the Fence

**Situation:**

Family is watching and a camera is out as you taxi for the solo.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What is the priority order right now?
- When does the celebration happen?
- How do you keep the significance of the day out of the cockpit?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Flying first, celebration after. The pattern is flown with the same outside-dominant scan and checklist discipline as every lesson. The day matters, but the landing matters more.

**Expected Performance:** Proficient.

# Flight Card

## Flight Setup

### Lesson Target

**Target Proficiency: Proficient — solo**

Primary lesson target:

**The first solo — 3 full-stop landings in the KDWH pattern, flown by the learner alone, after a supervised readiness check.**

Structure:

**Part 1: 3–5 supervised touch-and-go landings to confirm the day's readiness. Part 2: the instructor steps out with a handheld radio; the learner flies 3 full-stop solo landings.**

Boundary:

**The touch-and-gos are a real gate. If they are not clean — or the wind, weather, or the learner's state is not right — the solo waits. No schedule on a first solo.**

### Scope and Flexibility

This is the milestone, not a requirement to force it today.

If the warm-up, conditions, or readiness are not right:

- fly more supervised touch-and-gos,
- address whatever is not clean,
- and postpone the solo to a better day.

The instructor decides when to step out. The learner decides each takeoff, each landing, and every go-around.

---

## Part 1 — Supervised Readiness Check (Instructor Aboard)

#### Procedure

1. Thorough preflight as pilot in command.
2. Start, taxi, and runup with wind control and radio discipline (Ground 121.8, Tower 118.4).
3. Fly 3–5 touch-and-go landings in the pattern.
4. Instructor confirms each is good: stable approach, controlled touchdown, correct pattern, go-around judgment if needed.

#### Common Errors

- Rushing the preflight on a big day.
- Unstable touch-and-gos accepted as "good enough."
- Proceeding toward solo when the warm-up is not clean.

---

## The Transition

#### Procedure

1. Confirm the A.3 and A.4 endorsements are in the logbook.
2. Sign the A.6 solo endorsement with any limitations.
3. The learner taxies to parking or the run-up area; the instructor steps out with the handheld radio.
4. The instructor takes a monitoring position with the radio on the tower/CTAF frequency (118.4).
5. Brief the learner: fly it as trained, go around when it is not right, I am on the radio.

#### Common Errors

- Skipping the endorsement check before the learner solos.
- The instructor without a working radio or a clear monitoring position.
- Ambiguity about the frequency or the plan.

---

## Part 2 — The First Solo (Learner Alone)

#### Procedure

1. Start and taxi alone, with wind control and hold-short discipline.
2. Contact the tower and fly the pattern as trained.
3. Fly 3 full-stop landings — each one: stable approach, landing attitude, control through the roll, exit and clear, taxi back.
4. Go around whenever an approach is not right.
5. After the third full-stop, taxi to parking and shut down.

#### Common Errors

- Letting the empty seat or the occasion rush the flying.
- Forcing a landing rather than going around.
- Dropping the scan or the checklist with no one watching.
- Heads-down before clear of the runway.

---

## Return, Shutdown and Securing

#### Procedure

1. After the final landing: control, exit, fully clear, then checklist, reorient.
2. Taxi to parking; C172S shutdown, secure, postflight.
3. Log the solo flight time as pilot in command.

#### Common Errors

- Heads-down before clear of the runway.
- Forgetting to log the solo as PIC.

---

## Flight Completion Criteria

The first solo is satisfactory when the learner:

- completes a thorough preflight and safe surface operations as pilot in command,
- demonstrates 3–5 good supervised touch-and-go landings before the instructor steps out,
- flies 3 full-stop solo landings to the Stage I standard,
- goes around whenever an approach is not right,
- operates within the endorsement boundaries and student-pilot limitations,
- and lands safely, having flown the airplane — alone.

The instructor signs the AC 61-65K A.6 solo endorsement. The learner is now a solo pilot.
