# Lesson 8. Crosswind Operations and Landing Refinement

**Lesson:** 8  
**Stage:** Stage I — Pre-Solo Foundations  
**Type:** GROUND + FLIGHT  
**Target Proficiency:** Developing

## Objective

Develop the learner's ability to take off and land safely in a crosswind using both the crab method and the wing-low (sideslip) method, and to refine landings into consistent, controlled touchdowns. The learner will understand the C172S maximum demonstrated crosswind, judge when wind exceeds capability, and maintain directional control from the takeoff roll through touchdown and the after-landing roll.

## Completion Standards

By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:

- State the C172S maximum demonstrated crosswind velocity and explain what "demonstrated" means as a limitation.
- Read a crosswind-component chart and make a go/no-go decision against the airplane's capability.
- Perform a crosswind takeoff: full aileron into the wind initially, rudder to hold centerline, reducing aileron as speed builds, and lifting off into a crab.
- Perform a crosswind approach and landing using the crab method, transitioning to align the longitudinal axis with the runway before touchdown.
- Perform a crosswind approach and landing using the wing-low (sideslip) method: aileron into the wind to correct drift, opposite rudder to hold the longitudinal axis aligned with the runway.
- Explain and apply the core wing-low technique: rudder aligns the axis, aileron corrects the drift.
- Manage the increased drag and reduced vertical component of lift in a sideslip, protecting the stall margin.
- Maintain directional control through touchdown and the after-landing roll, with aileron into the wind.
- Refine landings toward consistent touchdowns on centerline, in a landing attitude, at the correct speed.

## Why This Matters

Most runways are not aligned with the wind most of the time, so crosswind operations are not an occasional specialty — they are the normal case. The crosswind landing is where ground-track control, energy management, coordination, and judgment all come together at the moment of least altitude and least margin. A pilot who cannot land in a crosswind is limited to calm days and aligned runways; a pilot who can is genuinely ready for solo operations in real conditions.

This lesson also carries a hard boundary of judgment: the C172S has a maximum demonstrated crosswind of 15 knots, and a demonstrated value is not a suggestion. Knowing when the wind exceeds the airplane's capability — or your own — and choosing another runway, another airport, or another day is as much a part of crosswind operations as the technique itself.

## Knowledge

### 1. The Maximum Demonstrated Crosswind

The C172S maximum demonstrated crosswind velocity is **15 knots**. "Demonstrated" means this is the crosswind component for which adequate control was actually shown during certification testing — it is not a guaranteed safe limit for every pilot in every condition. Gusts, turbulence, runway condition, and pilot proficiency all reduce the practical limit.

Read the crosswind-component chart to convert a reported wind at an angle to the runway into a crosswind component, then compare it to the demonstrated value and to your own ability. If it exceeds either, choose another runway, another airport, or another day.

**Source:**

[[pdf: /knowledge/POH-Cessna-172S.pdf | start=81 | end=81]]

[[pdf: /knowledge/Airplane_Flying_Handbook.pdf | start=189 | end=191]]

### 2. The Crosswind Takeoff

A crosswind tries to weathervane the airplane into the wind and to lift the upwind wing. The control positions counter both:

- **Aileron into the wind** — full aileron into the wind at the start of the roll keeps the upwind wing down.
- **Rudder for centerline** — hold the longitudinal axis parallel to the centerline, pointed straight ahead.
- **Reduce aileron as speed builds** — as the controls become effective, reduce the aileron to keep the wings level; too much aileron late in the roll produces a steep bank into the wind at lift-off.
- **Lift off into a crab** — after lift-off, establish a crab into the wind to hold the upwind ground track.

### 3. The Crab Method

In the crab method, the pilot holds a wings-level **crab into the wind** on final so the ground track stays on the centerline. The crab angle is maintained until just before touchdown, when the pilot uses rudder to align the longitudinal axis with the runway to avoid a side load.

- Crab holds the **ground track** — this is Lesson 6 wind correction applied to final.
- A common technique is to crab on the longer part of final and transition to the wing-low method before the round out.
- Aligning the axis too early or too late produces a side load.

**Source:**

[[pdf: /knowledge/Airplane_Flying_Handbook.pdf | start=186 | end=189]]

### 4. The Wing-Low (Sideslip) Method

In the wing-low method, the airplane's ground track and longitudinal axis stay aligned with the centerline throughout the approach, round out, touchdown, and after-landing roll.

The core technique — **rudder aligns the axis, aileron corrects the drift:**

1. Use **rudder** to align and hold the longitudinal axis with the runway direction.
2. The uncorrected crosswind now drifts the airplane; note the drift and oppose it with **aileron into the wind** — just enough bank to cancel the drift.
3. Adjust bank and rudder together as the crosswind changes to hold the centerline.
4. If full rudder cannot prevent a turn at the required bank, the wind is too strong for that runway — go elsewhere.

Because a sideslip increases drag and reduces the vertical component of lift, the sink rate rises and the stall margin shrinks. Manage airspeed with elevator and protect the margin — this is the Lesson 5 tie-in. In a strong crosswind, the C172S uses a reduced (minimum) flap setting.

**Source:**

[[pdf: /knowledge/Airplane_Flying_Handbook.pdf | start=186 | end=189]]

[[pdf: /knowledge/POH-Cessna-172S.pdf | start=109 | end=109]]

### 5. Touchdown, After-Landing Roll, and Turbulent Air

**Touchdown:** touch down with the longitudinal axis aligned with the runway and not drifting. In the wing-low method, touch down on the upwind main wheel first, then the other main, then the nosewheel.

**After-landing roll:** the crosswind is not finished at touchdown. Hold aileron into the wind through the roll to keep the upwind wing from lifting, and maintain directional control with rudder.

**Turbulent air:** in gusty crosswind conditions, use a power-on approach at an airspeed slightly above the normal approach speed for more positive control, with coordinated pitch and power adjustments.

## Risk Management

### Exceeding the Demonstrated Crosswind

#### Hazard

Attempting a takeoff or landing in a crosswind that exceeds the airplane's maximum demonstrated component or the pilot's ability.

#### Consequence

Loss of directional control, side load on the gear, runway excursion, or a ground loop.

#### Recognition

A reported or observed crosswind component at or above 15 knots, gusts beyond the spread you can manage, or a technique that is not holding the centerline.

#### Mitigation

Read the crosswind chart, compare the component to the demonstrated value and to your ability, and choose another runway, airport, or day when it exceeds either.

### Side Load on Touchdown

#### Hazard

Touching down while drifting sideways or with the longitudinal axis misaligned with the runway.

#### Consequence

Side loads on the landing gear, a swerve, tire or gear damage, or loss of control.

#### Recognition

The airplane tracking sideways over the runway at the flare, or the nose not aligned with the runway direction.

#### Mitigation

Align the axis before touchdown — transition out of the crab or establish the wing-low in time. Touch down aligned and not drifting, or go around.

### Stall/Sink in the Sideslip

#### Hazard

The sideslip's increased drag and reduced vertical component of lift raise the sink rate and bring the stall closer at low altitude.

#### Consequence

A high sink rate, a hard landing, or a stall close to the ground.

#### Recognition

Sink rate increasing, airspeed decaying, or decayed control response in the slip.

#### Mitigation

Manage airspeed with elevator, add power as needed in gusty conditions, keep the slip only as steep as required, and go around if the approach becomes unstable.

### Gusty / Turbulent Crosswind

#### Hazard

Gusts and turbulence on a crosswind approach cause sudden airspeed and drift changes.

#### Consequence

A balloon, a sudden sink, or being blown off the centerline at the flare.

#### Recognition

Fluctuating airspeed, gust spread on ATIS, or an approach that will not stabilize.

#### Mitigation

Use a power-on approach at a slightly higher speed, make smooth coordinated corrections, and go around if the approach cannot be stabilized.

### Runway Excursion on Rollout

#### Hazard

Losing directional control after touchdown as the crosswind continues to act on the airplane.

#### Consequence

Departing the runway side, a ground loop, or striking an obstacle.

#### Recognition

The nose weathervaning into the wind, the upwind wing lifting, or directional control decaying as speed falls.

#### Mitigation

Hold aileron into the wind and maintain directional control with rudder through the entire roll. The crosswind landing is not over until the airplane is stopped.

## Skills and Procedures

### Crosswind Takeoff

#### Purpose

Take off in a crosswind while holding the centerline, keeping the upwind wing from lifting, and lifting off into a crab that holds the upwind track.

#### Procedure

1. Verify the crosswind component against the demonstrated limit and your ability.
2. Full aileron into the wind; rudder holding the centerline.
3. Apply takeoff power smoothly and check engine instruments.
4. Reduce aileron as speed builds to keep the wings level.
5. Lift off at the POH speed and establish a crab into the wind to hold the upwind track.

#### Instructor Demonstration

Demonstrate one crosswind takeoff, verbalizing the control positions and the aileron reduction as speed builds.

#### Learner Practice

The learner performs crosswind takeoffs with coaching on control positions and the lift-off crab.

#### Common Errors

The most common errors made while performing crosswind takeoffs include the following:

- Failure to review AFM/POH performance and charts prior to takeoff.
- Failure to adequately clear the area prior to taxiing onto the active runway.
- Using less than full aileron pressure into the wind initially on the takeoff roll.
- Mechanical use of aileron control rather than judging lateral position of airplane on runway from visual clues and applying sufficient aileron to keep airplane centered laterally on runway.
- Side-skipping due to improper aileron application.
- Inadequate rudder control to maintain airplane parallel to centerline and pointed straight ahead in alignment with visual references.
- Excessive aileron input in the latter stage of the takeoff roll resulting in a steep bank into the wind at lift-off.
- Inadequate drift correction after lift-off.

#### Completion Standard

The learner performs a developing crosswind takeoff — full aileron into the wind initially, rudder for centerline, aileron reduced as speed builds, lift-off into a crab — with coaching.

### Crosswind Approach and Landing — Crab Method

#### Purpose

Hold a wings-level crab into the wind on final to keep the ground track on the centerline, then align the longitudinal axis before touchdown.

#### Procedure

1. Establish a crab into the wind on final so the ground track stays on the centerline.
2. Make small heading corrections to hold alignment.
3. Just before touchdown, use rudder to align the longitudinal axis with the runway, and aileron as needed to stop drift.
4. Touch down aligned and not drifting.

#### Instructor Demonstration

Demonstrate one crab-method approach and the transition to align the axis before touchdown.

#### Learner Practice

The learner flies crab-method approaches, optionally transitioning to wing-low before the round out.

#### Common Errors

- Inadequate compensation for wind drift on final approach.
- Unstable approach.
- Aligning the axis too early or too late, producing a side load.
- Touching down while drifting.

#### Completion Standard

The learner holds a developing crab on final and aligns the longitudinal axis before touchdown, touching down without drifting — with coaching.

### Crosswind Approach and Landing — Wing-Low (Sideslip) Method

#### Purpose

Hold the ground track and longitudinal axis aligned with the centerline throughout the approach and touchdown using a sideslip.

#### Procedure

1. Use rudder to align and hold the longitudinal axis with the runway direction.
2. Note the drift and oppose it with aileron into the wind — just enough bank to cancel it.
3. Adjust bank and rudder together to hold the centerline as the crosswind changes.
4. Manage airspeed with elevator; protect the stall margin.
5. Touch down on the upwind main wheel first, axis aligned, not drifting.
6. Hold aileron into the wind through the after-landing roll.

#### Instructor Demonstration

Demonstrate one wing-low approach and landing, verbalizing rudder-aligns-axis and aileron-corrects-drift.

#### Learner Practice

The learner flies wing-low approaches and landings with coaching on the rudder/aileron coordination and the touchdown.

#### Common Errors

Common errors in the performance of crosswind approaches and landings are:

1. Attempted landing in crosswinds that exceed the airplane's maximum demonstrated crosswind component.
2. Undershooting or overshooting the turn from base leg to final approach.
3. Inadequate compensation for wind drift on final approach.
4. Unstable approach.
5. Excessive sink rate or too low an airspeed from increased drag and reduced vertical lift during sideslip.
6. Failure to touch down with the longitudinal axis aligned with the runway.
7. Touching down while drifting.
8. Excessive airspeed on touchdown.
9. Failure to apply appropriate flight control inputs during rollout.
10. Failure to maintain direction control on rollout.
11. Excessive braking.
12. Loss of aircraft control.

#### Completion Standard

The learner flies a developing wing-low crosswind approach and landing — axis aligned with rudder, drift corrected with aileron, touchdown on the upwind main not drifting, control through the roll — with coaching.

## Scenario Application

### Scenario 1 — At the Demonstrated Limit

**Situation:**

ATIS reports a wind that, at the runway's angle, puts the crosswind component near 15 knots with gusts.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- How do you read the crosswind-component chart to find the component?
- Is a demonstrated value a guarantee for you today?
- What are your options if it exceeds the airplane's capability or yours?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Compute the crosswind component, compare it to 15 knots and to personal ability, and be willing to choose another runway, airport, or day. "Demonstrated" is a boundary, not a guarantee.

**Expected Performance:** Developing.

### Scenario 2 — Crab to Wing-Low

**Situation:**

You are crabbing on a longer final and the wind gusts as you near the flare.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- When do you transition from the crab to the wing-low method?
- What does the rudder do and what does the aileron do in the wing-low?
- What happens if you touch down still crabbing?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Transition smoothly to wing-low before the round out. Rudder aligns the axis, aileron corrects the drift. Touching down still crabbing puts a side load on the gear.

**Expected Performance:** Developing.

### Scenario 3 — The Gust at Touchdown

**Situation:**

Just as you flare, a gust lifts the upwind wing and starts the airplane drifting downwind across the runway.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What control inputs stop the drift and hold the axis through the roll?
- Is the crosswind landing finished at touchdown?
- When do you go around instead?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Aileron into the wind and rudder to hold the axis through the entire roll. The landing is not over until the airplane is stopped. If the approach or touchdown is not controlled, go around.

**Expected Performance:** Developing.

### Scenario 4 — Too Much Aileron at Lift-Off

**Situation:**

On the crosswind takeoff roll you hold full aileron into the wind all the way to lift-off, and the airplane lifts off in a steep bank into the wind.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What should happen to the aileron input as speed builds?
- Why is a steep bank at lift-off a problem?
- What is the correct sequence of control changes through the roll?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Full aileron into the wind initially, reduced as speed builds and the controls become effective, keeping the wings level, lifting off into a crab. Too much aileron late produces a steep bank at lift-off.

**Expected Performance:** Developing.

# Flight Card

## Flight Setup

### Lesson Target

**Target Proficiency: Developing**

Primary lesson target:

**Crosswind takeoff and crosswind approach and landing using both the crab method and the wing-low (sideslip) method, with landing refinement toward consistent, controlled touchdowns.**

Secondary airborne target:

**Go/no-go judgment against the 15-knot demonstrated crosswind and the day's actual conditions.**

Boundary:

**Crosswind operations and landing refinement only. No short-field or soft-field techniques.**

### Scope and Flexibility

This is an instructional plan, not a requirement to complete a set number of landings.

If the wind is too strong, too gusty, or beyond the learner's ability:

- reduce the number of crosswind landings,
- favor a single well-flown landing in each method over many rushed ones,
- refine the normal landing if crosswind conditions are not available,
- and do not fly when the crosswind exceeds the demonstrated value or the learner's limits.

Do not expand into short-field or soft-field work.

## Preflight and Cockpit

### Crosswind Briefing Before Start

#### Procedure

1. Complete the C172S preflight/checklist.
2. Obtain ATIS and read the wind; use the crosswind chart to find the component.
3. Compare the component to 15 knots and to ability; decide go/no-go.
4. Brief the crosswind takeoff control positions.
5. Brief both landing methods and the planned method for the conditions.
6. Brief the after-landing-roll control position.
7. Restate the go-around discipline — an unstable crosswind approach is a go-around.

#### Common Errors

- Not reading the crosswind component from the chart.
- Treating 15 knots as a guarantee rather than a boundary.
- No plan for which landing method to use.

---

## Engine Start and Taxi

Apply Lesson 2. Short version only: checklist, brake check, **wind-control positioning during taxi**, radio discipline, hold short and verify.

Wind-control positioning in a crosswind taxi applies today's aileron-into-the-wind principle on the ground.

---

## Before Takeoff

#### Procedure

1. C172S before-takeoff checklist.
2. Reconfirm the wind and the crosswind component.
3. Brief the crosswind takeoff: full aileron into the wind, rudder for centerline.
4. Line up only when authorized and ready.

#### Common Errors

- Entering without a current crosswind read.
- Not briefing the control positions.

---

## Crosswind Takeoff

#### Procedure

1. Full aileron into the wind; rudder holding the centerline.
2. Apply takeoff power smoothly; check engine instruments.
3. Reduce aileron as speed builds to keep the wings level.
4. Lift off at the POH speed into a crab that holds the upwind track.

#### Common Errors

- Less than full aileron into the wind initially.
- Mechanical aileron use rather than judging lateral position visually.
- Side-skipping from improper aileron.
- Excessive aileron late in the roll — steep bank into the wind at lift-off.
- Inadequate drift correction after lift-off.

---

## Pattern

Fly the pattern per Lesson 3 with crab on each leg for the wind. Plan the approach for the selected crosswind-landing method.

---

## Crosswind Approach and Landing — Crab Method

#### Procedure

1. Hold a crab into the wind on final; ground track on the centerline.
2. Small heading corrections to hold alignment.
3. Just before touchdown, rudder to align the longitudinal axis, aileron to stop drift.
4. Touch down aligned and not drifting.

#### Common Errors

- Inadequate drift correction on final.
- Unstable approach.
- Aligning the axis too early or too late.
- Touching down while drifting.

---

## Crosswind Approach and Landing — Wing-Low Method

#### Procedure

1. Rudder to align and hold the longitudinal axis with the runway.
2. Aileron into the wind to cancel the drift — just enough bank.
3. Adjust bank and rudder together to hold the centerline.
4. Manage airspeed with elevator; protect the stall margin.
5. Touch down on the upwind main wheel first, aligned, not drifting.
6. Hold aileron into the wind through the after-landing roll.

#### Common Errors

Common errors in the performance of crosswind approaches and landings are:

1. Attempted landing in crosswinds that exceed the airplane's maximum demonstrated crosswind component.
2. Undershooting or overshooting the turn from base leg to final approach.
3. Inadequate compensation for wind drift on final approach.
4. Unstable approach.
5. Excessive sink rate or too low an airspeed from increased drag and reduced vertical lift during sideslip.
6. Failure to touch down with the longitudinal axis aligned with the runway.
7. Touching down while drifting.
8. Excessive airspeed on touchdown.
9. Failure to apply appropriate flight control inputs during rollout.
10. Failure to maintain direction control on rollout.
11. Excessive braking.
12. Loss of aircraft control.

---

## After-Landing Roll

#### Procedure

1. Hold aileron into the wind through the roll.
2. Maintain directional control with rudder.
3. Exit the runway, fully clear, then checklist, and reorient.

#### Common Errors

- Releasing the into-wind aileron at touchdown.
- Weathervaning or the upwind wing lifting as speed falls.

---

## Return, Shutdown and Securing

#### Procedure

1. Control, exit, fully clear, then checklist, reorient.
2. Taxi to parking with wind-control positioning.
3. C172S shutdown, secure, postflight, log discrepancies.

#### Common Errors

- Heads-down before clear of the runway.
- Losing position after exit.

---

## Flight Completion Criteria

The flight portion is satisfactory for this stage when the learner demonstrates developing ability to:

- read the crosswind chart and make a sound go/no-go decision,
- perform a crosswind takeoff with correct, changing control positions,
- fly a crosswind approach and landing using the crab method, aligning the axis before touchdown,
- fly a crosswind approach and landing using the wing-low method — rudder aligns the axis, aileron corrects the drift,
- protect the stall margin in the sideslip,
- touch down on centerline, aligned, not drifting, with control through the roll,
- and go around rather than force an unstable crosswind approach.

The lesson may be repeated when crosswind judgment, the landing methods, or directional control are not yet safe to continue.
