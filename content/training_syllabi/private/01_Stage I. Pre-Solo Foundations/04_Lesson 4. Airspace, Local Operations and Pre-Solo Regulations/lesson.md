# Lesson 4. Airspace, Local Operations and Pre-Solo Regulations

**Lesson:** 4  
**Stage:** Stage I - Pre-Solo Foundations  
**Type:** GROUND + FLIGHT  
**Target Proficiency:** Developing

## Objective

Develop the learner's knowledge of airspace classification, student-pilot regulatory restrictions, and local operating procedures at David Wayne Hooks Memorial Airport (KDWH). The learner should understand the differences between Class D (tower) and Class G (non-tower) operations, apply VFR minimums correctly, recognize the importance of speaking up in a busy tower environment, and be prepared for pre-solo endorsement requirements.

In the flight component, the learner will practice touch-and-go takeoffs and landings using procedures from Lesson 3, reinforcing control quality and tower interaction in the Class D airspace context.

## Completion Standards

By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:

- Identify and describe KDWH airspace class for tower and non-tower hours
- Explain student-pilot restrictions under Part 61 (§61.87, §61.89) and recognize when operations are authorized
- Interpret VFR minimums by airspace class using the VFR minimums diagram
- State the operational differences between Class D, E, and G airspace
- Understand Class B airspace location and basic entry requirements (Mode C, clearance)
- Demonstrate the ability to speak up early and clearly in a busy tower environment: "Student pilot, need a 360" or "Unable, requesting vector"
- Apply Lesson 3 takeoff and landing procedures in the Class D airspace environment
- Maintain control quality and POH speeds during repeated touch-and-go cycles
- Comply with tower instructions, including downwind extensions, 360° requests, and runway/traffic-pattern changes
- Explain the purpose of each pre-solo endorsement and recognize their role in solo readiness

This lesson develops knowledge and procedural application with instructor supervision. Practical-test-level independence is not expected yet.

## Why This Matters

A student pilot must understand the airspace they operate in and the rules that govern flight. Class D airspace at KDWH is a controlled, towered environment with specific clearance, communication, and transponder requirements. Operating legally and safely requires knowing these rules.

Equally important is the ability to speak up. Busy tower environments can create confusion through downwind extensions, 360° requests, runway changes, or traffic-pattern switches. A student pilot who does not understand an instruction or believes they cannot safely comply has the right and responsibility to request clarification or state "unable."

## Knowledge

### 1. Airspace Classification — The National Airspace System

The United States airspace is divided into classes based on traffic density, control requirements, and pilot qualifications.

**Controlled Airspace (ATC services available):**

- **Class A:** FL180 and above (IFR only; not relevant to private-pilot pre-solo)
- **Class B:** Major metropolitan areas (Houston, Dallas, etc.); requires clearance and Mode C transponder
- **Class C:** Large airports; requires radio contact and transponder
- **Class D:** Towered airports; requires radio contact and transponder
- **Class E:** Controlled airspace with ATC services but less stringent entry requirements than D; includes airways and controlled airspace above Class G

**Uncontrolled Airspace:**

- **Class G:** Non-tower airports and airspace not otherwise classified; visual flight rules apply; see-and-avoid is primary responsibility

[[highlight:
Key Point |
KDWH is **Class D** during tower hours (1300–0400Z / 7:00 am–10:00 pm). When the tower is closed, the airspace converts to **Class G** from the surface to **700 feet AGL**, with **Class E** above that until Class B is reached.
]]

### 2. Class D Airspace — KDWH During Tower Hours

**Entry Requirements:**

- Radio contact with tower
- Clearance to enter (often granted with pattern entry instruction)
- Transponder set to assigned code (usually 1200, then tower may assign different)
- Compliance with tower altitude, speed, and routing instructions

**Operating Requirements:**

- Maintain two-way radio contact
- Comply with ATC instructions and restrictions
- Observe speed restrictions (typically 250 knots below 10,000 feet MSL, but tower may impose lower limits)
- Maintain prescribed altitude unless otherwise authorized

**Exit:**

- Tower authorization before departing
- Follows departure instructions (e.g., "Turn right heading 090 after departure")

### 3. Class G and Class E Airspace — KDWH When Tower Is Closed

When the tower closes (outside 1300–0400Z / 7:00 am–10:00 pm), the airspace over KDWH converts: **Class G from the surface to 700 feet AGL, with Class E above that until Class B is reached.**

**Entry:** No clearance required; no radio contact required (though good practice)

**Communication:** Use CTAF (Common Traffic Advisory Frequency); the tower frequency (118.4) becomes the CTAF when the tower is closed

**Pilot Responsibility:** See-and-avoid; continuous traffic scan; self-announce position and intentions

**Class E above 700 ft AGL:** Controlled airspace resumes overhead — VFR aircraft may operate without a clearance, but IFR traffic is separated by ATC and the Class E VFR minimums apply above the Class G ceiling.

**Advantages:** Less formal; no clearance delay

**Disadvantage:** No ATC separation service in the Class G layer; rely on visual awareness and radio position reports

### 4. VFR Weather Minimums by Airspace Class

Minimum visibility and ceiling to operate under VFR:

**Class D (at or below 10,000 ft MSL):**
- Visibility: 3 statute miles
- Cloud clearance: 1,000 feet above, 2,000 feet lateral, 500 feet below

**Class E (at or below 10,000 ft MSL):**
- Visibility: 3 statute miles
- Cloud clearance: 1,000 feet above, 2,000 feet lateral, 500 feet below

**Class E (above 10,000 ft MSL):**
- Visibility: 5 statute miles
- Cloud clearance: 1,000 feet above, 1,000 feet lateral, 1,000 feet below

**Class G (at or below 1,200 ft AGL):**
- Visibility: 1 statute mile
- Cloud clearance: clear of clouds

**Class G (above 1,200 ft AGL, at or below 10,000 ft MSL):**
- Visibility: 1 statute mile
- Cloud clearance: 1,000 feet above, 1,000 feet lateral, 1,000 feet below



[[highlight:
Key Point |
VFR minimums are **legal thresholds**, not go/no-go decision points. Safe operations often require more than legal minimums.
]]

### 5. Student-Pilot Restrictions — Part 61 § 61.87 and § 61.89

A student pilot may not:

- Act as pilot-in-command of a solo aircraft without an instructor endorsement
- Fly outside a designated area without instructor endorsement
- Carry passengers
- Fly at night (without night endorsement)
- Act as pilot-in-command of certain aircraft (complex, high-performance, pressurized, tailwheel) without specific endorsement
- Exceed speed limitations set by the instructor
- Descend below instructor-approved altitudes without specific authorization

**Pre-Solo Endorsements Required:**

- Pre-solo knowledge endorsement (confirms mastery of airspace, regulations, procedures)
- Solo flight endorsement for a specific area (sets lateral and vertical boundaries)
- Authorization to fly beyond the local area (solo cross-country endorsement; later)

### 6. Class B Airspace Awareness — Houston Area

Houston Class B airspace surrounds Houston Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) and includes areas around Hobby (HOU) and other area airports.

**Key Facts:**

- Class B is reserved for higher-performance aircraft and pilots with demonstrable ability
- All aircraft entering Class B require Mode C transponder (even VFR)
- All aircraft entering Class B require clearance from ATC before entry
- The **Mode C veil** (typically a 30-NM ring around Class B) requires Mode C transponder for VFR flight
- KDWH is Class D, not Class B, but is located within or near the Mode C veil

**Student-Pilot Implication:** A student pilot may not operate in Class B airspace without instructor authorization, training, and sign-off per Part 61.

[[pdf: /knowledge/Houston_TAC.pdf]]

### 7. Local Operations at KDWH — Busy Tower Environment

KDWH is a busy, training-oriented towered airport. Expect:

- **Downwind extensions:** "Extend your downwind, I'll call base" to sequence traffic
- **360° requests:** "Turn left 360" or "Make a right 360" for spacing
- **Runway/traffic-pattern changes:** "Change left to right traffic" or "Switch from Runway 17 Right to 17 Left" as conditions require
- **Multiple touch-and-gos:** Rapid sequence of takeoffs and landings with coaching

**Student-Pilot Communication in Busy Tower Environment:**

A student pilot has the right and responsibility to:

- **Speak up early:** If you do not understand an instruction, ask for clarification immediately, not after committing to the maneuver.
- **State your status clearly:** "Student pilot, say again?" or "Hooks Tower, Skyhawk 826SW, student pilot, say again please."
- **Declare inability:** "Unable" is a complete sentence. "Unable to comply; student pilot needing vector" or "Unable; student pilot unable to extend downwind."
- **Request direction:** "Hooks Tower, Skyhawk 826SW, student pilot, request additional direction or return to pattern."

## Risk Management

### Airspace Bust

#### Hazard

Operating outside assigned or authorized airspace (wrong altitude, Class B without clearance, beyond solo area).

#### Consequence

FAA violation, bust of pilot certificate, loss of currency, enforcement action.

#### Recognition

Altimeter reading, visual position relative to airport, radio instruction, or later discovery.

#### Mitigation

Know the boundaries before flight. Maintain altitude discipline. Use the radio to verify position and authorization. Ask for help.

### VFR Flight into Marginal VFR or IFR Conditions

#### Hazard

Continuing flight as VFR when conditions drop below VFR minimums.

#### Consequence

Spatial disorientation, loss of control, collision with terrain.

#### Recognition

Decreasing visibility, ceiling lowering, loss of clear horizon.

#### Mitigation

Check weather before flight. Maintain awareness in flight. Land if minimums are approached.

### Communication Failure or Misunderstanding in Class D

#### Hazard

Misunderstanding a tower instruction (downwind extension, 360°, runway change) or losing radio contact.

#### Consequence

Collision, airspace violation, emergency, loss of control.

#### Recognition

Unclear instruction, conflicting tower calls, radio static or loss of signal.

#### Mitigation

Say back the instruction. Ask for repetition if unclear. Speak up early. Maintain radio contact; if lost, return to pattern and try again.

### Student-Pilot Restriction Violation

#### Hazard

Flying beyond authorized area, altitude, or airspace without endorsement.

#### Consequence

Accident in unfamiliar terrain, airspace violation, loss of certificate.

#### Recognition

Position check, altitude check, airspace awareness.

#### Mitigation

Know your endorsements. Know your authorized area. Respect the boundaries until you are trained and endorsed to expand them.

### Overconfidence or Peer Pressure

#### Hazard

Attempting a maneuver or flight outside your training or authorization because an instructor, other pilot, or your own ego suggests it.

#### Consequence

Loss of control, accident, airspace violation.

#### Recognition

Pressure to exceed limits, uncertainty about the task, unfamiliar procedures.

#### Mitigation

Defer to your instructor's judgment. Your job is to train safely within set limits, not to impress or push faster than safe.

## Skills and Procedures

### Verifying KDWH Airspace and Legal Status

#### Procedure

1. Check current time and tower service hours (1300–0400Z / 7:00 am–10:00 pm for Class D; otherwise Class G surface to 700 ft AGL with Class E above).
2. Review airport information and active runway.
3. Determine Class D or Class G based on current time.
4. If Class D: plan radio contact, transponder setting, and ATC compliance.
5. If Class G: plan CTAF use and self-announce position.

#### Common Errors

- Assuming tower is always open at a training airport.
- Forgetting to set transponder.
- Not monitoring CTAF on ground for early awareness.

---

### Understanding VFR Minimums and Making a Legal/Prudent Decision

#### Procedure

1. Check the VFR Airspace Minimums diagram for the class and altitude you plan.
2. Identify minimum visibility and cloud clearance required.
3. Obtain current weather (METAR, ATIS, or tower).
4. Compare actual to required minimums.
5. If actual ≥ required, flight is **legal**.
6. Assess additional factors: wind, ceiling trend, visibility trend, your experience.
7. Decide: **prudent** (above minimums with safety buffer) or **marginal** (legal but tight).
8. A prudent pilot often chooses not to fly when conditions are merely legal.

#### Common Errors

- Confusing legal with prudent.
- Continuing into marginal conditions.
- Not rechecking conditions during flight.

---

### Speaking Up in a Busy Tower Environment

#### Procedure

1. Listen on the frequency before transmitting.
2. Understand the tower's instruction before responding.
3. If unclear, say: "Hooks Tower, Skyhawk 826SW, say again, student pilot."
4. If you cannot comply, say: "Unable; student pilot requesting vector" or "Unable to extend downwind; student pilot requesting standard pattern."
5. If you need help, say: "Skyhawk 826SW, student pilot, need additional direction."
6. Never guess or attempt an unclear maneuver.
7. Use plain language; avoid scripts.

#### Common Errors

- Responding "Roger" to an instruction you did not fully understand.
- Attempting a downwind extension or 360° because you felt pressured.
- Not using the word "student pilot" to clarify your status.
- Delaying communication until too late.

## Scenarios & Application

### Scenario 1: Saturday Morning at KDWH

**Time:** 0900 local (1500 UTC)
**Tower status:** Tower is open (Class D)
**Condition:** Ceiling 1,500 feet AGL, visibility 4 miles, wind light

**Question:** Is this legal Class D VFR? Is it prudent?

**Answer:** Legal (3 SM minimum for Class D). However, 1,500 feet ceiling is tight for pattern work, especially with mountains or higher terrain nearby. The ceiling is low enough that a go-around or rejected landing would be conducted close to or in the clouds. Prudent decision: hold for better conditions or brief very carefully.

### Scenario 2: Evening at KDWH

**Time:** 1800 local (0000 UTC next day)
**Tower status:** Tower closes at 2000 local (0400 UTC)
**Plan:** Touch-and-go landings before tower closes

**Question:** What changes occur when the tower closes? What do I do for the last landing?

**Answer:** KDWH transitions from Class D to Class G (surface to 700 ft AGL, with Class E above). Once the tower closes, you switch to CTAF (118.4), announce your position, and use self-announce procedures. Radio contact is not required, but good airmanship demands continued position reports. Approach and landing procedures do not change; only the communication and ATC separation concept changes.

### Scenario 3: Tower Issues a Downwind Extension

**Tower:** "Skyhawk 826SW, extend your downwind, I'll call base."

**Question:** You are uncomfortable with a downwind extension (too slow, too low, uncertain). What do you say?

**Answer:** "Hooks Tower, Skyhawk 826SW, student pilot unable to extend downwind; say if you can vector me for a longer final or request to make a 360."

Alternatively: "Hooks Tower, Skyhawk 826SW, request a 360 to let traffic clear."

Do **not** attempt a maneuver you do not understand or believe you cannot safely execute.

### Scenario 4: Solo Area Authorization

**Situation:** You are in solo training. Your endorsement says, "Authorized solo within 25 NM of KDWH below 5,000 feet MSL."

**Question:** Can you fly to an airport 30 NM away? Can you climb to 5,500 feet for a maneuver?

**Answer:** No to both. The boundaries are set by your instructor. Violating them is a solo restriction violation and grounds for loss of solo privileges. Respect the limits; when you are ready for more, your instructor will endorse you for cross-country or altitude expansion.

### Scenario 5: Speaking Up for Clarity

**Tower:** "Skyhawk 826SW, left traffic 17 Right, cleared to land."

**You hear:** "Left traffic... cleared to land" but miss whether it is 17 Right or 17 Left.

**Action:** Before committing to the pattern, radio back: "Hooks Tower, Skyhawk 826SW, confirm runway 17 Right, left traffic, cleared to land."

**Tower:** Confirms.

This saves you from turning to the wrong runway or wrong pattern direction.

# Flight Card

## Overview

**Primary Objective:** Practice touch-and-go takeoffs and landings in the KDWH Class D environment.

**No New Concepts:** Takeoff and landing procedures are from Lesson 3. This flight reinforces those procedures while introducing airspace awareness and tower interaction in a busy, controlled environment.

**Skills Applied:**
- Lesson 1: Outside references, trim, coordination, control quality
- Lesson 2: Radio communication, tower interaction, airport orientation
- Lesson 3: Takeoff, landing, and pattern procedures
- Lesson 4: Class D airspace compliance, VFR minimums awareness, speaking up when unclear

**Estimated Duration:** Approximately 1.0–1.5 hours flight time

**Workload:** Busy tower environment with repeated pattern cycles, downwind extensions, possible 360° requests, and tower-directed runway/traffic-pattern changes.

**Safety Focus:** Workload management, tower communication clarity, and willingness to speak up if confused.

---

## Preflight and Cockpit

### Airspace Briefing Before Start

#### Procedure

1. Verify current time and tower service hours (1300–0400Z / 7:00 am–10:00 pm for Class D, otherwise Class G surface to 700 ft AGL with Class E above).
2. Confirm ATIS or airport information for active runway(s) and NOTAMs.
3. Confirm Class D status for today.
4. Plan tower frequency (118.4) and transponder code (typically 1200 initially).
5. Confirm VFR minimums are met: 3 SM visibility and 1,000 feet AGL ceiling minimum for Class D.
6. Brief tower communication: ground for taxi, tower for takeoff/landing.
7. Brief the student: "We are in Class D airspace. Tower controls all operations. If you do not understand an instruction, ask for clarification. If you cannot comply, say 'unable.'"

#### Common Errors

- Assuming tower is open without verifying.
- Forgetting transponder setup.
- Not confirming weather meets minimums.

---

## Engine Start and Taxi

### KDWH Ground Control

Apply Lesson 2 procedures (no reteach):

1. Start and systems checks per C172S checklist.
2. Contact KDWH Ground (121.8): "Hooks Ground, Skyhawk 826SW at [position], requesting taxi for takeoff, touch-and-gos, [runway] or request."
3. Ground issues taxi clearance and runway.
4. Read back runway and taxiway.
5. Taxi using Lesson 2 habits: wind control, brake check, hold-short verification, diagram orientation.

---

## Before Takeoff

### KDWH Tower — Takeoff Clearance

1. Complete C172S before-takeoff checklist.
2. Verify runway, wind, and traffic.
3. Contact tower: "KDWH Tower, Skyhawk 826SW, runway [number], ready for takeoff, request touch-and-gos."
4. Tower clears.
5. Read back clearance, runway, and traffic-pattern direction.
6. Enter runway only when cleared.

---

## Touch-and-Go Sequence

### Takeoff and Climb

**Procedure** (from Lesson 3):

1. Smooth takeoff power.
2. Engine instruments.
3. Centerline; anticipate left-turn tendency.
4. Outside references.
5. Proper lift-off at POH speed.
6. Climb at POH VY.
7. Trim.
8. Track upwind.

**Class D Awareness:** Comply with tower altitude restrictions and be ready for downwind extensions or 360° requests.

---

### Closed Traffic Pattern

**Procedure** (from Lesson 3; with tower interaction):

1. Climb upwind to within 300 ft of TPA, then turn XWind.
2. Turn downwind with crab; timely landing checklist.
3. Be ready for tower instruction: downwind extension, 360°, or runway/pattern change.
4. If unclear, ask: "Say again, student pilot."
5. Base with drift correction.
6. Final aligned; crab for track.
7. If not stabilized, go around.

---

### Touch-and-Go Landing

**Procedure** (from Lesson 3; touch-and-go specific):

1. Aiming point → round out → landing attitude on mains.
2. Hold back-elevator; directional control; no excessive braking.
3. After touchdown, maintain directional control and rollout.
4. Reduce flaps gradually; accelerate smoothly to takeoff power.
5. Be ready for next tower instruction.

---

### Repeated Cycles

Repeat pattern and touch-and-go as tower sequences allow. Manage workload: fly the airplane first, then read tower, then respond.

---

## Return, Shutdown and Securing

### After Final Landing

1. Control, exit runway, fully clear, then checklist.
2. Reorient using Lesson 2 habits.
3. Contact ground for taxi to parking.
4. C172S shutdown and secure.
5. Postflight and discrepancies.

---

## Flight Completion Criteria

The flight is satisfactory when the learner demonstrates developing ability to:

- Contact and comply with KDWH tower (Class D discipline).
- Execute multiple touch-and-go takeoffs and landings using Lesson 3 procedures.
- Maintain control quality and POH speeds (Lesson 1 standards).
- Manage workload in a busy tower pattern.
- Speak up early if a tower instruction is unclear or workload is overwhelming.
- Transition smoothly from landing to takeoff in touch-and-go sequence.
- Demonstrate understanding of Class D airspace and VFR minimums.

# Debrief

## Post-Flight Review

Use the Flight Card and Kneeboard to review:

1. **Class D Compliance:** Did you establish radio contact, comply with clearances, and respect altitude/speed restrictions?
2. **Airspace Awareness:** Were you aware of your altitude, position, and Class D boundaries throughout?
3. **Tower Communication:** Did you understand tower instructions? Did you speak up early if confused?
4. **Workload Management:** How did you manage repeated takeoffs and landings? Were there instructions you did not fully understand?
5. **Touch-and-Go Technique:** Were takeoffs and landings consistent? Did you manage transition smoothly?
6. **Speaking Up:** Did you feel pressure to comply with an unclear instruction? Would you speak up next time?

## VFR Minimums Review

1. What were actual conditions during the flight (ceiling, visibility)?
2. Compare actual to Class D minimums (3 SM, 1,000 feet).
3. Was this a prudent operation, or merely legal?

## Solo Readiness

- **Review endorsement requirements:** What three pre-solo endorsements do you now understand?
- **Review solo restrictions:** If authorized solo in local area (e.g., 25 NM, below 5,000 feet), would you be comfortable? What would you do if tower issued an unclear instruction?
