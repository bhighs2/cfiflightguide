# Lesson 10. Pre-Solo Flight Proficiency Check

**Lesson:** 10  
**Stage:** Stage I — Pre-Solo Foundations  
**Type:** GROUND + FLIGHT · EVALUATION  
**Target Proficiency:** Proficient — solo-ready

## Objective

Evaluate the learner's proficiency and safety on the maneuvers and procedures required by 14 CFR § 61.87(c) and (d) for a single-engine airplane, in the make and model to be flown solo (C172S), at the airport where the solo will be conducted (KDWH). On satisfactory demonstration of all fifteen items, sign the pre-solo flight-training endorsement (AC 61-65K A.4).

## Completion Standards

The learner demonstrates satisfactory proficiency and safety, as judged by the instructor, on each of the fifteen § 61.87(d) maneuvers and procedures:

1. Proper flight preparation — preflight planning, powerplant operation, and aircraft systems.
2. Taxiing and surface operations, including runups.
3. Takeoffs and landings, including normal and crosswind.
4. Straight-and-level flight and turns in both directions.
5. Climbs and climbing turns.
6. Airport traffic patterns, including entry and departure procedures.
7. Collision avoidance, windshear avoidance, and wake-turbulence avoidance.
8. Descents, with and without turns, using high- and low-drag configurations.
9. Flight at various airspeeds from cruise to slow flight.
10. Stall entries from various flight attitudes and power combinations, with recovery at the first indication and recovery from a full stall.
11. Emergency procedures and equipment malfunctions.
12. Ground reference maneuvers.
13. Approaches to a landing area with simulated engine malfunctions.
14. Slips to a landing.
15. Go-arounds.

On satisfactory demonstration of all fifteen, the instructor signs the AC 61-65K A.4 pre-solo flight-training endorsement.

## Why This Matters

Lesson 9 proved the learner knows the rules, the airplane, and the airport. Lesson 10 proves the learner can fly them — all of it, in sequence, without coaching. This is the flight gate before solo. § 61.87 is explicit: the student must have received and logged the training **and** demonstrated satisfactory proficiency and safety on the maneuvers and procedures in the make and model to be flown, at the airport where the solo will occur.

This is the instructor's last full evaluation before the learner flies alone. Anything marginal here is addressed here — not discovered by the learner at 800 feet on a solo downwind.

## Risk Management

### Evaluation Pressure Degrading Performance

#### Hazard

The pressure of an evaluation causes a proficient learner to underperform.

#### Consequence

A marginal-looking maneuver from a learner who is actually ready; an unnecessary delay.

#### Recognition

An early item flown below the learner's demonstrated standard, with self-correction once settled.

#### Mitigation

Brief the flight as a normal Stage I flight that happens to be observed. Allow the learner to settle. Distinguish nerves from a genuine gap.

### A Marginal Item Passed to Stay on Schedule

#### Hazard

The instructor signs off an item that was not satisfactory to keep the day moving toward solo.

#### Consequence

A solo pilot with an unaddressed weakness — discovered alone.

#### Recognition

An item that needed prompting, or a maneuver flown to "good enough" rather than the standard.

#### Mitigation

The standard is proficiency and safety on every item. A marginal item is re-demonstrated or retrained; the endorsement waits.

### Overloading the Flight

#### Hazard

Trying to evaluate all fifteen items in too short a flight, rushing the learner through them.

#### Consequence

Rushed, degraded maneuvers that do not reflect true proficiency.

#### Recognition

Back-to-back maneuvers with no time to stabilize, or the learner behind the airplane from workload.

#### Mitigation

Sequence the flight logically and allow room. If it does not all fit safely, complete the evaluation over the available time and finish remaining items before the endorsement.

### Solo-Readiness Judgment

#### Hazard

Technical proficiency is present, but the judgment — go/no-go, go-around, speaking up — is not yet solo-grade.

#### Consequence

A solo pilot who can fly the maneuvers but makes poor decisions alone.

#### Recognition

Hesitation on a go/no-go, an approach forced rather than abandoned, or silence when a call was needed.

#### Mitigation

Evaluate judgment alongside stick-and-rudder. A go-around chosen on the evaluation flight is a sign of readiness, not failure.

## Evaluation Conduct

### Purpose

Demonstrate satisfactory proficiency and safety on all fifteen § 61.87(d) items in the C172S at KDWH, unassisted, as the final gate before solo.

### Procedure

1. Brief the flight sequence and the standard: proficiency and safety, without coaching.
2. Preflight and surface operations (items 1–2).
3. Takeoff, pattern, and airwork: four fundamentals, climbs, descents, slow flight, stalls, ground reference (items 3–9, 12).
4. Emergencies: simulated engine-failure approach and a slip to a landing (items 11, 13, 14).
5. Pattern work: normal and crosswind takeoffs and landings, go-around, pattern entry and departure (items 3, 6, 7, 15).
6. Stall series and airspeed envelope (item 10).
7. Debrief against the standard and make the endorsement decision.

### Common Errors

- Coaching an item and then counting it as demonstrated.
- Rushing the sequence so maneuvers are not flown to standard.
- Skipping an item to save time and signing anyway.
- Judging only stick-and-rudder and ignoring decision-making.
- Signing A.4 with any item marginal.

### Completion Standard

Every § 61.87(d) item is demonstrated to satisfactory proficiency and safety, without coaching. The instructor signs the AC 61-65K A.4 endorsement.

## Scenario Application

### Scenario 1 — The Rushed Start

**Situation:**

You arrive having had a difficult morning. Preflight feels rushed, and you are tempted to skip straight to flying so the check "goes well."

**Discuss / Decide:**

- Which § 61.87(d) items does the preflight and surface work satisfy?
- What does a rushed preflight signal to the evaluator?
- What do you do if you do not feel ready?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Items 1 and 2 are flight preparation and surface operations — they are evaluated like everything else. A rushed preflight is a finding. If you are not ready, say so — the check can be rescheduled; the standard does not move.

**Expected Performance:** Proficient.

### Scenario 2 — The Unstable Final on Check Day

**Situation:**

On a normal-approach-and-landing item, the approach goes high and fast.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What is the correct action, and what does choosing it show the evaluator?
- Is a go-around on an evaluation flight a failure?
- Which § 61.87(d) item does this satisfy?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Go around. A go-around chosen on the evaluation flight demonstrates judgment and satisfies item 15 — it is a sign of readiness, not a failure. Forcing the landing is the finding.

**Expected Performance:** Proficient.

### Scenario 3 — Simulated Failure on the Check

**Situation:**

At altitude during the airwork, the instructor pulls power: "Simulated engine failure."

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What is the sequence, and which items does it cover?
- Where do you look first, and what do you not touch first?
- How does this differ from the same maneuver in Lesson 7?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

Best glide, best field, then checklist — flying the airplane first. This covers items 11 and 13 (and slips on the approach, item 14). The difference from Lesson 7 is that you do it without coaching, to standard.

**Expected Performance:** Proficient.

### Scenario 4 — The Marginal Maneuver

**Situation:**

Your first stall recovery was tentative — you recovered correctly but slowly, and the instructor asked you to do it again.

**Discuss / Decide:**

- What does a request to repeat an item mean?
- Is repeating an item a failure of the check?
- What is the standard each item must meet?

**Desired Learner Outcome:**

A repeat means the first demonstration did not clearly show satisfactory proficiency and safety. Repeating is not failing — it is the evaluation working. Every item must be demonstrated to standard, without coaching, before the endorsement.

**Expected Performance:** Proficient.

## Preflight and Surface (Items 1–2)

#### Procedure

1. Flight preparation: preflight planning, powerplant operation, aircraft systems.
2. Taxiing and surface operations, including runups, with wind control and radio discipline.

#### Common Errors

- A rushed or incomplete preflight.
- Poor wind-control positioning or hold-short discipline.
- Radio errors or failure to verify.

---

## Takeoff, Pattern, and Airwork (Items 3–9, 12)

#### Procedure

1. Normal and crosswind takeoff (item 3).
2. Pattern with correct entry and departure; collision, windshear, and wake-turbulence avoidance (items 6–7).
3. Straight-and-level and turns both directions; climbs and climbing turns (items 4–5).
4. Descents with and without turns, high- and low-drag (item 8).
5. Airspeed envelope to slow flight (item 9).
6. A ground reference maneuver with wind correction (item 12).

#### Common Errors

- Unstable or uncoordinated airwork.
- Poor wind correction or ground track.
- Pattern entry/departure errors.
- Altitude or airspeed not held to standard.

---

## Stalls (Item 10)

#### Procedure

1. Clearing turns and a briefed recovery floor.
2. Stall entries from various attitudes and power combinations.
3. Recovery at the first indication, and recovery from a full stall.

#### Common Errors

- Not clearing the area.
- Missing the first indication.
- Power or wings-level before reducing AOA.
- Secondary stall.

---

## Emergencies, Engine-Out Approach, and Slip (Items 11, 13, 14)

#### Procedure

1. Simulated engine failure: best glide, best field, checklist — flying first.
2. Approach to the landing area with the simulated malfunction.
3. A slip to a landing to manage energy.

#### Common Errors

- Denial or delay; not holding best glide.
- Poor field selection or stretching the glide.
- Checklist before the airplane and field.
- Slip mishandled — airspeed, direction, or transition.

---

## Landings and Go-Around (Items 3, 15)

#### Procedure

1. Normal and crosswind landings to standard — centerline, aligned, not drifting, control through the roll.
2. At least one go-around, chosen and executed correctly.

#### Common Errors

- Unstable approach continued.
- Axis misaligned or drifting at touchdown.
- Loss of directional control on rollout.
- Indecision or delay on the go-around.

---

## Return, Shutdown and Securing

#### Procedure

1. Final landing, control, exit, fully clear, then checklist, reorient.
2. Taxi to parking; C172S shutdown, secure, postflight, log discrepancies.

#### Common Errors

- Heads-down before clear of the runway.
- Losing position after exit.

---

## Flight Completion Criteria

The evaluation is satisfactory when the learner demonstrates, **without coaching**, satisfactory proficiency and safety on all fifteen § 61.87(d) items:

- flight preparation and surface operations,
- normal and crosswind takeoffs and landings,
- the four fundamentals and climbs,
- pattern entry, departure, and avoidance,
- descents in both drag configurations and the airspeed envelope to slow flight,
- stall entries with recovery at first indication and from a full stall,
- emergency procedures and a simulated engine-out approach,
- a ground reference maneuver,
- a slip to a landing,
- and a go-around.

Any item that required coaching or was not clearly satisfactory is re-demonstrated or retrained before the endorsement. On satisfactory completion of all fifteen, the instructor signs the AC 61-65K A.4 endorsement.
