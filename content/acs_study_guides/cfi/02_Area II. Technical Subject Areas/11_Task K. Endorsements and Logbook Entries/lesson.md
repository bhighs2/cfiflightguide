# Task K. Endorsements and Logbook Entries

**Area II — Technical Subject Areas**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands logbook entries and endorsements, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate the required skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

The "power of the pen" is one of the most powerful tools a CFI holds. A signature in a logbook authorizes a student to solo, to cross the country alone, and to sit for a checkride — and a careless or missing endorsement can ground a student or expose the instructor. This lesson takes an aspiring CFI through the required logbook entries and endorsements necessary to take a student pilot from first lesson through the private-pilot practical test.

This is a ground-only knowledge Task. The ACS skill element requires the applicant to **describe and prepare** actual logbook entries and endorsements for at least two events.

## Schedule / Equipment

- Ground: 30 minutes
- Student Q&A: 10 minutes
- Copy of AC 61-65K (Appendix A sample endorsements)
- 14 CFR Part 61
- Endorsement quick reference (optional)
- Whiteboard / markers (optional)

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** explain the required elements of a logbook entry and an entry for training given, when entries are required, and the required training and endorsements for student solo, solo cross-country, and the private-pilot checkride; describe flight-instructor record-keeping and certificate renewal and reinstatement; and prepare correct endorsements for assigned events.

**Instructor:** deliver the ground lesson, have the learner look up each regulation and the matching AC 61-65K sample endorsement, and require the learner to write out correct endorsements for a solo, a solo cross-country, and a practical-test recommendation.

## Completion Standards

The learner can explain and correctly prepare:

- **Required logbook entries** — § 61.51(a), and the entries required for training given.
- **When logbook entries are required.**
- **Required training and endorsements** for student solo, solo cross-country, and the private-pilot practical test.
- **Flight-instructor record-keeping** requirements.
- **Flight-instructor certificate renewal and reinstatement** requirements.
- The risk of **endorsements without appropriate limitations or expiration dates**.

# Teach

Teach endorsements as the CFI's signature carrying legal weight: a logbook entry documents what was done, and an endorsement authorizes what the pilot may do next. Use the actual regulation for the requirement and **AC 61-65K Appendix A** for the exact sample wording. The Overview maps the ACS knowledge, risk, and skill elements; the sections below teach the material once, in the order an instructor would take a student from first lesson to checkride.

## AI.II.K.K1 — Logbook Entries and Entries for Instruction Given

A logbook entry documents training and aeronautical experience. The required entries and the entries for **instruction given** are governed by **§ 61.51**.

### When entries are required — § 61.51(a)

A pilot must log the training and aeronautical experience used to meet the requirements for a **certificate, rating, or flight review**, and the **recent flight experience (currency)** requirements.

### What every entry contains — § 61.51(b)

Each logbook entry includes the **date, total flight time, the points of departure and arrival (to/from), the aircraft type and identification, the name of the safety pilot if required, the type of pilot experience (solo, PIC, dual, etc.), and the conditions of flight (day, night, actual or simulated instrument)**.

### The entry for training given — § 61.51(h)

When an instructor logs training, the entry must be **endorsed legibly (signed)** by the authorized instructor and include the instructor's **certificate number** and **certificate expiration** (for example, "Exp 02/28/27") or recency-of-experience expiration (for example, "RE 02/28/27"), a **description of the training given**, and the **training time**.

[[highlight:
Flight Guide Instructor Principle |
Teach the learner to read a logbook entry against § 61.51: does it have the date, time, to/from, aircraft, type of experience, and conditions? For training, does it have the instructor's signature, certificate number and expiration, the description, and the time? A missing element makes the entry fail its purpose.
]]

## AI.II.K.K2 — Student Pilot Pre-Solo, Solo, and Cross-Country Endorsements

Student-pilot endorsements must be in the **make and model** the student will fly, and the training must precede the endorsement. Before any training endorsement, complete the prerequisites: the **TSA security endorsement (49 CFR § 1552.15(c))**, the **medical certificate** (MedXPress), and the **student pilot certificate** (IACRA).

### Pre-solo and solo

- **§ 61.87(b) — Pre-solo aeronautical knowledge:** a knowledge test on the applicable rules and the flight characteristics/operational limitations of the make and model (AC 61-65K **A.3**).
- **§ 61.87(c)(1) and (2) — Pre-solo flight training:** the required flight training in the make and model (§ 61.87(d)) (AC 61-65K **A.4**).
- **§ 61.87(n) — Initial 90-day solo** (AC 61-65K **A.6**); **§ 61.87(p) — additional 90-day solo** (AC 61-65K **A.7**). Note the **expiration** — the solo endorsement is time-limited.

### Solo cross-country

- **§ 61.93(b)(1) — Solo takeoffs and landings at an airport within 25 NM** (AC 61-65K **A.8**).
- **§ 61.93(c)(1) and (2) — Solo cross-country (general):** the cross-country training in the make and model, by the training instructor (AC 61-65K **A.9**).
- **§ 61.93(c)(3) — Solo cross-country (per flight):** the instructor who **reviewed the specific flight planning** endorses that particular cross-country (AC 61-65K **A.10**).

[[highlight:
Flight Guide Instructor Principle |
Distinguish the two cross-country endorsements. The general XC endorsement (A.9) says the student is trained; the per-flight endorsement (A.10) says an instructor reviewed this specific route, weather, and planning. A student who has the general endorsement still needs the per-flight review before each solo cross-country.
]]

## AI.II.K.K3 — Other Required Endorsements

Some operations require their own logbook endorsements:

- **Class B airspace — § 61.95:** ground and flight training and an endorsement to operate at the specific Class B primary airport (or the student/private-pilot provisions).
- **SFAR** — Special Federal Aviation Regulations may impose their own training and endorsement requirements (for example, special-awareness training).
- **Additional aircraft qualifications** — **complex, high-performance, and tailwheel** are **endorsements, not separate certificates** (§ 61.31). Each requires the specified flight training and an instructor endorsement in the logbook.

## AI.II.K.K4 — Recommending a Practical Test

Preparing a practical-test recommendation means assembling the right knowledge-test and practical-test endorsements for the certificate sought — **initial certification** (solo at 16, private pilot at 17), **additional pilot certification** (commercial/CFI at 18), or **additional aircraft qualification**.

### Initial pilot certification (private pilot)

- **§ 61.35(a)(1) — Review of a home-study curriculum** (AC 61-65K **A.86**; see AC 61-65K section 10).
- **§§ 61.35(a)(1), 61.103(d), 61.105 — Knowledge test** (AC 61-65K **A.36**).
- **§ 61.39(a)(6)(i) and (ii) — Prerequisites for the practical test** (AC 61-65K **A.1**) — logged training within **2 calendar months**.
- **§ 61.39(a)(6)(iii) — Review of knowledge-test deficiencies** (AC 61-65K **A.2**).
- **§§ 61.103(f), 61.107(b), 61.109 — Flight proficiency / practical test** (AC 61-65K **A.37**).

### Additional certification and aircraft qualification

- **All checkrides** require the prerequisites endorsement (**A.1**) and the knowledge-test-deficiency review (**A.2**), plus the relevant knowledge and practical test endorsements.
- **Added class** (for example, adding airplane single-engine to an existing certificate): usually **no written test and no minimum training time**, and the checkride still requires A.1 and A.2. See **§ 61.63(b) or (c)** (AC 61-65K **A.78**).
- **Solo without category/class** — § 61.31(d)(2) (AC 61-65K **A.76**).

## AI.II.K.K5 — The Flight Review Endorsement

A **flight review** under **§ 61.56(a) and (c)** requires **1 hour of flight and 1 hour of ground** instruction. On satisfactory completion, the instructor endorses the pilot's logbook (AC 61-65K **A.69**). **An unsuccessful flight review is not endorsed as completed** — the pilot simply does not receive the endorsement, and there is no logbook "failure" entry.

## AI.II.K.K6 — Required Flight Instructor Records

Under **§ 61.189(b)**, the flight instructor must **maintain records for 3 years**, including the **name of each person endorsed for solo** (with the date of the endorsement) and each person endorsed for a **knowledge or practical test**. These records document the instructor's endorsements if a question later arises.

## AI.II.K.K7 — Renewal and Reinstatement

The flight-instructor certificate itself has **recency-of-experience (§ 61.197)** and **reinstatement** requirements — see AC 61-65K sections 30–31 for the renewal methods and the reinstatement path when the certificate expires. Teach the learner that the instructor's own currency is a regulatory requirement, not a formality.

## AI.II.K.R1 — Risk Management: Endorsements Without Limitations or Expiration

The named risk in this Task is an **endorsement without appropriate limitations or expiration dates** — and, more broadly, an endorsement that is missing, wrong, or premature.

### How the Risk Develops

- A solo endorsement without its 90-day expiration or the required limitations.
- A per-flight cross-country endorsement signed without reviewing the actual route and weather.
- A practical-test recommendation without the within-2-calendar-months training or the deficiency review.
- A missing signature, certificate number, or expiration that makes the entry invalid.

### Instructor Mitigation

1. **Recognize** — every endorsement has required elements and, where applicable, a limitation and an expiration.
2. **Assess** — is the required training complete, in the right make and model, and current?
3. **Mitigate** — use the exact AC 61-65K wording, apply the required limitations and the expiration date, and confirm the prerequisites before signing.
4. **Verify** — read the endorsement back against the regulation and the sample before the student depends on it.

### Common Errors and Corrections

- **Endorsing before the training:** complete the required make-and-model training first, then endorse.
- **Omitting the expiration or limitations:** a solo endorsement is time-limited; a cross-country per-flight endorsement is flight-specific. Apply them.
- **Confusing the two cross-country endorsements:** general XC training (A.9) versus the per-flight planning review (A.10).
- **An invalid training entry:** missing signature, certificate number/expiration, description, or time fails § 61.51(h).
- **Not keeping instructor records:** § 61.189(b) requires retaining endorsement records for 3 years.

## Teaching the Task — AI.II.K.S1

The ACS skill element requires the applicant to **describe and prepare** logbook entries and endorsements for **at least two** events from K1–K5. Have the learner write out, from a scenario you assign, the correct entries and endorsements — for example, a **first solo** (pre-solo knowledge, pre-solo training, and the 90-day solo endorsement) and a **practical-test recommendation** (A.1, A.2, and the practical-test endorsement). Check each against the regulation and the AC 61-65K sample wording, and confirm the limitations and expiration dates are present.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

1. When must a pilot log flight time, and what does § 61.51(b) require in every entry?

[[answer:
§ 61.51(a) requires logging the training and aeronautical experience used to meet the requirements for a certificate, rating, or flight review, and the recent-experience (currency) requirements. Per § 61.51(b), each entry includes the date, total flight time, points of departure and arrival, aircraft type and identification, the safety-pilot name if required, the type of pilot experience (solo/PIC/dual/etc.), and the conditions of flight (day/night/actual or simulated instrument).
]]

2. What must a logbook entry for training given contain?

[[answer:
Per § 61.51(h), the entry must be endorsed legibly (signed) by the authorized instructor and include the instructor's certificate number and certificate (or recency-of-experience) expiration, a description of the training given, and the training time.
]]

3. What endorsements and prerequisites are required before a student pilot's first solo?

[[answer:
First the prerequisites: the TSA security endorsement (49 CFR § 1552.15(c)), a medical certificate, and a student pilot certificate. Then, in the make and model: the pre-solo aeronautical knowledge test (§ 61.87(b); A.3), the pre-solo flight training (§ 61.87(c)(1)–(2); A.4), and the initial 90-day solo endorsement (§ 61.87(n); A.6), with additional 90-day solos under § 61.87(p) (A.7).
]]

4. What is the difference between the two solo cross-country endorsements?

[[answer:
The general solo cross-country endorsement (§ 61.93(c)(1)–(2); A.9) records that the student received cross-country training in the make and model from the training instructor. The per-flight endorsement (§ 61.93(c)(3); A.10) is signed by the instructor who reviewed the specific route, weather, and planning for that particular cross-country. A student needs both — training once, and a review before each solo XC.
]]

5. Which aircraft qualifications are endorsements rather than separate certificates?

[[answer:
Complex, high-performance, and tailwheel are logbook endorsements under § 61.31, not separate certificates — each requires the specified flight training and an instructor endorsement. Class B airspace (§ 61.95) and certain SFAR operations also require their own endorsements.
]]

6. What endorsements are required to recommend a private-pilot applicant for the practical test?

[[answer:
The prerequisites endorsement (§ 61.39(a)(6)(i)–(ii); A.1) — including logged training within 2 calendar months — the review of knowledge-test deficiencies (§ 61.39(a)(6)(iii); A.2), and the flight-proficiency/practical-test endorsement (§§ 61.103(f), 61.107(b), 61.109; A.37), with the knowledge-test endorsement (A.36) and home-study review (A.86) as applicable.
]]

7. How is a flight review documented, and what happens if it is not completed satisfactorily?

[[answer:
A flight review under § 61.56(a) and (c) requires 1 hour of flight and 1 hour of ground instruction. On satisfactory completion the instructor endorses the logbook (A.69). If not completed satisfactorily, the instructor simply does not endorse it — no "failure" entry is made.
]]

8. What records must a flight instructor keep, and for how long?

[[answer:
Under § 61.189(b), the instructor must maintain records for 3 years, including the name of each person endorsed for solo (with the date) and each person endorsed for a knowledge or practical test.
]]

9. What is the named risk in this Task, and how do you manage it?

[[answer:
Endorsements without appropriate limitations or expiration dates — and missing, wrong, or premature endorsements generally. Manage it by recognizing each endorsement's required elements and expiration, assessing that the training is complete and current, using the exact AC 61-65K wording with the required limitations, and verifying the endorsement against the regulation before the student depends on it.
]]

## Completion Standard

The learner explains the required logbook entries and the entries for instruction given; the student-pilot pre-solo, solo, and cross-country endorsements and their prerequisites; the other required endorsements (Class B, SFAR, complex/high-performance/tailwheel); the endorsements for recommending a practical test; the flight-review endorsement; and the flight-instructor record-keeping and renewal/reinstatement requirements. The learner prepares at least two correct endorsements from assigned scenarios, with the required limitations and expiration dates, verified against the regulation and AC 61-65K.
