# Task E. Elements of Effective Teaching in a Professional Environment

**Area I — Fundamentals of Instructing**

## Objective

To determine the applicant understands effects of instructor behavior on effective teaching, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate appropriate skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

Aviation instructors are trainers, safety advocates, and role models. Effective instruction depends on the instructor's ability to help learners meet established standards while presenting a knowledgeable, professional, and respectful example.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested Schedule**

- Ground lesson: approximately 45–60 minutes
- Instructor-delivery practice and critique: approximately 15 minutes

**Equipment**

- Aviation Instructor's Handbook
- CFI Airplane ACS
- Whiteboard or tablet
- Flight Guide visual aids

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner Actions**

- Explain instructor and flight-instructor responsibilities.
- Describe methods for maintaining standards and minimizing learner frustration.
- Identify professional behaviors that support effective teaching.
- Deliver a short lesson using a positive, respectful, and organized approach.

**Instructor Actions**

- Teach to established standards and adapt the method to learner needs.
- Keep learners informed of objectives, progress, and deficiencies.
- Demonstrate safety practices, professionalism, and current technical knowledge.
- Evaluate learner performance using observable elements and specific correction.

## Completion Standards

The applicant can deliver ground or flight instruction on an assigned task in a manner consistent with aviation instructor responsibilities, flight-instructor responsibilities, professional characteristics, ethics, learner evaluation, and professional development.

# Teach

## FI.I.E.K1 — Aviation Instructor Responsibilities

Aviation instructors transfer knowledge, help learners meet and exceed established standards, measure performance against those standards, and provide positive reinforcement. Learners view instructors as role models, so instructors must project a knowledgeable and professional image.

### FI.I.E.K1a — Helping Learners

Learning should be enjoyable and interesting, but standards are not reduced to make a task easy. Satisfaction comes from meeting the challenge of a difficult task. State the objective for each lesson so the learner understands the purpose and can measure progress.

### FI.I.E.K1b — Providing Adequate Instruction

Learners differ in background, interests, temperament, thinking, ability, and confidence. The instructor learns about the individual and adjusts the teaching method as training progresses. For a discouraged learner, break a complex task into discrete elements and increase difficulty as confidence grows. For a fast learner, raise the performance demand without inventing deficiencies or using unfair criticism.

### FI.I.E.K1c — Training to Established Standards of Performance

The instructor trains the applicant to the established standards in applicable ACS tasks, subject areas, procedures, and maneuvers. The ACS is a testing document, not a teaching document; instruction must build the knowledge, skill, and judgment needed to meet the standard.

### FI.I.E.K1d — Emphasizing the Positive

Positive, professional instruction supports learning. Threat, fear, negative self-concept, and excessive information can interfere with perception and learning. Introduce normal operations before complex emergencies, adapt the method when needed, and present safe practices as supporting satisfying, efficient operations.

### FI.I.E.K1e — Minimizing Learner Frustrations

Motivate learners by relating the lesson to their goals. Keep them informed about expectations, progress, examinations, assignments, and deficiencies. Treat learners as individuals, give deserved credit, criticize constructively, remain consistent, and admit instructor errors when they occur.

## FI.I.E.K2 — Flight Instructor Responsibilities

Flight instruction is a habit-building period in which learners develop correct use of attention, memory, and judgment. The instructor helps mold a safe pilot, supervises training, and determines when a learner has demonstrated consistent proficiency for solo.

For solo readiness, the instructor observes the learner from preflight through engine shutdown and verifies consistent performance without instructor assistance in the tasks necessary for the flight. The instructor remains current and proficient in the training aircraft and teaches safe use of risk mitigation, positive aircraft control, control exchange, collision avoidance, checklist use, ADM, and other applicable ACS safety areas.

## FI.I.E.K3 — Flight Instructor Qualifications and Professionalism

A flight instructor is thoroughly familiar with the functions, characteristics, and proper use of the instruments, avionics, and aircraft systems used for training. The instructor remains current on pilot-training techniques, certification requirements, and safety procedures.

Professional instructors maintain high levels of knowledge, training, and currency. They are sincere and honest, accept learners as individuals, maintain a professional appearance and habits, use proper language, and demonstrate a calm, thoughtful, disciplined demeanor. They avoid contradictory directions, unreasonable demands, unfair criticism, and flippant behavior.

## FI.I.E.K4 — Professional Development

Aviation changes rapidly, so professional instructors continually develop their knowledge and teaching skill. They remain alert for ways to improve qualifications, effectiveness, and service to learners. Continuing education includes current technical publications, training courses, government programs, seminars, workshops, and professional organizations.

## FI.I.E.K5 — Instructor Ethics and Conduct

Instructor ethics and conduct are expressed through safety, good judgment, risk management, accountability, responsibility, courtesy, prudent operating practices, and adherence to applicable laws and regulations. An instructor's safety advocacy must be matched by consistent personal example, because learners commonly imitate instructor habits.

The Aviator's Model Code of Conduct offers broad guidance for airmanship, safety, training and proficiency, security, technology, environmental issues, and promotion of general aviation. It complements legal requirements but is not itself a standard.

## Instructor Application — Delivering a Professional Lesson

For the assigned instructional task, state the objective and standard, match the presentation to the learner's experience and readiness, and use a calm, respectful, positive manner. Observe performance, identify specific corrections, and keep the learner informed of progress. Demonstrate the safety and professional conduct expected in aviation operations.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What are the five aviation instructor responsibilities in Task E?

[[answer:
Helping learners, providing adequate instruction, training to established standards of performance, emphasizing the positive, and minimizing learner frustrations.
]]

### 2. How does an instructor help a discouraged learner without reducing standards?

[[answer:
Break a complex task into discrete elements, assign more readily attained goals, allow the learner to gain confidence in each element, and then increase goal difficulty while retaining the standard.
]]

### 3. Why is the ACS not a complete teaching document?

[[answer:
The ACS identifies the standards and critical tasks used for testing. The instructor still develops instruction that builds the knowledge, skill, judgment, and progressive practice needed to meet those standards.
]]

### 4. How can an instructor minimize learner frustration?

[[answer:
Motivate learners, keep them informed, approach them as individuals, give credit when due, criticize constructively, be consistent, and admit errors.
]]

### 5. What does a flight instructor verify before solo?

[[answer:
The learner consistently performs the required flight tasks without instructor assistance, from preflight through engine shutdown, including control, traffic-pattern, navigation, and communication skills appropriate to the flight.
]]

### 6. What behaviors characterize professionalism in an instructor?

[[answer:
Current knowledge and training, sincerity, acceptance and respect for learners, professional appearance and habits, a calm and disciplined demeanor, proper language, and consistent conduct.
]]

### 7. Why is professional development necessary for instructors?

[[answer:
Aviation changes rapidly. Continuing development keeps the instructor current in aviation knowledge, teaching methods, procedures, qualifications, and services provided to learners.
]]

### 8. How does instructor conduct affect safety?

[[answer:
Learners treat instructors as role models and may imitate their habits. Safety instruction is credible when the instructor consistently demonstrates the safety practices being taught.
]]
