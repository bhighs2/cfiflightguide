# Task C. Straight Climbs and Climbing Turns

**Area VIII - Fundamentals of Flight**

## Objective

To determine that the instructor applicant understands straight climbs and climbing turns, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate the maneuvers while simultaneously providing effective instruction, and analyze and correct common errors.

## Purpose

A climb is sustained by **excess power**, not elevator alone. This Task teaches the applicant to establish a climb attitude, set power, trim for airspeed, compensate for left-turning tendencies, maintain coordinated flight, and level off at a target altitude. Climbing turns combine climb control with bank, coordination, and increased workload.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 20 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.6-1.0 hour.
- Checklist, AFH/ACS, performance information, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and approved visuals.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Establish a straight climb, trim for the target climb speed, perform climbing turns in both directions, maintain outside scan and coordination, and level off at a target altitude.

**Instructor:** Use Binns' sequence of four fundamentals, controls/trim, climb aerodynamics, climb speeds, left-turning tendencies, integrated references, and climbing turns. Demonstrate while teaching without losing traffic awareness.

## Completion Standards

The applicant maintains outside scan, trims appropriately, establishes the normal climb attitude and airspeed, turns to assigned headings, holds heading within **+/-10 degrees**, airspeed within **+/-10 knots**, and levels off within **+/-100 feet** while providing effective instruction.

# Teach

## AI.VIII.C.K1 - Purpose of and Procedures for Straight Climbs and Climbing Turns

A straight climb is established by setting the climb attitude, applying the required power, maintaining coordination, and trimming for the target airspeed. A climbing turn combines the climb with bank and requires additional attention to pitch, power, rudder, heading, and traffic.

Procedure:

1. Clear the area and establish the desired direction.
2. Apply the required power smoothly and anticipate left-turning tendencies.
3. Establish the climb pitch attitude using the natural horizon.
4. Verify the target climb airspeed and trim.
5. For a climbing turn, bank smoothly with coordinated rudder and add the pitch/power compensation needed to maintain airspeed and climb performance.
6. Level off before the target altitude, reduce pitch, adjust power, and trim.

## AI.VIII.C.K2 - Flight Control and Trim Use

**Elevator** establishes pitch attitude and climb airspeed. **Throttle** supplies the excess power required for climb. **Rudder** counters left-turning tendencies and coordinates climbing turns. **Ailerons** establish bank. **Trim** relieves control pressure after attitude, power, and performance stabilize.

Do not fly the climb by mechanically pulling until the airspeed indicator shows a number. Establish the outside attitude, verify performance, and use smooth pressure. In a climbing turn, maintain the desired bank with small aileron inputs and use elevator/power to prevent altitude or airspeed deviations.

## AI.VIII.C.K3 - Pilot Visual References When Performing the Maneuver

Use the natural horizon and climb sight picture as the primary reference. The nose will appear higher than in level flight; in a climbing turn, judge bank from the panel/horizon relationship and use the attitude indicator as a brief cross-check.

Use the altimeter and VSI to verify climb performance, the airspeed indicator to verify the pitch/speed relationship, and the heading indicator to confirm direction. Return attention outside after each check.

## AI.VIII.C.K4 - Integrated Flight Instruction

Teach the learner to use outside references to establish attitude and instruments to verify performance. While demonstrating, explain the expected response before the input, then keep the explanation short enough to preserve control, scan, and collision avoidance.

Demonstrate climbs and climbing turns in both directions. Ask the learner to identify pitch, bank, rudder, airspeed, altitude trend, and heading without fixating on a single instrument.

## AI.VIII.C.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### 1. Airspeed Chasing

**Error:** Establishing climb pitch primarily by the airspeed indicator and chasing the airspeed.

**Correction:** Set pitch by the natural horizon, allow response, verify airspeed, and trim.

### 2. Excessive Elevator Pressure

**Error:** Applying elevator too aggressively and producing an excessive climb angle.

**Correction:** Use smooth pressure and the target attitude; respect the airplane's climb capability.

### 3. Rudder Errors in Climbing Turns

**Error:** Inadequate or inappropriate rudder during climbing turns.

**Correction:** Coordinate rudder with bank and use the slip/skid indication and aircraft feel.

### 4. Yaw During Climb

**Error:** Allowing yaw from inadequate right rudder pressure.

**Correction:** Anticipate left-turning tendencies and maintain coordinated rudder pressure.

### 5. Nose Fixation and One-Wing-Low Climb

**Error:** Fixating on the nose and climbing with one wing low.

**Correction:** Use both wingtips/horizon and maintain an outside scan.

### 6. Uncoordinated Climbing Turn

**Error:** Initiating a climbing turn without coordinated controls, resulting in little turn and one wing low.

**Correction:** Bank with coordinated rudder and maintain the climb attitude.

### 7. Slip Counteracting Climb

**Error:** A slip reduces climb performance and produces little or no altitude gain.

**Correction:** Center coordination, reduce unnecessary bank, and re-establish the climb attitude.

### 8. Unstable Pitch or Bank

**Error:** Inability to keep pitch and bank constant during climbing turns.

**Correction:** Stabilize pitch/bank separately, use small pressures, and trim after the condition is stable.

### 9. Exceeding Climb Capability

**Error:** Attempting to climb beyond the airplane's available performance.

**Correction:** Reduce pitch to preserve airspeed, accept the available climb performance, and reassess weight, density altitude, and power.

### 10. Poor Level-Off

**Error:** Excessive forward elevator during level-off causes altitude loss or low-G flight.

**Correction:** Begin level-off early, reduce pitch smoothly, adjust power, and trim without unloading the airplane.

## Risk Management

### AI.VIII.C.R1 - Distractions and Situational Awareness

Prioritize aircraft control, climb performance, traffic, and collision avoidance. Pause instruction or secondary tasks if workload increases.

### AI.VIII.C.R2 - Collision Hazards

Clear before climbing turns and continue scanning above, below, and in the turn direction. The nose-high climb attitude can hide traffic and obstacles.

## Skills

### AI.VIII.C.S1 - Establish, Maintain, and Level Off From Climbs and Climbing Turns

Clear, set power, establish climb attitude, trim for airspeed, coordinate turns, hold heading/airspeed, and level off smoothly at the target altitude.

### AI.VIII.C.S2 - Analyze and Correct Common Errors

Identify whether the deviation is pitch, power, bank, rudder, trim, scan, or workload related; apply a small correction and verify the result outside and inside.

# Fly

## Setup

- Select a safe practice area, altitude, heading, and climb speed.
- Clear the area and brief traffic, terrain, weather, and target altitude.
- Establish straight-and-level flight before each climb; verify exchange of controls.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Clear and establish the desired heading.
2. Apply climb power, set climb pitch, anticipate right rudder, and verify airspeed.
3. Trim after the climb stabilizes.
4. For a climbing turn, bank with coordinated rudder and add required pitch/power compensation.
5. Lead the level-off, lower pitch smoothly, adjust power, and trim.

## Learner Practice

Practice straight climbs and climbing turns in both directions, with target headings and altitudes. Require the learner to narrate outside attitude references, power, airspeed, rudder, and altitude trend.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### Pitch/Power and Airspeed

**Recognize:** Airspeed chasing, overpitch, or failure to climb.

**Correct:** Re-establish attitude outside, verify performance, and adjust power/pitch smoothly.

### Coordination and Track

**Recognize:** Yaw, slip/skid, one wing low, or turn not developing.

**Correct:** Coordinate rudder with bank, reduce overcontrol, and use both wingtips/horizon.

### Level-Off

**Recognize:** Overshoot, altitude loss, low-G sensation, or excessive forward pressure.

**Correct:** Lead the level-off, lower pitch gradually, adjust power, and trim.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching straight climbs and climbing turns: outside scan, climb attitude, power, airspeed, trim, coordination, heading control, level-off, risk management, and common-error correction.

[[answer:
A sustained climb requires excess power: power available must exceed power required for level flight.

[[answer:
The airplane needs coordinated bank and additional pitch/power compensation for the loss of vertical lift, while maintaining airspeed and heading control.
