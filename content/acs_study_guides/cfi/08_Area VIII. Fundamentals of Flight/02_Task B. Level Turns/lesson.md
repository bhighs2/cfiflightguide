# Task B. Level Turns

**Area VIII - Fundamentals of Flight**

## Objective

To determine that the instructor applicant understands level turns, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate the maneuver while simultaneously providing effective instruction, and analyze and correct common errors.

## Purpose

Level turns introduce the pilot to the relationship among **bank, lift, load factor, pitch, power, rudder, and coordination**. A level turn is not produced by yawing the nose with rudder; the airplane turns because bank redirects part of the lift toward the center of the turn.

For the instructor applicant, teach a repeatable visual process: clear the turn, bank with coordinated controls, compensate for reduced vertical lift, hold the bank and altitude, lead the rollout, and return to straight-and-level flight without overcontrol.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 20 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.6-1.0 hour, adjusted for conditions and traffic.
- Checklist, AFH/ACS, airport or practice-area references, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and approved Flight Guide visuals.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain turn aerodynamics and control use, clear in the direction of turn, establish and maintain a coordinated level turn, roll out on heading, and analyze the supplied common errors.

**Instructor:** Use the Binns sequence of four fundamentals, controls/trim, turn mechanics, level-turn altitude/heading control, outside/inside references, coordination, power, and error correction. Demonstrate while teaching without allowing the lesson to degrade traffic scanning.

## Completion Standards

The learner explains the purpose, controls, trim, visual references, integrated instruction, risks, and common errors. During flight, the applicant maintains outside scan and coordinated flight, trims appropriately, turns and rolls out within **+/-15 degrees**, maintains altitude within **+/-100 feet** and airspeed within **+/-10 knots**, while providing effective instruction.

# Teach

## AI.VIII.B.K1 - Purpose of and Procedures for Level Turns

A level turn maintains **constant altitude and airspeed** while changing heading. The procedure is:

1. **Clear** in the direction of turn and confirm traffic/terrain/weather awareness.
2. **Bank** smoothly with coordinated aileron and rudder.
3. Add appropriate **back pressure/power** to compensate for reduced vertical lift and increased drag.
4. **Neutralize controls** as the desired bank is reached and hold the bank.
5. Lead the desired heading, then apply opposite aileron to roll wings level.
6. Neutralize the ailerons and adjust pitch/power to return to straight-and-level flight.

The airplane turns through **bank**, not rudder alone. A flat, rudder-only turn is uncoordinated and increases stall/spin risk.

## AI.VIII.B.K2 - Flight Control and Trim Use

**Ailerons** establish and remove bank. **Elevator** maintains the pitch attitude needed to hold altitude. **Rudder** coordinates the turn, counters adverse yaw during roll-in/roll-out, and keeps the ball centered. **Power** supports airspeed and altitude as drag/load factor change.

During a constant-bank turn, the ailerons are near neutral after the desired bank is established. Small elevator/power changes hold altitude and airspeed. Trim only after the attitude and performance stabilize; trim relieves pressure but does not establish the maneuver.

Use a light grip and **control pressures rather than large control movements**. Overcontrol creates oscillation in bank, pitch, and heading.

## AI.VIII.B.K3 - Pilot Visual References When Performing the Maneuver

Use the **natural horizon, wingtip relationship, cowling/panel sight picture, and outside landmarks** as primary references. The nose may appear to rise or fall during a turn because the pilot is offset from the aircraft centerline; use the panel/horizon relationship and wing references rather than the nose alone.

Briefly cross-check the attitude indicator, heading, altimeter, airspeed, and turn coordinator. Instruments verify performance; they do not replace the outside scan in VMC.

## AI.VIII.B.K4 - Integrated Flight Instruction

The FAA integrated method combines outside visual attitude control with brief instrument verification. Approximately **90 percent of attention** should remain outside for attitude and traffic; instrument checks should be brief and purposeful.

While demonstrating, explain the learner's scan and the expected aircraft response without allowing instruction to interrupt control, clearing, or collision avoidance. Ask the learner to identify the outside cue, the confirming instrument, the correction, and the result.

Teach both left and right turns. Do not allow the learner to gain proficiency in only one direction or to use a habitual seated lean as a substitute for a level sight picture.

## AI.VIII.B.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### 1. Inadequate Clearing

**Error:** Failure to clear adequately in the direction of turn.

**Correction:** Clear before each turn and maintain an outside scan throughout the maneuver.

### 2. Altitude Deviation

**Error:** Gaining or losing altitude during the turn.

**Correction:** Add measured elevator/power compensation for reduced vertical lift; use the altimeter as a brief performance check.

### 3. Bank-Angle Drift

**Error:** Failing to hold the desired bank constant.

**Correction:** Establish the target bank, neutralize the ailerons, and make small corrections rather than chasing the attitude.

### 4. Instrument-Only Turn

**Error:** Attempting the turn solely by instrument reference.

**Correction:** Re-establish the outside horizon/wing reference, then use instruments to verify heading, altitude, and coordination.

### 5. Leaning Away From the Turn

**Error:** Leaning away from the direction of turn while seated.

**Correction:** Sit centered and use the airplane's reference points; do not move the body to manufacture a bank indication.

### 6. Insufficient Feel for Slip or Skid

**Error:** Inability to detect uncoordinated flight without instruments.

**Correction:** Teach pressure, sound, seat-of-the-pants cues, visual yaw, and the relationship between rudder and bank; verify with the slip/skid indicator.

### 7. Nose-Only Bank Reference

**Error:** Attempting to hold bank by referencing only the nose.

**Correction:** Use both wingtips and the horizon; the nose alone can hide a wing-low condition.

### 8. Flat Skidding Turn

**Error:** Using rudder to make a flat turn instead of banking.

**Correction:** Bank toward the turn with coordinated rudder. Do not use a skid to avoid banking.

### 9. Excessive Rudder in the Turn

**Error:** Holding too much rudder in the direction of turn.

**Correction:** Reduce rudder to restore coordination; bank establishes the turn and rudder only coordinates it.

### 10. One-Direction Proficiency

**Error:** Practicing turns in only one direction.

**Correction:** Alternate left and right turns and compare control feel, visual references, and rollout timing.

### 11. Failure to Coordinate Controls

**Error:** Aileron, elevator, and rudder inputs are not coordinated.

**Correction:** Roll in/out with coordinated rudder, compensate pitch for altitude, and make smooth proportional corrections.

## Risk Management

### AI.VIII.B.R1 - Distractions and Situational Awareness

Keep instruction, radio, checklist, and cockpit tasks subordinate to aircraft control and outside scan. If the learner becomes overloaded, simplify the task and restore straight-and-level flight before continuing.

### AI.VIII.B.R2 - Collision Hazards

Clear before the turn and scan both wingtips, the area ahead, and the turn path. Do not let head-down instrument work, instruction, or correction eliminate traffic awareness.

## Skills

### AI.VIII.B.S1 - Establish, Maintain, and Roll Out of a Level Turn

Clear, bank smoothly, coordinate rudder, compensate for altitude/airspeed, hold the desired bank, lead the rollout, level the wings, and trim after stabilization.

### AI.VIII.B.S2 - Analyze and Correct Common Errors

Identify the error from the outside picture, aircraft feel, or instrument trend; apply a timely, measured correction; and return to the desired bank, altitude, airspeed, and coordination.

# Fly

## Setup

- Select a suitable practice area, altitude, heading, and airspeed.
- Clear in both directions and brief collision avoidance, terrain, weather, and task priorities.
- Establish straight-and-level flight before each turn; verify positive exchange of controls.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Clear in the direction of turn.
2. Bank smoothly with coordinated aileron/rudder.
3. Add elevator back pressure and power as necessary to maintain altitude/airspeed.
4. Neutralize ailerons at the desired bank and hold the turn using outside references.
5. Lead the rollout, apply opposite aileron, and level the wings.
6. Neutralize controls, adjust pitch/power, trim, and verify straight-and-level performance.

## Learner Practice

Practice left and right level turns with headings selected by the learner or instructor. Require a clearing scan before each turn and brief verbal identification of bank, pitch, altitude trend, airspeed, and coordination.

Introduce mild distractions only after the learner can maintain a stable turn. Return to straight-and-level flight whenever workload, traffic, or weather awareness degrades.

## Instructor Demonstration Technique

Use progressive cues:

- **Entry:** “Clear, bank, coordinate, compensate.”
- **Maintain:** “Wings/panel reference, altitude, airspeed, ball.”
- **Rollout:** “Lead heading, opposite aileron, level, neutralize.”
- **Correction:** “Small pressure, wait for response, verify outside.”

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### Altitude and Bank Oscillation

**Recognize:** The airplane climbs/descends or bank oscillates during the turn.

**Correct:** Stabilize bank first, use small pitch/power corrections, and avoid chasing the instruments.

### Slip/Skid or Rudder-Only Control

**Recognize:** Ball displacement, sideways seat pressure, one wing low, or nose yaw without appropriate bank.

**Correct:** Coordinate rudder with aileron, level the wings, and re-enter with a measured bank.

### Instrument Fixation or Traffic Scan Loss

**Recognize:** Learner stares inside or misses traffic during clearing/turning.

**Correct:** Simplify, restore outside scan, and use brief instrument confirmation only.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching level turns: clearing, coordinated bank, altitude/airspeed compensation, outside visual references, integrated instrument cross-check, controlled rollout, trim, and common-error correction in both directions.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. Why does an airplane turn when it banks?

[[answer:
Banking redirects part of the lift vector horizontally toward the center of the turn. Rudder coordinates the turn but does not replace bank.
]]

### 2. How do you maintain altitude in a level turn?

[[answer:
As bank reduces the vertical component of lift, use appropriate back pressure/pitch and, as needed, power to maintain altitude and airspeed. Use the altimeter and airspeed as brief performance checks.
]]

### 3. What is the role of rudder?

[[answer:
Rudder coordinates the airplane, counters adverse yaw during roll-in/roll-out, and keeps the ball centered. Excessive rudder or rudder-only turning creates a skid or slip.
]]

### 4. What visual references should the learner use?

[[answer:
Use the natural horizon, wingtips, cowling/panel sight picture, and outside landmarks. The wingtips are especially useful for detecting one-wing-low attitude; instruments verify performance but should not be chased.
]]

### 5. What are the supplied AFH common errors?

[[answer:
Inadequate clearing; altitude gain/loss; inconsistent bank; instrument-only flying; leaning away from the turn; poor slip/skid feel; nose-only bank reference; flat skidding turns; excessive rudder; practicing only one direction; and failure to coordinate the controls.
]]

### 6. What is the proper rollout sequence?

[[answer:
Lead the desired heading, apply opposite aileron to return the wings level, neutralize the ailerons, adjust pitch/power to recover straight-and-level performance, and trim after stabilization.
]]
