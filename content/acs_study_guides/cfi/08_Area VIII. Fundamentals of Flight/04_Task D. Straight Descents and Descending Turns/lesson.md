# Task D. Straight Descents and Descending Turns

**Area VIII - Fundamentals of Flight**

## Objective

To determine that the instructor applicant understands straight descents and descending turns, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate the maneuvers while simultaneously providing effective instruction, and analyze and correct common errors.

## Purpose

Descents introduce energy management, reduced-power engine operation, glide awareness, and the relationship among pitch, power, airspeed, and rate of descent. Descending turns combine those skills with bank, coordination, and collision avoidance.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 20 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.6-1.0 hour.
- Checklist, AFH/ACS, POH/AFM engine-operation guidance, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and approved visuals.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Establish straight descents and descending turns, maintain target airspeed/heading/rate, use trim and appropriate power, apply carburetor heat or clear the engine as applicable, and recover smoothly to level flight.

**Instructor:** Use Binns' sequence of controls/trim, outside references, reduced power, descent forces, airspeed, downwash, engine considerations, glides, descending turns, and error correction.

## Completion Standards

The applicant maintains outside scan and coordinated flight, establishes and trims an appropriate descent, turns to assigned headings, maintains heading within **+/-10 degrees**, airspeed within **+/-10 knots**, and levels off within **+/-100 feet** while providing effective instruction.

# Teach

## AI.VIII.D.K1 - Purpose of and Procedures for Straight Descents and Descending Turns

A straight descent is established by reducing power, allowing the nose to lower, setting the desired descent attitude/airspeed, trimming, and maintaining the desired heading and rate. A descending turn combines the descent with coordinated bank and requires appropriate pitch/ power compensation.

Procedure:

1. Clear the area and establish the target heading/altitude/airspeed.
2. Reduce power smoothly; anticipate the nose-down tendency.
3. Establish the descent pitch attitude and trim for airspeed.
4. Use the VSI/altimeter to verify the rate and altitude trend.
5. For a descending turn, bank with coordinated rudder and maintain airspeed/heading control.
6. Lead the level-off, add power, raise pitch smoothly, and trim.

### Glides

A **glide** is a power-off or reduced-power descent. Use the POH/AFM best-glide speed and configuration. Pitch controls airspeed; the selected landing area and geometry determine the desired path. Never raise the nose to stretch a glide; lower the nose to preserve airspeed and select the safest reachable area.

## AI.VIII.D.K2 - Flight Control and Trim Use

**Elevator** establishes descent attitude and airspeed. **Throttle** controls energy and descent rate when power is available. **Rudder** coordinates descending turns. **Ailerons** establish bank. **Trim** relieves pressure after the descent stabilizes.

Power reduction can cause the nose to drop because propeller downwash over the horizontal stabilizer decreases. Anticipate the change, establish the desired attitude, then trim. In a descending turn, maintain coordination and compensate for reduced vertical lift.

## AI.VIII.D.K3 - Pilot Visual References When Performing the Maneuver

Use the natural horizon and descending sight picture as primary references. The nose is lower than in level flight; in a descending turn, judge bank from the panel/horizon and wing relationship. Briefly verify airspeed, altitude, VSI, heading, and coordination with instruments.

For a glide, use outside terrain/landing-area references and the airspeed attitude. Maintain a broad scan rather than fixating on the desired landing point.

## AI.VIII.D.K4 - Integrated Flight Instruction

Teach the learner to establish attitude outside, verify performance inside, and return outside. Explain the expected effects of power reduction, downwash change, trim, bank, rudder, and engine precautions while preserving aircraft control and collision avoidance.

Practice both left and right descending turns, straight powered descents, and glides so the learner understands the difference between reduced-power descent and power-off energy management.

## AI.VIII.D.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### 1. Inadequate Clearing

**Error:** Failure to clear for traffic in the turn direction or descent.

**Correction:** Clear before entry and keep scanning during the descent/turn.

### 2. Excessive Glide Angle

**Error:** Inadequate elevator back pressure during glide entry produces an overly steep glide.

**Correction:** Lower the nose only enough to establish the target airspeed; trim and stabilize.

### 3. Speed Before Pitch

**Error:** Lowering pitch before slowing to approximate glide speed.

**Correction:** Establish the target speed/attitude before settling into the glide.

### 4. Instrument-Only Glide

**Error:** Establishing/maintaining the glide solely by instruments.

**Correction:** Use the outside horizon and landing-area references, then briefly verify instruments.

### 5. Poor Sensory Awareness

**Error:** Inability to sense airspeed through sound and feel.

**Correction:** Develop awareness of control pressure, airflow sound, vibration, and aircraft response.

### 6. Chasing Airspeed

**Error:** Inability to stabilize the glide while chasing the airspeed indicator.

**Correction:** Set attitude, allow stabilization, then make small proportional corrections.

### 7. Stretching the Glide

**Error:** Applying back pressure to reach a distant landing point.

**Correction:** Lower pitch to preserve airspeed and select the safest reachable area.

### 8. Uncoordinated Gliding Turns

**Error:** Skidding/slipping and failing to recognize different rudder forces without power.

**Correction:** Coordinate with rudder and use the slip/skid indicator, sound, and seat-of-the-pants cues.

### 9. Failure to Lower Pitch in Turn Entry

**Error:** Airspeed decreases when entering a gliding turn.

**Correction:** Lower pitch as bank is established and maintain the target airspeed.

### 10. Excessive Rudder During Recovery

**Error:** Excessive rudder pressure during recovery from a gliding turn.

**Correction:** Relax rudder smoothly as the wings level; avoid an abrupt yaw.

### 11. Poor Straight-Glide Recovery

**Error:** Inadequate pitch control during recovery from a straight glide.

**Correction:** Raise pitch smoothly, add power as appropriate, prevent airspeed loss, and trim.

### 12. Low-Altitude Cross-Control

**Error:** Cross-controlling during gliding turns near the ground.

**Correction:** Maintain coordination and avoid steep/low turns; select a safer path or go around when available.

### 13. Bank Drift

**Error:** Failure to maintain constant bank during gliding turns.

**Correction:** Hold the outside bank reference and make small aileron corrections.

## Risk Management

### AI.VIII.D.R1 - Distractions and Situational Awareness

Prioritize aircraft control, airspeed, terrain/traffic, and the descent path. Simplify the task if workload rises.

### AI.VIII.D.R2 - Collision Hazards

Clear before turns and maintain an outside scan throughout descent and glide. Descending turns can move the airplane through traffic layers quickly.

## Skills

### AI.VIII.D.S1 - Establish, Maintain, and Level Off

Clear, reduce power, establish attitude/airspeed, trim, coordinate turns, maintain heading/rate, and level off smoothly with appropriate power and pitch.

### AI.VIII.D.S2 - Analyze and Correct Common Errors

Identify deviations in pitch, airspeed, bank, coordination, power, trim, scan, or engine management; apply a measured correction and verify the result.

# Fly

## Setup

- Select a safe practice area, altitude, heading, target airspeed, descent rate, and level-off altitude.
- Brief engine precautions, carburetor heat/engine clearing as applicable, traffic, terrain, and recovery.
- Establish straight-and-level flight before each descent or glide.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Clear the area and reduce power smoothly.
2. Allow the nose to lower, establish the descent attitude/airspeed, and trim.
3. Verify heading, airspeed, altitude trend, and rate with brief instrument checks.
4. For a descending turn, bank with coordinated rudder and maintain the target airspeed/rate.
5. For a glide, establish best glide/POH speed, maintain outside landing-area scan, and avoid stretching.
6. Lead the level-off, add power, raise pitch smoothly, and trim.

## Learner Practice

Practice straight descents, descending turns in both directions, and glides. Require the learner to verbalize power, attitude, airspeed, trim, coordination, engine precautions, and level-off lead point.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### Speed and Glide Stabilization

**Recognize:** Airspeed chasing, overly steep glide, or pitch changes that destabilize the descent.

**Correct:** Establish attitude, verify speed, trim, and use power/path control appropriately; never stretch the glide.

### Coordination and Bank

**Recognize:** Slip/skid, excessive rudder, cross-control, or changing bank.

**Correct:** Use coordinated aileron/rudder, maintain the outside bank reference, and reduce bank or recover when near the ground.

### Engine and Level-Off Management

**Recognize:** Forgotten carburetor heat/engine clearing, abrupt pitch, altitude loss, or low-G level-off.

**Correct:** Follow POH/AFM precautions, lead the level-off, add power smoothly, and maintain safe airspeed.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching straight descents, descending turns, and glides: outside scan, pitch/power/trim, airspeed/rate control, coordination, engine management, heading control, smooth level-off, risk management, and common-error correction.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. How is a descent initiated?

[[answer:
Reduce power, establish the nose-low descent attitude, adjust pitch for airspeed, and trim. Anticipate the nose-down effect of reduced power/downwash.
]]

### 2. How is a glide different from a powered descent?

[[answer:
A glide uses idle or no power and relies on pitch/airspeed and geometry for energy management. Use best-glide/POH speed and never stretch the glide by pulling.
]]

### 3. What changes in a descending turn?

[[answer:
Add coordinated bank and maintain airspeed while compensating for the changed lift components and turn forces. Lower pitch as needed when entering the gliding turn.
]]

### 4. Name the supplied AFH common errors.

[[answer:
Inadequate clearing, excessive glide angle, lowering pitch before slowing, instrument-only glide, poor sensory awareness, airspeed chasing, stretching the glide, uncoordinated gliding turns, failure to lower pitch in turn entry, excessive rudder on recovery, poor straight-glide recovery, low-altitude cross-control, and bank drift.
]]
