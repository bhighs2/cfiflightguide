# Task F. Before Takeoff Check

**Area V — Preflight Procedures**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands before-takeoff checks, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate the required skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

No pilot wants an unexpected engine or flight-control failure, and few are more dangerous than one on takeoff. The before-takeoff check and run-up confirm the engine and airplane are airworthy and functioning before the pilot commits to the takeoff roll. A flight instructor must perform the check properly, brief the departure, and teach the procedure and the judgment behind it.

This is a ground and aircraft Task. The ACS skill elements require the applicant to **review takeoff performance, complete the checklist, position the airplane, divide attention, and verify the engine parameters and configuration**.

## Schedule / Equipment

- Preflight ground: 5 minutes in the airplane
- Initial: 5 minutes before flight — introduction and demonstration
- Every flight: 5 minutes before flight — learner performs
- Airplane checklist

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** explain the purpose of the before-takeoff check and how to execute it, then perform it without assistance — position the airplane, follow the checklist and perform a flight-control check, perform the engine run-up (temperatures and pressures, magnetos, carburetor heat, idle check), perform a pre-departure briefing, and perform the pre-lineup and lineup check.

**Instructor:** deliver the ground lesson in the airplane, demonstrate the procedure, and debrief after each flight.

## Completion Standards

- **Ground:** the learner can explain the purpose of the procedure and how to execute it properly.
- **Flight:** the learner performs the procedure without assistance — positioning the airplane appropriately for wind and other aircraft; following the appropriate checklists and performing a flight-control check; performing an engine run-up (temperatures and pressures, magnetos, carburetor heat, idle check); performing a pre-departure briefing (emergency procedures, V-speeds, departure plan); and performing the pre-lineup and lineup check (verifying the clearance, ensuring the runway is clear, confirming the takeoff runway).

### Purpose

**The before-takeoff check is the last systematic opportunity to find a problem before committing the airplane to flight.**

The lesson should answer one question:

**“Is the airplane, the runway, and the departure plan ready for takeoff?”**

Teach it in the same order the pilot actually makes that decision:

1. **Position the airplane safely for the run-up.**
2. **Use the checklist while dividing attention inside and outside.**
3. **Prove the engine, systems, instruments, and flight controls are normal.**
4. **Configure the airplane for takeoff.**
5. **Confirm takeoff performance and the departure plan.**
6. **Brief what happens if the takeoff does not go as planned.**
7. **Reassess runway, wake, and traffic conditions.**
8. **Make the final go/no-go decision.**

The checklist is not a ritual.

For every item, ask:

**“What am I checking, what should I see, and what result would make me stop?”**

---

### Position the Airplane for Run-Up

Before increasing power, choose a run-up position that is safe for the airplane and everyone around it.

Consider:

- **Wind.**
- **Other aircraft.**
- **People and vehicles.**
- **Buildings and open hangars.**
- **Loose gravel or debris.**
- **Surface condition.**

Whenever practical:

- Use a firm surface.
- Point into the wind for cooling.
- Avoid directing propeller blast toward people or property.
- Leave enough room to stop if the airplane begins to move.

After stopping:

- Set or hold the brakes as appropriate.
- Confirm the airplane is stable.
- Keep looking outside during the entire check.

This directly supports **AI.V.F.S3 — positioning the airplane with consideration for wind, aircraft, vessels, buildings, and other hazards.**

---

### Use the Checklist — But Keep Looking Outside

Now begin the aircraft-specific before-takeoff checklist.

The checklist:

- Puts the checks in a logical sequence.
- Reduces omissions.
- Standardizes the procedure.
- Helps detect a malfunction before takeoff.

But the pilot still has to divide attention.

While working inside, continue checking outside for:

- The airplane creeping forward.
- Traffic moving behind or beside you.
- Vehicles or people entering the area.
- Runway activity changing.
- Propeller-blast hazards.

**Do not let a run-up become a heads-down event.**

That is **AI.V.F.R1 — division of attention** and **AI.V.F.S4 — dividing attention inside and outside the airplane**.

---

### First Ask: Is the Engine Ready for a Run-Up?

Before applying run-up power, verify the engine is in the operating condition specified by the manufacturer.

Check the applicable:

- **Oil temperature.**
- **Oil pressure.**
- **Cylinder-head temperature**, if installed.
- **Fuel pressure or fuel flow**, if applicable.
- Other engine indications.

If an indication is abnormal or meaningfully different from what is normally expected:

**stop and determine why before continuing.**

Do not rationalize an abnormal indication because it is technically near a limit.

---

### Prove the Engine and Systems Are Normal

Now perform the run-up exactly as specified by the POH/AFM.

The purpose is not to move controls.

The purpose is to **observe whether each system responds normally.**

### Ignition / Magneto Check

On a conventional dual-magneto system:

- Select each magneto individually.
- Observe the RPM drop.
- Compare it with the POH limit.
- Compare the difference between the two magnetos.
- Listen for roughness.

Abnormal results include:

- **Excessive RPM drop.**
- **No RPM drop.**
- **Excessive difference between magnetos.**
- **Persistent roughness.**

A run-up outside the manufacturer's limits is not acceptable for takeoff.

### Carburetor Heat — If Equipped

If the airplane has a carburetor:

- Apply carburetor heat as directed by the POH.
- Verify the expected RPM response.
- Listen for roughness that could indicate carburetor ice.
- Return the system to the required takeoff position.

Teach the installed system.

Do not add a carburetor-heat check to a fuel-injected airplane.

### Propeller Check — If Equipped

If the airplane has a constant-speed propeller:

- Exercise the propeller control according to the POH.
- Verify the expected RPM or manifold-pressure response.
- Observe oil-pressure response when applicable.

Again:

**teach the airplane that is actually installed.**

### Electrical System

Verify the electrical system is operating normally.

Depending on the airplane, this may include:

- Voltage.
- Ammeter.
- Loadmeter.
- Alternator or generator indication.
- Annunciators.

The objective is to confirm:

- The charging system is online.
- The system is carrying the expected load.
- No abnormal electrical indication is present.

### Vacuum / Pressure or Electronic Flight System

If the airplane uses a vacuum or pressure system:

- Verify the system is within the manufacturer's range.
- Confirm the associated instruments are operating normally.

If the airplane uses electronic displays instead, verify the applicable:

- **AHRS.**
- **ADC.**
- **Backup instruments.**
- **Annunciations.**

Teach the installed system rather than a generic one.

---

### Verify the Flight Instruments

Before takeoff, the flight instruments should be:

- **Set.**
- **Reasonable.**
- **Operating normally.**

Check as applicable:

- Altimeter.
- Heading indicator or HSI.
- Magnetic compass comparison.
- Attitude indicator.
- Airspeed indicator.
- Turn coordinator.
- Vertical-speed indicator.

Any instrument that should have responded during taxi or turns should now make sense.

If an indication does not agree with the airplane's actual motion:

**resolve it before takeoff.**

---

### Verify the Flight Controls — Free and Correct

Move the controls through the appropriate range and verify:

- **Free movement.**
- **Correct movement.**
- **Correct direction.**

For a conventional airplane:

- Yoke/stick right → **right aileron up, left aileron down.**
- Yoke/stick left → **left aileron up, right aileron down.**
- Yoke/stick aft → **elevator up.**
- Yoke/stick forward → **elevator down.**
- Rudder pedals → **rudder moves in the commanded direction.**

Look outside at the control surfaces when practical.

Do not merely stir the controls and call the check complete.

---

### Configure the Airplane for Takeoff

Once the systems are proven normal, configure the airplane.

Verify the applicable:

- **Trim.**
- **Flaps.**
- **Fuel selector.**
- **Mixture.**
- **Propeller.**
- **Cowl flaps.**
- Other aircraft-specific configuration items.

Then set up the avionics so the airplane is ready for the initial departure.

Verify as applicable:

- Departure frequency.
- Navigation source.
- GPS flight plan.
- Initial course.
- Heading bug.
- Transponder code.
- ADS-B status.
- Flight director or autopilot settings.

Most importantly:

**confirm the active navigation source is the source you actually intend to use.**

The goal is to finish setup before entering the runway.

---

### Idle Check — If Required

If the POH requires an idle check:

- Reduce power as specified.
- Confirm the engine continues to operate normally at idle.
- Return to the appropriate power setting.

An engine that will not idle normally may become a problem later when power is reduced for landing.

---

### Confirm Takeoff Performance — §91.103

Now move from **“Is the airplane working?”** to **“Can it safely depart from this runway today?”**

Under **§91.103**, the PIC must become familiar with all available information concerning the flight, including runway lengths and takeoff and landing distance information appropriate to the aircraft and conditions.

Review performance using the actual:

- **Aircraft weight.**
- **Density altitude.**
- **Runway length.**
- **Runway surface.**
- **Runway slope.**
- **Wind.**
- **Temperature.**
- **Obstacles.**

Know:

- Expected takeoff distance.
- Expected climb performance.
- Available runway.
- Obstacle considerations.

Do not assume yesterday's performance applies today.

### Review the Applicable V-Speeds

Review the speeds applicable to the airplane and operation, such as:

- Rotation speed.
- **VX.**
- **VY.**
- Other manufacturer-defined takeoff speeds.

Use the POH/AFM.

Do not substitute speeds from another airplane.

---

### Brief the Departure Before Entering the Runway

Now turn the performance numbers into an actual plan.

A concise departure briefing should cover:

- **Runway.**
- **Wind.**
- **Takeoff configuration.**
- **Initial departure path.**
- **Relevant V-speeds.**
- **Expected climb.**
- **What you will do if something goes wrong.**

The point is not to recite a script.

The point is to make the important decisions **before the airplane begins accelerating.**

---

### Brief an Engine Failure on the Takeoff Roll

Before takeoff, decide what happens if a serious malfunction occurs before liftoff.

The priorities are:

1. **Maintain directional control.**
2. **Reduce power.**
3. **Stop on the available runway.**
4. Use braking and the aircraft-specific procedure as appropriate.

Do not wait for the malfunction to happen before deciding whether you are stopping.

---

### Brief an Engine Failure After Liftoff

After liftoff, the available choices depend on:

- **Altitude.**
- **Remaining runway.**
- **Terrain.**
- **Wind.**
- **Aircraft performance.**
- **Obstacles.**
- **Airport environment.**

At low altitude, the priorities are:

1. **Maintain aircraft control.**
2. Establish the appropriate glide attitude.
3. Select the best available landing area.
4. Follow the aircraft-specific emergency procedure as time permits.

Do **not** teach a fixed “turn-back altitude” as a universal rule.

Any return-to-runway plan must be:

- Aircraft-specific.
- Airport-specific.
- Condition-specific.
- Based on training and demonstrated performance.

This is the core of **AI.V.F.R4 — the risk of powerplant failure or another malfunction during takeoff.**

---

### Reassess if the Runway Changes

If ATC changes the runway, the takeoff plan changes with it.

Before using the new runway, recheck:

- **Runway length.**
- **Wind.**
- **Takeoff performance.**
- **Obstacles.**
- **Taxi route.**
- **Departure path.**
- **Navigation setup.**

Then **re-brief the departure**.

Do not carry the old runway's assumptions onto the new runway.

That is **AI.V.F.R2 — unexpected runway changes.**

---

### Reassess Wake Turbulence

Before entering the runway, look at what has recently:

- Departed.
- Landed.
- Crossed the runway.
- Performed a low approach.

Wake vortices can **sink, drift, and remain near the runway**, especially in light winds.

If wake avoidance is questionable:

**wait.**

An ATC clearance does not remove the pilot's responsibility to avoid an unsafe wake encounter.

FAA guidance specifically advises pilots to adjust the flight path or delay operations when necessary to avoid wake turbulence.

That is **AI.V.F.R3 — wake turbulence**.

---

### Final Check Before Crossing the Hold Line

Before entering the runway:

At a towered airport:

- Verify the takeoff or runway-entry clearance.

At a nontowered airport:

- Make the appropriate CTAF call.

Then:

- **Verify the runway designation.**
- **Look both directions.**
- **Check final approach.**
- **Confirm traffic.**
- **Confirm no hold-short restriction remains.**

At night or in low visibility:

**slow down and positively identify the runway before entering it.**

If anything does not make sense:

**stop and ask.**

---

### Final Go / No-Go Decision

The before-takeoff check ends with a decision.

Ask:

- **Is the engine operating normally?**
- **Are the systems and instruments normal?**
- **Are the flight controls free and correct?**
- **Is the airplane configured correctly?**
- **Is takeoff performance adequate?**
- **Is the runway acceptable?**
- **Is the departure path acceptable?**
- **Is wake turbulence acceptable?**
- **Do I know what I will do if the engine fails?**
- **Am I comfortable committing this airplane to flight?**

If the answer to an important question is no:

**do not take off.**

---

### Common Errors

- Using memory instead of the checklist.
- Rushing the run-up.
- Poor run-up positioning.
- Prop blasting aircraft, people, or property.
- Fixating inside the cockpit.
- Failing to verify engine temperatures and pressures.
- Accepting marginal engine performance.
- Improper magneto/ignition check.
- Improper carburetor-heat or propeller check when applicable.
- Incomplete flight-control check.
- Failing to verify correct control direction.
- Failing to configure the airplane for takeoff.
- Incorrect navigation source or avionics setup.
- Failing to review takeoff performance.
- Failing to brief an abnormal takeoff.
- Teaching a universal turn-back altitude.
- Failing to re-brief after a runway change.
- Failing to consider wake turbulence.
- Entering the runway without verifying clearance, runway, and traffic.
- Continuing despite an unexplained abnormal indication.

## AI.V.F.K1 — Purpose of the Before-Takeoff Checklist Items

The before-takeoff check is the **systematic AFM/POH procedure for checking the engine, controls, systems, instruments, and avionics before flight**, performed after taxiing to a run-up position near the takeoff end of the runway. Teach the three sub-elements.

### K1a — Reasons for Checking Each Item

Each checklist item exists to confirm a system is functioning before the flight depends on it. Use the AFM/POH checklist, **read and do**, touching each control and confirming the result — **no hurry**. The systems checked on most airplanes:

- **Fuel system** — set per the AFM/POH, verified ON, and the correct tank(s) selected.
- **Trim** — set for takeoff (elevator, and rudder/aileron if equipped).
- **Flight controls** — checked through their **entire range** of movement; a partial check is not acceptable.
- **Engine operation** — the run-up confirms the engine develops power, the magnetos/FADEC are within limits, and the carburetor heat functions.
- **Electrical system** — voltage in range and the battery charging.
- **Vacuum system** — an acceptable vacuum (typically 4.8–5.2 inHg at 2,000 rpm) so the gyroscopic instruments indicate properly; allow them time to spool up.
- **Flight instruments** — rechecked and set; the directional gyro and the magnetic compass in agreement, and the heading bug set to the runway or assigned heading.
- **Avionics** — frequencies, navigation sources and courses, autopilot preselects, and transponder code set for the departure.

### K1b — Detecting Malfunctions

The run-up is a diagnostic. Teach the learner to detect the malfunction, not just complete the motion:

- **Magneto check** — a drop in rpm on each magneto (per the POH limit) and a smooth engine; a fouled plug or a dead mag shows as an excessive or absent drop. Note the EGT behavior if equipped. A bad mag check or plug fouling may be cleared by a brief lean and run-up, or it grounds the airplane.
- **Carburetor heat check** — a drop in rpm when heat is applied confirms it works; apply heat and watch for the drop and any roughness that indicates existing ice.

- **Idle check** — the engine idles smoothly without the throttle advancing.
- **Temperatures and pressures** — all in the green; an air-cooled engine may need time to warm to the minimum oil temperature before takeoff power. Head the airplane **into the wind** for the run-up to minimize overheating.

### K1c — Ensuring the Aircraft Is in Safe Operating Condition

The before-takeoff check confirms the aircraft is in the safe operating condition the manufacturer recommends. Position the airplane for the run-up on a **firm, debris-free surface** (loose objects hurled by the propeller damage the prop and the tail), clear of other aircraft and the taxiway, with nothing behind it in the propeller blast. Allow the airplane to roll slightly forward to align the nosewheel/tailwheel. Then complete the checklist, the **flight-control check**, and the configuration (flaps) for takeoff.

## The Pre-Departure Briefing and the Lineup Check

Beyond the checklist, two habits complete the before-takeoff discipline:

- **The pre-departure (takeoff) briefing** — made **out loud, even alone**. It covers the takeoff procedure, the V-speeds and the abort/reject point, the emergency procedures (land ahead, the turn-back altitude), and the expected departure (the pattern or turnout direction). Briefing the failure before it happens is what makes the answer immediate.
- **The pre-lineup and lineup check** — confirm the **clearance for takeoff** (or announce intentions on the CTAF), visually verify the **final approach is clear**, and confirm the **runway number agrees with the directional gyro/heading indicator** — preventing a runway incursion or a wrong-runway departure.

## Risk Management

### AI.V.F.R1 — Division of Attention During the Check

The pilot must **divide attention between the inside and the outside** during the run-up. A slipping parking brake or inadequate toe brakes can let the airplane creep forward unnoticed if attention is fixed inside. Split attention — one item inside, then a look outside.

### AI.V.F.R2 — Unexpected Runway Changes by ATC

A changed runway invalidates the takeoff plan and briefing. Re-brief the new runway, the new abort point, and the new departure before taking the runway — do not carry the old plan onto a new runway.

### AI.V.F.R3 — Wake Turbulence

A departing or landing aircraft ahead leaves wake turbulence. Note the traffic, allow the wake to dissipate or move, and apply the appropriate spacing before taking the runway.

### AI.V.F.R4 — Powerplant Failure or Malfunction During Takeoff

A powerplant failure on takeoff is the emergency the run-up and the briefing prepare for. Consider the operational factors — the airplane's characteristics, the runway/takeoff-path length, the surface conditions, the environmental conditions, and the obstructions — and set the abort point and the turn-back altitude accordingly. The briefing decides the answer before the roll begins.

### Common Errors and Corrections

- **Not using the appropriate checklist:** read and do, touch each item, confirm the result — every time.
- **Improper positioning of the airplane:** firm, debris-free surface, into the wind, clear of other aircraft and the prop blast.
- **Accepting marginal engine performance:** a bad mag check, abnormal temps/pressures, or a failed idle check grounds the airplane.
- **An improper flight-control check:** exercise the full range of movement, not a partial one.
- **Not configuring the aircraft for takeoff:** set the flaps, trim, and the required configuration.
- **Not reviewing the takeoff and emergency procedures:** brief them out loud before the roll.
- **Runway incursion / traffic conflict:** verify the clearance, the clear final, and the correct runway before taking the runway.

## Skills — Teaching the Task

The ACS skill elements require the applicant to **review performance, complete the checklist, position the airplane, divide attention, and verify the parameters and configuration**:

- **AI.V.F.S1 — Review takeoff performance:** the learner reviews the takeoff performance and briefs the departure, V-speeds, abort point, and emergencies.
- **AI.V.F.S2 — Complete the checklist:** the learner completes the before-takeoff checklist.
- **AI.V.F.S3 — Position the airplane:** the learner positions the airplane for wind and the presence of other aircraft, vessels, or buildings.
- **AI.V.F.S4 — Divide attention:** the learner divides attention inside and outside the flight deck during the check.
- **AI.V.F.S5 — Verify parameters and configuration:** the learner verifies the engine parameters and the airplane configuration are suitable for takeoff.

Divide attention between inside and outside because a slipping brake can let the airplane creep forward unnoticed while attention is fixed inside.
]]

5. What does the pre-departure (takeoff) briefing cover?

[[answer:
Said out loud, even alone: the takeoff procedure, the V-speeds and abort/reject point, the emergency procedures (land ahead, the turn-back altitude), and the expected departure (pattern or turnout). Briefing the failure before it happens makes the answer immediate.
]]

6. What does the pre-lineup and lineup check confirm?

[[answer:
The clearance for takeoff (or CTAF intentions announced), a visually clear final approach, and that the runway number agrees with the directional gyro/heading indicator — preventing a runway incursion or a wrong-runway departure.
]]

7. What are the four risk areas in this Task and their mitigations?

[[answer:
R1 division of attention — split inside/outside attention during the run-up. R2 unexpected runway changes — re-brief the new runway before taking it. R3 wake turbulence — note the traffic and allow the wake to dissipate. R4 powerplant failure on takeoff — consider the airplane, runway, surface, environment, and obstructions; set the abort point and turn-back altitude in the briefing.
]]

## Completion Standard

The learner explains the purpose of the before-takeoff check and its items, and performs it without assistance: reviews the takeoff performance, completes the checklist, positions the airplane appropriately, divides attention inside and outside, performs the run-up and detects malfunctions, verifies the engine parameters and configuration, briefs the departure, and performs the pre-lineup and lineup check.
