# Task F. Before Takeoff Check

**Area V — Preflight Procedures**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands before-takeoff checks, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate the required skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

No pilot wants an unexpected engine or flight-control failure, and few are more dangerous than one on takeoff. The before-takeoff check and run-up confirm the engine and airplane are airworthy and functioning before the pilot commits to the takeoff roll. A flight instructor must perform the check properly, brief the departure, and teach the procedure and the judgment behind it.

This is a ground and aircraft Task. The ACS skill elements require the applicant to **review takeoff performance, complete the checklist, position the airplane, divide attention, and verify the engine parameters and configuration**.

## Schedule / Equipment

- Preflight ground: 5 minutes in the airplane
- Initial: 5 minutes before flight — introduction and demonstration
- Every flight: 5 minutes before flight — learner performs
- Airplane checklist

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** explain the purpose of the before-takeoff check and how to execute it, then perform it without assistance — position the airplane, follow the checklist and perform a flight-control check, perform the engine run-up (temperatures and pressures, magnetos, carburetor heat, idle check), perform a pre-departure briefing, and perform the pre-lineup and lineup check.

**Instructor:** deliver the ground lesson in the airplane, demonstrate the procedure, and debrief after each flight.

## Completion Standards

- **Ground:** the learner can explain the purpose of the procedure and how to execute it properly.
- **Flight:** the learner performs the procedure without assistance — positioning the airplane appropriately for wind and other aircraft; following the appropriate checklists and performing a flight-control check; performing an engine run-up (temperatures and pressures, magnetos, carburetor heat, idle check); performing a pre-departure briefing (emergency procedures, V-speeds, departure plan); and performing the pre-lineup and lineup check (verifying the clearance, ensuring the runway is clear, confirming the takeoff runway).

# Teach

Teach the before-takeoff check as the last chance to catch a malfunction before committing to the takeoff roll. The Overview maps the ACS knowledge, risk, and skill elements; the sections below teach each element once, in ACS order. This Task has a single Knowledge element (K1) with three sub-elements — teach each.

## AI.V.F.K1 — Purpose of the Before-Takeoff Checklist Items

The before-takeoff check is the **systematic AFM/POH procedure for checking the engine, controls, systems, instruments, and avionics before flight**, performed after taxiing to a run-up position near the takeoff end of the runway. Teach the three sub-elements.

### K1a — Reasons for Checking Each Item

Each checklist item exists to confirm a system is functioning before the flight depends on it. Use the AFM/POH checklist, **read and do**, touching each control and confirming the result — **no hurry**. The systems checked on most airplanes:

- **Fuel system** — set per the AFM/POH, verified ON, and the correct tank(s) selected.
- **Trim** — set for takeoff (elevator, and rudder/aileron if equipped).
- **Flight controls** — checked through their **entire range** of movement; a partial check is not acceptable.
- **Engine operation** — the run-up confirms the engine develops power, the magnetos/FADEC are within limits, and the carburetor heat functions.
- **Electrical system** — voltage in range and the battery charging.
- **Vacuum system** — an acceptable vacuum (typically 4.8–5.2 inHg at 2,000 rpm) so the gyroscopic instruments indicate properly; allow them time to spool up.
- **Flight instruments** — rechecked and set; the directional gyro and the magnetic compass in agreement, and the heading bug set to the runway or assigned heading.
- **Avionics** — frequencies, navigation sources and courses, autopilot preselects, and transponder code set for the departure.

### K1b — Detecting Malfunctions

The run-up is a diagnostic. Teach the learner to detect the malfunction, not just complete the motion:

- **Magneto check** — a drop in rpm on each magneto (per the POH limit) and a smooth engine; a fouled plug or a dead mag shows as an excessive or absent drop. Note the EGT behavior if equipped. A bad mag check or plug fouling may be cleared by a brief lean and run-up, or it grounds the airplane.
- **Carburetor heat check** — a drop in rpm when heat is applied confirms it works; apply heat and watch for the drop and any roughness that indicates existing ice.

- **Idle check** — the engine idles smoothly without the throttle advancing.
- **Temperatures and pressures** — all in the green; an air-cooled engine may need time to warm to the minimum oil temperature before takeoff power. Head the airplane **into the wind** for the run-up to minimize overheating.

### K1c — Ensuring the Aircraft Is in Safe Operating Condition

The before-takeoff check confirms the aircraft is in the safe operating condition the manufacturer recommends. Position the airplane for the run-up on a **firm, debris-free surface** (loose objects hurled by the propeller damage the prop and the tail), clear of other aircraft and the taxiway, with nothing behind it in the propeller blast. Allow the airplane to roll slightly forward to align the nosewheel/tailwheel. Then complete the checklist, the **flight-control check**, and the configuration (flaps) for takeoff.

## The Pre-Departure Briefing and the Lineup Check

Beyond the checklist, two habits complete the before-takeoff discipline:

- **The pre-departure (takeoff) briefing** — made **out loud, even alone**. It covers the takeoff procedure, the V-speeds and the abort/reject point, the emergency procedures (land ahead, the turn-back altitude), and the expected departure (the pattern or turnout direction). Briefing the failure before it happens is what makes the answer immediate.
- **The pre-lineup and lineup check** — confirm the **clearance for takeoff** (or announce intentions on the CTAF), visually verify the **final approach is clear**, and confirm the **runway number agrees with the directional gyro/heading indicator** — preventing a runway incursion or a wrong-runway departure.

]]

## Risk Management

### AI.V.F.R1 — Division of Attention During the Check

The pilot must **divide attention between the inside and the outside** during the run-up. A slipping parking brake or inadequate toe brakes can let the airplane creep forward unnoticed if attention is fixed inside. Split attention — one item inside, then a look outside.

### AI.V.F.R2 — Unexpected Runway Changes by ATC

A changed runway invalidates the takeoff plan and briefing. Re-brief the new runway, the new abort point, and the new departure before taking the runway — do not carry the old plan onto a new runway.

### AI.V.F.R3 — Wake Turbulence

A departing or landing aircraft ahead leaves wake turbulence. Note the traffic, allow the wake to dissipate or move, and apply the appropriate spacing before taking the runway.

### AI.V.F.R4 — Powerplant Failure or Malfunction During Takeoff

A powerplant failure on takeoff is the emergency the run-up and the briefing prepare for. Consider the operational factors — the airplane's characteristics, the runway/takeoff-path length, the surface conditions, the environmental conditions, and the obstructions — and set the abort point and the turn-back altitude accordingly. The briefing decides the answer before the roll begins.

### Common Errors and Corrections

- **Not using the appropriate checklist:** read and do, touch each item, confirm the result — every time.
- **Improper positioning of the airplane:** firm, debris-free surface, into the wind, clear of other aircraft and the prop blast.
- **Accepting marginal engine performance:** a bad mag check, abnormal temps/pressures, or a failed idle check grounds the airplane.
- **An improper flight-control check:** exercise the full range of movement, not a partial one.
- **Not configuring the aircraft for takeoff:** set the flaps, trim, and the required configuration.
- **Not reviewing the takeoff and emergency procedures:** brief them out loud before the roll.
- **Runway incursion / traffic conflict:** verify the clearance, the clear final, and the correct runway before taking the runway.

## Skills — Teaching the Task

The ACS skill elements require the applicant to **review performance, complete the checklist, position the airplane, divide attention, and verify the parameters and configuration**:

- **AI.V.F.S1 — Review takeoff performance:** the learner reviews the takeoff performance and briefs the departure, V-speeds, abort point, and emergencies.
- **AI.V.F.S2 — Complete the checklist:** the learner completes the before-takeoff checklist.
