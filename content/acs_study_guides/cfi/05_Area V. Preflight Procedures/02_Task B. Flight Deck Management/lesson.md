# Task B. Flight Deck Management

**Area V — Preflight Procedures**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands flight deck management, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate the required skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

The FAA requires pilots to brief all passengers on certain items before every flight — but a safe flight demands far more than the legal minimum. Organizing the flight deck, securing the cabin, briefing the passengers, and managing the workload through single-pilot resource management all reduce risk and free the pilot to fly. A flight instructor must model these habits and teach them.

This is a ground and aircraft Task. The ACS skill elements require the applicant to **secure the aircraft, conduct a passenger briefing, manage the automation, and apply ADM/SRM**.

## Schedule / Equipment

- Ground: 5 minutes in the airplane
- Initial: 5 minutes before flight — introduction and demonstration
- Every flight: 5 minutes before flight — learner performs
- Airplane checklist
- Tablet / EFB software (optional)

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** explain the purpose of flight deck management, passenger briefings, and SRM, then prepare the cockpit and passengers for flight — give a passenger briefing, organize the needed items within reach before engine start, and use all available resources and automation to reduce workload.

**Instructor:** deliver the ground lesson, demonstrate the procedures before flight, and debrief after each flight.

## Completion Standards

- **Ground:** the learner can explain the purpose of flight deck management, passenger briefings, and SRM.
- **Flight:** the learner can prepare the cockpit and passengers for flight by giving a passenger briefing (safety belts, doors, windows, and emergency procedures), organizing the needed items within easy reach before engine start, and exhibiting SRM by using all available resources and automation — including checklists — to reduce workload.

## ACS Elements

### Knowledge

- **AI.V.B.K1** — Passenger briefing requirements, including operation and required use of safety restraint systems.
- **AI.V.B.K2** — Use of appropriate checklists.
- **AI.V.B.K3** — Requirements for current and appropriate navigation data.
- **AI.V.B.K4** — Securing items and cargo.

### Risk Management

- **AI.V.B.R1** — Use of systems or equipment, including automation and portable electronic devices.
- **AI.V.B.R2** — Inoperative equipment.
- **AI.V.B.R3** — Passenger distractions.

### Skills

- **AI.V.B.S1** — Secure all items in the aircraft.
- **AI.V.B.S2** — Conduct an appropriate passenger briefing, including identifying the pilot-in-command (PIC), use of safety belts, shoulder harnesses, doors, passenger conduct, sterile aircraft, propeller blade avoidance, and emergency procedures.
- **AI.V.B.S3** — Properly program and manage the aircraft's automation, as applicable.
- **AI.V.B.S4** — Appropriately manage risks by utilizing ADM, including SRM/CRM.

## Ground Lesson Outline

1. Arranging and securing the cockpit and cabin.
2. Safety belts and shoulder harnesses — the requirements.
3. The passenger safety briefing.
4. Single-pilot resource management (SRM) — checklists, navigation data, resources, and automation.

# Teach

Teach flight deck management as the work done on the ground that makes the flight safer in the air: a secured cabin, briefed passengers, and a managed workload free the pilot to fly. The Overview maps the ACS knowledge, risk, and skill elements; the sections below teach the material once, in the order an instructor would prepare a flight.

## AI.V.B.K1 — Passenger Briefing Requirements

The regulations drive the restraint requirements. **§ 91.107** — the PIC must ensure each person is **briefed on how to fasten and unfasten** the safety belt and shoulder harness before takeoff, and **notified to fasten** them before movement on the surface, takeoff, and landing; each person must occupy an approved seat with the belt (and harness if installed) properly secured during surface movement, takeoff, and landing. **§ 91.105** — **flight crewmembers** at their stations must use the safety belt, and the shoulder harness during takeoff and landing.

The **passenger safety briefing** covers, at minimum:

- **Who is the pilot in command** — and, when flying with another pilot, who is PIC.
- **Safety belts and shoulder harnesses** — how to fasten, adjust, and unfasten them.
- **Doors and windows** — how to operate them, and how to exit in an emergency.
- **Passenger conduct** — acceptable behavior, the **sterile cockpit** during critical phases (taxi, takeoff, landing), and **staying clear of the flight controls and rudder pedals**.
- **Propeller blade avoidance** — the danger around the airplane, especially with the engine running.
- **Portable electronic devices** — what is permitted.
- **Emergency procedures** — what to do and how to egress.

[[highlight:
Flight Guide Instructor Principle |
The passenger briefing is a legal requirement and a safety tool, not a courtesy. A passenger who knows how to open the door, when to be quiet, and to stay clear of the controls and the propeller is a managed risk instead of an unknown one.
]]

## AI.V.B.K2 — Use of Appropriate Checklists

**Use the appropriate checklists — read and do, every time.** The checklist is the defense against the missed item and the foundation of consistent, safe operation. A checklist read from memory is a checklist that misses items.

## AI.V.B.K3 — Requirements for Current and Appropriate Navigation Data

**Use current and appropriate navigation data.** Download the EFB/database updates before flight and confirm the charts and databases are current. Flying on an expired database is a planning and airspace risk — the moving map is only as good as its currency.

## AI.V.B.K4 — Securing Items and Cargo

Organize the flight deck so that **everything the pilot will need is within easy reach before engine start** — charts, the EFB, the checklist, a pen, and a flashlight. **Secure all items**: nothing loose on the glareshield, the floor, or the seats, where it can slide into the controls or become a projectile. **Secure the cargo** and confirm the **controls move freely** and fully. Adjust the **seats, rudder pedals, safety belts, and shoulder harnesses** for the occupants before flight.

## Risk Management

### AI.V.B.R1 — Use of Systems and Equipment, Automation, and Portable Devices

Automation — the autopilot and the flight/navigation systems — reduces workload but **removes the pilot from the loop**, reducing situational awareness and inviting complacency. **Program and manage the automation deliberately**: know its capabilities and limits, monitor it continuously, and stay ahead of it. Manage **portable electronic devices** so they support rather than distract.

### AI.V.B.R2 — Inoperative Equipment

Handle inoperative equipment per the airworthiness process (Task B, Area III — § 91.213); do not depart with a required item inoperative.

### AI.V.B.R3 — Passenger Distractions

The passenger briefing and the sterile cockpit manage distractions; the PIC sets the expectation before engine start.

### Common Errors and Corrections

- **Not securing essential items for easy access:** arrange and secure everything needed before engine start.
- **Not adjusting the belts, harnesses, pedals, and seats:** adjust the equipment and controls for the occupants.
- **Skipping the occupant briefing:** brief the emergency procedures and the use of the safety belts — it is required.
- **Not using all available resources:** use ATC, weather briefers, checklists, and even passengers to reduce workload.
- **Weak SRM/ADM:** apply the structured decision-making and resource management, not improvisation.

## Skills — Teaching the Task

The ACS skill elements require the applicant to **secure, brief, manage, and apply ADM**:

- **AI.V.B.S1 — Secure all items:** the learner secures the cabin and cargo and confirms the controls move freely.
- **AI.V.B.S2 — Conduct the passenger briefing:** the learner delivers a complete briefing — PIC, belts and harnesses, doors, conduct, sterile cockpit, propeller avoidance, devices, and emergency procedures.
- **AI.V.B.S3 — Manage the automation:** the learner programs and manages the aircraft's automation appropriately.
- **AI.V.B.S4 — Apply ADM/SRM:** the learner uses the available resources and a structured decision process to manage the flight's risks. **Single-pilot resource management (SRM)** integrates situational awareness, human resource management, task management, and aeronautical decision-making — the effective pilot uses **all available resources** (weather briefers, line and maintenance personnel, ATC, crewmembers, and even passengers) and communicates with inquiry, advocacy, and assertion.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

1. What does § 91.107 require for safety belts and passenger briefing?

[[answer:
Before takeoff, the PIC must ensure each person is briefed on how to fasten and unfasten the safety belt and shoulder harness (if installed). Before surface movement, takeoff, and landing, the PIC must ensure each person is notified to fasten them, and each person must occupy an approved seat with the belt (and harness) properly secured. § 91.105 adds that flight crewmembers at their stations use the belt, and the shoulder harness during takeoff and landing.
]]

2. What does a complete passenger safety briefing cover?

[[answer:
Who is the PIC; how to fasten, adjust, and unfasten the safety belts and shoulder harnesses; how to operate the doors and windows and exit in an emergency; acceptable passenger conduct and the sterile cockpit during critical phases; staying clear of the flight controls and rudder pedals; propeller blade avoidance; portable electronic devices; and what to do in an emergency.
]]

3. What is single-pilot resource management (SRM)?

[[answer:
SRM is the single-pilot application of crew resource management — integrating situational awareness, human resource management, task management, and aeronautical decision-making. The effective pilot uses all available resources (weather briefers, line and maintenance personnel, ATC, crewmembers, and passengers) and communicates with inquiry, advocacy, and assertion.
]]

4. What is the risk in cockpit automation, and how do you manage it?

[[answer:
Automation reduces workload but removes the pilot from the loop, reducing situational awareness and inviting complacency. Manage it by programming and managing the automation deliberately, knowing its capabilities and limits, monitoring it continuously, and staying ahead of it — and by managing portable electronic devices so they support rather than distract.
]]

5. What are the requirements for checklists and navigation data?

[[answer:
Use the appropriate checklists, read and do, every time — the checklist is the defense against the missed item. Use current and appropriate navigation data — download the EFB/database updates and confirm the charts and databases are current before flight.
]]

6. How do you prepare the flight deck and cabin for flight?

[[answer:
Arrange everything needed within easy reach before engine start; secure all items (nothing loose on the glareshield, floor, or seats) and the cargo; confirm the controls move freely and fully; and adjust the seats, rudder pedals, safety belts, and shoulder harnesses for the occupants.
]]

## Completion Standard

The learner explains the purpose of flight deck management, the passenger-briefing requirements, and SRM; secures the cabin and cargo; delivers a complete passenger briefing; uses the appropriate checklists and current navigation data; programs and manages the automation; and applies ADM/SRM to manage the flight's risks — preparing the cockpit and passengers for a safe flight.
