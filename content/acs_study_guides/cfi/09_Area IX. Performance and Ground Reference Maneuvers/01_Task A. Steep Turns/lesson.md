# Task A. Steep Turns

**Area IX — Performance and Ground Reference Maneuvers**

## Objective

Determine that the instructor applicant understands steep turns, applies that knowledge, manages the associated risks, demonstrates the maneuver to commercial-pilot standards while providing effective instruction, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

## Purpose

A steep turn develops precise, coordinated control during a high-load-factor level turn. It makes the relationships among **bank angle, vertical lift, angle of attack, load factor, stall speed, power, turn rate, and turn radius** immediately visible and teaches the instructor to manage aircraft control, orientation, traffic scan, and explanation at the same time.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested Schedule:** **20–30 minutes ground**, **20–30 minutes initial flight practice**, and **10 minutes debrief**.

**Equipment:** C172S POH and checklist, CFI ACS, Airplane Flying Handbook, model airplane or whiteboard, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Review the assigned sources; explain the aerodynamics, risks, procedure, and standards; perform coordinated steep turns in both directions; teach while flying; recognize and correct deviations.

**Instructor:** Confirm aircraft limitations and clearing procedures; demonstrate from outside references; introduce one error at a time when safe; coach measured corrections; debrief against the ACS.

## Completion Standards

The instructor applicant explains every ACS element, performs coordinated **360° turns in both directions at approximately 50° bank**, maintains **altitude ±100 feet, airspeed ±10 knots, bank ±5°**, and rolls out on the entry heading **±10°** while providing effective instruction and maintaining collision avoidance.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedure

A steep turn is a level turn using a bank angle of approximately **45° or greater**. The CFI Task uses approximately **50° bank** and requires a complete 360° turn in each direction. The maneuver develops precise control, coordination, orientation, division of attention, and understanding of high-load-factor aerodynamics.

<figure class="lesson-reference-figure">
		<img src="/knowledge/Steep_Turns.png" alt="Steep-turn flight path and outside visual references" loading="lazy">
		<figcaption>Steep turns require a stable bank, a repeatable horizon reference, and an outside-dominant scan.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Procedure overview:** clear; configure; establish the POH-recommended entry speed; select heading and outside references; roll smoothly to approximately **50°**; add back pressure and power as bank passes **30°**; maintain attitude, bank, coordination, and scan; begin rollout before the target heading; remove added back pressure and power through **30°**; establish straight-and-level flight; repeat opposite direction.

## K2 — Pilot Sensations and Control Forces

The pilot feels **increased apparent weight** as load factor rises. The airplane requires increasing **elevator back pressure** to preserve vertical lift and may require additional power to offset induced drag. Control pressures change during entry and rollout, so abrupt inputs produce altitude and airspeed deviations.

Instructor Emphasis |
Teach the learner to notice pressure and sight picture without becoming sensation-led. Outside attitude is primary; instruments verify performance.

## K3 — Aerodynamics Associated with Steep Turns

Banking tilts the total lift vector. To keep altitude, the wing must produce enough additional total lift for its vertical component to continue balancing weight. That requires higher angle of attack, producing greater load factor and induced drag.

<figure class="lesson-reference-figure">
		<img src="/knowledge/Load_Factor_Angle_Bank.png" alt="Relationship between bank angle and load factor in a level turn" loading="lazy">
		<figcaption>In a level turn, load factor increases rapidly as bank angle becomes steep.</figcaption>
</figure>

### K3a — Maintaining Coordinated Flight

Use **coordinated aileron and rudder** during roll-in and rollout, then maintain the ball centered while holding bank. Rudder is not used to tighten the turn. A skid increases stall/spin risk; a slip wastes performance and distorts the sight picture.

### K3b — Overbanking Tendencies

The outside wing travels a larger arc and moves faster than the inside wing, tending to produce more lift and increase bank. At steep bank angles the pilot may need **slight opposite aileron pressure** to hold the selected bank.

<figure class="lesson-reference-figure">
		<img src="/knowledge/Overbanking_Tendency.png" alt="Outside wing traveling farther and faster during a turn" loading="lazy">
		<figcaption>Overbanking tendency increases as turn rate and bank increase.</figcaption>
</figure>

### K3c — Maneuvering Speed and Weight

Establish the **manufacturer's recommended speed** and never enter above the applicable $V_A$. The C172S POH lists **95 KIAS** as the normal-category recommended steep-turn entry speed. $V_A$ decreases as aircraft weight decreases; do not assume the maximum-gross-weight value applies at a lighter weight.

### K3d — Load Factor and Accelerated Stalls

At approximately **50° bank**, maintaining altitude requires substantially more than 1 G. Stall speed rises with the square root of load factor, so a stall can occur at an airspeed well above the published 1-G stall speed. If stall warning or buffet occurs, **reduce angle of attack, level the wings, and recover**.

		<img src="/knowledge/Vg_Diagram.png" alt="V-g diagram showing increased stall speed with load factor" loading="lazy">
		<figcaption>The accelerated-stall boundary moves to a higher airspeed as load factor increases.</figcaption>
</figure>

### K3e — Rate and Radius of Turn

For a given airspeed, increasing bank increases turn rate and decreases turn radius. For a given bank, increasing airspeed decreases turn rate and increases radius. Accurate airspeed and bank control therefore stabilize both the maneuver geometry and the rollout lead.

## K4 — Common Errors

### Clearing and Scan Errors

**Error:** Not clearing the area or failing to scan during the turn.  
**Correction:** Complete clearing turns, announce the scan plan, and keep an outside-dominant scan throughout both turns.

### Pitch, Bank, Power, Trim, and Airspeed Errors

**Error:** Inadequate pitch control, altitude gain/loss, varying bank, ineffective trim or power, or poor airspeed control.  
**Correction:** Hold the bank with aileron, use proportional elevator pressure for altitude, adjust power for airspeed, and trim only enough to reduce sustained pressure without masking control feel.

### Coordination and Orientation Errors

**Error:** Poor coordination, disorientation, instrument fixation, premature recovery, or missed rollout heading.  
**Correction:** Reestablish outside references, center the ball, identify the target heading before entry, and begin a smooth rollout using an appropriate lead.

## Risk Management

### Division of Attention and Collision Hazards

**Hazard:** Fixation on altitude, bank, or heading reduces traffic scan and orientation.  
**Recognition:** Long instrument dwell, lost reference heading, or late traffic calls.  
**Consequence:** Midair collision, disorientation, or maneuver deviation.  
**Mitigation:** Clear first; use short instrument verification; keep the scan **outside → attitude → traffic → quick instrument check → outside**.

### Low Altitude, Stall/Spin, and CFIT

**Hazard:** High load factor, excessive bank, low airspeed, or uncoordinated flight reduces stall margin.  
**Recognition:** Decaying airspeed, increasing back pressure, stall warning, buffet, ball displaced, or terrain rising in the windshield.  
**Consequence:** Accelerated stall, spin, or CFIT.  
**Mitigation:** Use a safe training altitude, remain within POH/AFM limitations, stay coordinated, and recover immediately from a stall indication or unsafe trend.

### Distraction and Disorientation

**Hazard:** Teaching while maneuvering can displace aircraft control and traffic avoidance.  
**Recognition:** Explanation continues while a deviation grows.  
**Consequence:** Loss of control or situational awareness.  
**Mitigation:** **Aviate first.** Pause instruction, correct the airplane, restore orientation, then resume concise teaching.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** During a right steep turn, the learner fixates on the altimeter. Bank increases, airspeed decays, altitude begins to fall, and the ball moves left.

**Discuss / Decide:** Which deviation is causal, what must be corrected first, and when should the maneuver be terminated?

**Desired Learner Outcome:** Reduce angle of attack if required, correct bank and coordination, restore outside references and airspeed, and discontinue the maneuver if control or traffic awareness is compromised.

# Fly

## Setup

- **Altitude:** Select an altitude that provides safe recovery margins and complies with local procedures and airspace.
- **Clearing:** Perform clearing turns; scan above, below, and through the maneuver area.
- **Configuration:** **Clean**, mixture as required, checklist complete.
- **Heading:** Select a cardinal heading or prominent visual line and note rollout references.
- **Power / speed:** Approximately **2300 RPM** as conditions require; establish **95 KIAS** for the C172S.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Stabilize straight-and-level at **95 KIAS** and announce the target heading.
2. Roll smoothly into approximately **50° bank** with coordinated aileron and rudder.
3. Passing **30° bank**, increase elevator back pressure to hold altitude and add power as needed to hold airspeed.
4. Hold a constant outside attitude and bank; use brief instrument checks; continue traffic scan.
5. Lead rollout by approximately **10°** or as experience and turn rate require.
6. Passing **30° bank** in rollout, reduce back pressure and remove the added power.
7. Establish straight-and-level flight on heading, then repeat in the opposite direction.

## Learner Practice

Practice in building blocks: stabilized entry; 90° segments; complete 360° turn; opposite direction; then simultaneous explanation. Introduce only one simulated error at a time and never allow teaching to compromise aircraft control or collision avoidance.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Not clearing the area** — Stop and clear before entry; maintain the scan throughout.
2. **Inadequate pitch control on entry or rollout** — Coordinate elevator pressure with bank change.
3. **Gaining or losing altitude** — Reestablish the outside pitch reference; use measured elevator correction.
4. **Failure to maintain constant bank angle** — Counter overbanking and hold the selected wing-horizon relationship.
5. **Poor flight control coordination** — Center the ball with coordinated rudder; never tighten with rudder.
6. **Ineffective use of trim** — Trim only after attitude and pressure are established.
7. **Ineffective use of power** — Add power for induced drag; remove it during rollout.
8. **Inadequate airspeed control** — Coordinate pitch and power; recover from an unsafe decay.
9. **Becoming disoriented** — Return to outside references and terminate if orientation is not promptly restored.
10. **Performing by reference to the flight instruments rather than visual references** — Make outside attitude primary and instruments confirmatory.
11. **Failure to scan for other traffic during the maneuver** — Use a deliberate outside traffic scan through the turn.
12. **Attempting to start recovery prematurely** — Use the target heading and a consistent rollout lead.
13. **Failure to stop the turn on the designated heading** — Anticipate rollout and match roll rate to lead.

## Completion Standards

The applicant clears, establishes **95 KIAS** in the C172S or the applicable manufacturer's speed, performs coordinated **360° turns at approximately 50° bank in both directions**, maintains **altitude ±100 feet, airspeed ±10 knots, bank ±5°**, rolls out **±10°**, teaches effectively, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. Why must back pressure and usually power increase in a steep level turn?

[[answer:
Bank tilts the lift vector. Additional angle of attack and total lift are required to preserve the vertical component, increasing load factor and induced drag; elevator supplies the angle of attack and power offsets the added drag.
]]

### 2. What speed applies to a C172S steep-turn entry?

[[answer:
The POH recommends **95 KIAS**. Never exceed the applicable maneuvering speed, and remember that $V_A$ decreases with weight.
]]

### 3. What are the CFI ACS performance tolerances?

[[answer:
Approximately **50° bank**; altitude **±100 feet**; airspeed **±10 knots**; bank **±5°**; rollout heading **±10°**; then repeat opposite direction.
]]

### 4. Why does stall speed increase?

[[answer:
Maintaining altitude at steep bank requires increased load factor. The wing reaches critical angle of attack at a higher indicated airspeed under that load.
]]

### 5. How should the instructor correct instrument fixation?

[[answer:
Restore an outside-dominant scan, use a repeatable wing-horizon and nose-horizon sight picture, and limit instruments to brief performance checks.
]]

### 6. What must happen if stall warning occurs?

[[answer:
Reduce angle of attack, level the wings, apply power as appropriate, and recover. Do not continue a deteriorating demonstration merely to complete the turn.
]]
