# Task C. Chandelles

**Area IX — Performance and Ground Reference Maneuvers**

## Objective

Determine that the instructor applicant understands chandelles, manages associated risks, demonstrates the maneuver to commercial-pilot standards while providing effective instruction, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

## Purpose

A chandelle is a **maximum-performance 180° climbing turn** ending wings level, nose high, and just above stall speed. It develops smooth control, coordination, energy management, and understanding of changing control effectiveness as airspeed decreases.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **20–30 minutes ground**, **20–30 minutes flight**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** C172S POH/checklist, ACS, AFH, model/whiteboard, maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain and demonstrate both phases, risks, standards, and corrections while maintaining visual scan.  
**Instructor:** Verify limitations and altitude, demonstrate both directions, coach smooth inputs, and evaluate instruction as well as control.

## Completion Standards

The applicant establishes approximately **30° bank**, completes a smooth constant-bank/increasing-pitch first 90° and constant-pitch/decreasing-bank second 90°, rolls out **±10°** just above stall speed without stalling, and resumes level flight with minimum altitude loss.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures

Clear, enter below applicable $V_A$, establish approximately **30° bank**, apply maximum allowable power and increase pitch through the first 90°. At 90°, hold pitch constant and begin a smooth rollout. Reach wings level at 180°, just above stall speed, then accelerate without losing altitude.

[[mnemonic:
Bank–Pitch, Then Pitch–Bank |
**First 90°:** bank stays, pitch changes. **Second 90°:** pitch stays, bank changes.
]]

## K2 — Aerodynamics

As pitch increases and airspeed decreases, left-turning tendencies and rudder demand increase. Maintain coordination continuously. Counter **overbanking** to hold 30° in the first half. Enter no faster than the applicable $V_A$, remembering $V_A$ decreases with weight. Turning load factor raises stall speed; recover immediately from stall warning or buffet.

## K3 — Maximum-Performance Configuration

Use the POH/AFM configuration: **clean**, propeller as applicable, mixture as required, and maximum allowable power. For the C172S training profile, stabilize at **105 KIAS** and approximately **2,300 RPM**, then apply full throttle smoothly as the maneuver begins.

## K4 — Pitch Control with Decreasing Airspeed

Increase pitch smoothly to approximately **15° nose-up by the 90° point**; do not snap to the attitude. At 90°, **hold 15° nose-up** while bank decreases at a constant rate. Increasing pitch during rollout causes an early stall; lowering pitch finishes too fast and sacrifices performance.

## K5 — Common Errors

Teach each error by contrasting the correct two-phase picture with the observed deviation. Correct **bank first-half errors**, **pitch second-half errors**, coordination, timing, and scan before attempting another complete maneuver.

## Risk Management

### Stall/Spin and Energy
**Hazard:** High power, decreasing speed, bank, and increasing angle of attack.  
**Recognition:** Stall warning, buffet, yaw, excessive pitch, or weak control response.  
**Consequence:** Stall/spin or loss of control.  
**Mitigation:** Remain coordinated; recover at any stall indication; never force completion.

### Altitude, Airspace, and Collision
**Hazard:** Inadequate vertical room or fixation during the climbing turn.  
**Recognition:** Overlying airspace/terrain conflict or scan interruption.  
**Consequence:** Airspace conflict, collision, or CFIT.  
**Mitigation:** Begin high enough to finish above **1,500 AGL**, allow climb room, clear, and keep an outside scan.

### Confined-Area Misapplication
**Hazard:** Treating a training chandelle as a guaranteed escape maneuver.  
**Recognition:** Terrain clearance depends on an unverified radius or climb.  
**Consequence:** Terrain impact.  
**Mitigation:** Use performance planning and conservative escape routes; do not improvise near terrain.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** At 100° of turn the learner continues raising pitch while rolling out and the stall warning activates.  
**Desired outcome:** Reduce angle of attack, maintain coordination, level the wings, recover, then explain that pitch should have remained constant after 90°.

# Fly

## Setup

- **Clear:** Two 90° clearing turns; choose a 180° visual reference.
- **Altitude:** Sufficient to complete no lower than **1,500 feet AGL** with airspace above.
- **Configuration:** Clean; mixture rich as applicable.
- **Entry:** **105 KIAS**, approximately **2,300 RPM** in the C172S training profile.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Establish and hold approximately **30° bank**.
2. Smoothly apply full throttle and increase pitch.
3. **First 90°:** constant 30° bank; pitch continuously increases to approximately **15° nose-up**.
4. **At 90°:** hold **15° nose-up**; begin coordinated constant-rate rollout.
5. **Second 90°:** pitch constant; bank continuously decreases.
6. **At 180°:** wings level **±10°**, just above stall speed; momentarily demonstrate control.
7. Lower pitch smoothly and accelerate while minimizing altitude loss; cruise checklist.

## Learner Practice

Practice the first 90°, the second 90°, then complete maneuvers in both directions. Add simultaneous instruction only after the sight picture and coordination are stable.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Not clearing the area** — Stop, clear, and restart.
2. **Initial bank too shallow resulting in a stall** — Establish approximately 30°; do not substitute pitch for turning.
3. **Initial bank too steep resulting in failure to gain maximum performance** — Reduce to approximately 30°.
4. **Allowing bank to increase after initial establishment** — Counter overbanking and hold the wing-horizon picture.
5. **Not starting recovery at 90°** — Use the selected 90° reference as the rollout trigger.
6. **Allowing pitch to increase during the second 90°** — Freeze the pitch picture at 90°.
7. **Leveling wings before 180°** — Slow and time the rollout to finish on reference.
8. **Low pitch on recovery, airspeed well above stall** — Increase pitch progressively in the first half and hold it in the second.
9. **Control pressures not smooth** — Use continuous, progressive inputs.
10. **Poor coordination** — Increase rudder as speed decays; center the ball.
11. **Stalling at any point** — Reduce angle of attack and recover immediately.
12. **Executing a steep turn instead of a climbing maneuver** — Add pitch and power; prioritize altitude gain.
13. **Not scanning for traffic** — Maintain outside traffic scan through both phases.
14. **Performing by instruments rather than visual references** — Use horizon and selected references; instruments verify.

## Completion Standards

Perform both phases smoothly and coordinated; establish approximately **30° bank**; finish at **180° ±10°** just above stall speed without stalling; recover with minimum altitude loss; teach and correct effectively.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What defines each phase?
[[answer: First 90°: constant bank and increasing pitch. Second 90°: constant pitch and decreasing bank.]]

### 2. What is the C172S recommended entry speed?
[[answer: 105 KIAS.]]

### 3. What happens at 180°?
[[answer: Wings level within ±10°, just above stall speed, momentarily controlled without stalling.]]

### 4. Why does rudder demand change?
[[answer: High power, increasing angle of attack, and decreasing airspeed strengthen left-turning tendencies and reduce control effectiveness.]]

### 5. What is the recovery from stall warning?
[[answer: Reduce angle of attack, maintain coordination, level the wings, and recover; do not force completion.]]

### 6. Why is a chandelle not a guaranteed terrain-escape maneuver?
[[answer: Actual climb and radius vary with airplane, weight, density altitude, wind, and technique; terrain clearance requires planning.]]
