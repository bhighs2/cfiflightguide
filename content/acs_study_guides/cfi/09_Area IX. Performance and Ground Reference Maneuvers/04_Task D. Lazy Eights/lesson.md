# Task D. Lazy Eights

**Area IX — Performance and Ground Reference Maneuvers**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands lazy eights, manages risk, demonstrates continuously changing pitch, bank, and airspeed while providing effective instruction, and corrects common errors.

## Purpose

Lazy eights develop **smoothness, coordination, visual planning, energy management, and timing** through two symmetrical 180° climbing and descending turns. No control position remains fixed; the airplane flows continuously through the maneuver.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **25–35 minutes ground**, **25–35 minutes flight**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** C172S POH/checklist, ACS, AFH, `Lazy_Eight.png`, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Identify 45°/90°/135° references, explain energy changes, demonstrate symmetrically while teaching, and correct errors.  
**Instructor:** Demonstrate an outside-reference rhythm, guard against abrupt control and stalls, and evaluate each 180° point.

## Completion Standards

Maintain coordination and continuous pitch/roll/airspeed change; reach approximately **30° bank at the steepest point**; at each 180°, return to entry altitude **±100 feet**, airspeed **±10 knots**, and heading **±10°**; complete the specified symmetrical loops and teach effectively.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures

Clear, stabilize, select distant **45°/90°/135°** points, and begin simultaneous gentle pitch and bank. Flow through one 180° arc, cross the reference line at entry altitude/airspeed/heading, and immediately repeat opposite. The maneuver must look **lazy**, not segmented.

## K2 — Aerodynamics and Coordination

Climbing trades airspeed for altitude; descending trades altitude for airspeed. Turn rate and overbanking change as speed changes. Use coordinated aileron/rudder and changing elevator pressure. High power and angle of attack near the top increase left-turning tendencies, especially in the right-turning half.

## K3 — Performance and Airspeed Limitations

Enter at the manufacturer’s recommended speed and never above applicable $V_A$. The C172S POH recommends **105 KIAS**. Protect the slow top from a stall and the descending segment from acceleration beyond limitations.

## K4 — Phases from Entry to Recovery

- **45°:** maximum pitch-up, approximately **15–20° nose-up**, with approximately **15° bank**.
- **90°:** highest altitude and minimum airspeed; pitch passes level; approximately **30° maximum bank**.
- **135°:** maximum pitch-down, approximately **15–17° nose-down**, with bank reduced to approximately **15°**.
- **180°:** wings level at entry heading, altitude, and airspeed; continue opposite.

All values are reached by **continuous change**, never by stopping at checkpoints.

## K5 — Common Errors

The central diagnostic is symmetry. A poor 180° endpoint usually began with bad references, rushed pitch, excessive bank, or uncoordinated energy management earlier in the arc. Correct the **cause**, not merely the endpoint.

## Risk Management

### Accelerated Stall / Uncoordinated Flight
**Hazard:** Slow, banked, high-AOA flight near 90°. **Recognition:** buffet, warning, yaw, control mushiness. **Consequence:** stall/spin. **Mitigation:** coordinated flight, modest bank, immediate AOA reduction at warning.

### Energy and Limitations
**Hazard:** Excessive pitch up or down. **Recognition:** near-stall too early or excessive descending speed. **Consequence:** stall, overspeed, asymmetry. **Mitigation:** slow continuous inputs and an early abort when energy does not match the checkpoint.

### Collision / Orientation
**Hazard:** Reference or instrument fixation. **Recognition:** traffic scan stops or points are lost. **Consequence:** collision or disorientation. **Mitigation:** clear, use distant references, scan outside, and pause teaching to aviate.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** At 90° the pitch drops abruptly through the horizon and speed builds early.  
**Desired outcome:** Smooth the pitch rate before 90°, avoid holding the nose at the top, and let the airplane flow through the horizon while bank reaches its maximum.

# Fly

## Setup

- **Clear:** Two 90° clearing turns.
- **Altitude:** Complete no lower than **1,500 feet AGL**.
- **Configuration:** Clean; approximately **2,300 RPM**; **105 KIAS** C172S.
- **References:** Select distant **45°**, **90°**, and **135°** points off the wing.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Begin simultaneous **slow** pitch and bank.
2. **45°:** maximum pitch-up, **15–20° nose-up**; ~15° bank.
3. Reduce pitch while increasing bank.
4. **90°:** pitch level, ~30° bank, minimum speed safely above stall.
5. Continue reducing pitch and gradually reduce bank.
6. **135°:** maximum pitch-down, **15–17° nose-down**; ~15° bank.
7. **180°:** level at entry altitude, airspeed, and heading.
8. Repeat opposite; restore cruise/checklist.

## Learner Practice

First fly one 180° arc, then paired loops. Have the learner verbalize only **trend cues**: “pitch increasing/bank increasing,” then “pitch decreasing/bank increasing,” etc., to preserve smooth control.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Not clearing the area** — Clear and maintain traffic scan.
2. **Not symmetrical across each 180°** — Compare endpoints; slow the rates.
3. **Improper 45°/90°/135° references** — Select distant, distinct points before entry.
4. **Ineffective planning** — Brief direction, altitude, airspace, and abort cues.
5. **Altitude gain/loss at 180°** — Correct energy pacing earlier, not at the line.
6. **Pitch rapidly falling through horizon at top** — Reduce pitch rate smoothly; do not hold then release.
7. **Airspeed or bank standards not met** — Pace energy and cap bank near 30°.
8. **Control roughness** — Use continuous fingertip pressure.
9. **Poor coordination** — Match rudder to changing bank, power, and speed.
10. **Stalling at any point** — Reduce AOA and recover immediately.
11. **Executing a steep turn instead of a climbing maneuver** — Begin with more pitch and less bank.
12. **Not scanning for traffic** — Restore outside scan between references.
13. **Flying by instruments rather than visual references** — Horizon/ground points primary; instruments verify.

## Completion Standards

Maintain coordination and continuous change; approximately **30° maximum bank**; return at each 180° to altitude **±100 feet**, airspeed **±10 knots**, heading **±10°**; complete symmetrical loops and teach effectively.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What changes continuously?
[[answer: Pitch, bank, control pressure, turn rate, altitude, and airspeed; no checkpoint is a pause.]]

### 2. What occurs at 45°, 90°, 135°, and 180°?
[[answer: 45° maximum pitch-up at approximately 15–20° nose-up/~15° bank; 90° level pitch/~30° bank/minimum speed; 135° maximum pitch-down at approximately 15–17° nose-down/~15° bank; 180° wings level at entry conditions.]]

### 3. What is the C172S entry speed?
[[answer: 105 KIAS.]]

### 4. What are the 180° tolerances?
[[answer: Altitude ±100 feet, airspeed ±10 knots, heading ±10°.]]

### 5. Why does the top often become rough?
[[answer: The pilot holds pitch, then releases it, instead of continuously reducing pitch as bank increases toward 90°.]]

### 6. What is the immediate response to stall warning?
[[answer: Reduce angle of attack, maintain coordination, and recover; do not continue the maneuver.]]
