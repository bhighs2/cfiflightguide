# Task F. Eights on Pylons

**Area IX — Performance and Ground Reference Maneuvers**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands eights on pylons, manages associated risks, demonstrates the maneuver while providing effective instruction, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

## Purpose

Eights on pylons are the most advanced ground-reference maneuver. They develop coordinated control while the pilot varies altitude to remain at the **pivotal altitude** for changing groundspeed, keeping a pylon stationary on a lateral wing reference.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **30–40 minutes ground**, **30–45 minutes flight**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** ACS, AFH, POH/checklist, wind information, calculator/pivotal-altitude card, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Calculate pivotal altitude, select pylons and entry, explain sight-line corrections, demonstrate while teaching, and correct errors.  
**Instructor:** Verify area, altitude, and pylon suitability; demonstrate pitch-only pylon correction and coordination; guard low-altitude risks.

## Completion Standards

Determine approximate pivotal altitude, select suitable pylons permitting straight-and-level transition, enter correctly, maintain the line-of-sight reference with smooth altitude changes, remain coordinated, use bank appropriate to distance and conditions **not exceeding 40°**, and teach/correct effectively.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures

Select two pylons on a line approximately **perpendicular to the wind**. Enter the maneuver on a **45° midpoint downwind** at approximate pivotal altitude, turn around the first while maintaining the pylon on a wing reference, roll out diagonally between pylons, transition wings level for approximately **3–5 seconds**, then turn oppositely around the second and exit on the entry heading.

## K2 — Aerodynamics and Coordination

Bank determines the turn geometry for the chosen pylon distance, while pitch changes altitude and groundspeed. Use coordinated aileron/rudder for every bank change. **Never move the pylon with rudder**; that creates a slip/skid and does not correct pivotal altitude.

## K3 — Pivotal Altitude

Pivotal altitude, $h_p$ in feet AGL, is the altitude at which the selected pylon appears stationary along a line parallel to the airplane’s lateral axis during the turn. It varies with **groundspeed**, $GS$ in knots, squared, not bank angle:

$$
h_p = \frac{GS^2}{11.3}
$$

| Groundspeed | Approx. pivotal altitude |
|---:|---:|
| **70 kt** | **434 ft AGL** |
| **80 kt** | **566 ft AGL** |
| **90 kt** | **717 ft AGL** |
| **100 kt** | **885 ft AGL** |

Use the calculated value as an estimate. Actual groundspeed changes around each pylon, so altitude must change continuously.

[[mnemonic:
Ahead—Descend; Behind—Climb |
If the pylon moves **ahead toward the nose**, lower the nose and descend. If it moves **behind toward the tail**, raise the nose and climb. Use pitch, not rudder.
]]

## K4 — Effect of Wind on Ground Track

Enter on a **45° line through the midpoint, downwind**, near the highest groundspeed and pivotal altitude. Groundspeed falls while turning upwind, requiring a descent to the lower pivotal altitude; it rises returning downwind, requiring a climb. Apply wind correction between pylons to arrive at the second at the proper distance.

## K5 — Phases from Entry to Recovery

1. **Plan:** Wind, altitude, two pylons roughly **2/3–3/4 mile apart on a line perpendicular to the wind**, emergency field, and wing reference.
2. **Enter:** Enter the maneuver on a **45° midpoint downwind**, about **100 KIAS / 2,300 RPM** for the local C172 profile, near **900 feet AGL** when groundspeed is about 100 knots.
3. **First pylon:** Bank for distance, no more than **40°**; pitch to hold sight line.
4. **Transition:** Roll out diagonally, wings level **3–5 seconds**, correct wind and position.
5. **Second pylon:** Turn opposite and repeat.
6. **Exit:** Roll out on the entry heading and restore cruise.

## K6 — Common Errors

Diagnose whether the error is **altitude (pitch)**, **distance (bank/position)**, **wind/entry planning**, or **coordination**. Rudder is never the pylon-position correction.

## Risk Management

### Low Altitude / Stall-Spin / CFIT
**Hazard:** Banked flight near terrain with changing pitch and speed. **Recognition:** low speed, excessive bank, skid, rising obstacles. **Consequence:** stall/spin or CFIT. **Mitigation:** open area, safe pivotal-altitude range, **≤40° bank**, coordination, and early termination.

### Collision / Fixation
**Hazard:** Staring at the pylon. **Recognition:** traffic and altitude scan disappear. **Consequence:** collision or loss of orientation. **Mitigation:** pylon glance, attitude, traffic, instruments, next pylon; repeat.

### Energy / Emergency Landing
**Hazard:** Excessive pitch correction or no suitable landing area. **Recognition:** speed decays/builds or emergency options disappear. **Consequence:** stall, overspeed, or no forced-landing margin. **Mitigation:** smooth corrections, suitable terrain, stable entry, and discontinue before margins erode.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** The pylon moves toward the nose and the learner applies inside rudder.  
**Desired outcome:** Remove rudder, restore coordination, lower the nose to descend toward the higher pivotal altitude associated with increasing groundspeed, and reassess distance/bank.

# Fly

## Setup

- Determine pivotal altitude from expected **groundspeed**.
- Clear; clean configuration; suitable power and speed below $V_A$.
- Select two prominent pylons approximately **2/3–3/4 mile apart on a line perpendicular to the wind**, allowing **3–5 seconds wings level** between them, over open terrain with an emergency option.
- Select a wing reference parallel to the lateral axis.
- Enter the maneuver on a **45° midpoint downwind**.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Enter on a **45° midpoint downwind** near the highest anticipated pivotal altitude; local reference is approximately **900 feet AGL at 100 knots groundspeed**.
2. Turn around the first pylon at the proper distance; bank **≤40°**.
3. If pylon moves **toward nose/ahead**, pitch forward and descend.
4. If pylon moves **toward tail/behind**, pitch back and climb.
5. Maintain coordination; never use rudder to hold the pylon.
6. Roll out diagonally between pylons; hold wings level 3–5 seconds and correct wind.
7. Turn oppositely around the second pylon and repeat.
8. Exit on the entry heading; cruise checklist.

## Learner Practice

Practice one pylon first, then transition geometry, then full eights with simultaneous instruction. Require early rejection of poor pylons or unsafe altitude.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Attempting to maintain the pylon with rudder** — Restore coordination; correct with pitch.
2. **Failure to clear above, below, and both sides** — Discontinue and clear.
3. **Poor pylon selection** — Choose distinct points with spacing, open terrain, and emergency options.
4. **Improper wing reference point** — Use a sight line parallel to the lateral axis.
5. **No stable level entry** — Stabilize speed, altitude, and configuration first.
6. **Inadequate altitude control** — Use smooth pitch based on pylon movement.
7. **Improper wind assessment** — Recompute entry and transition for groundspeed/drift.
8. **Poor constant-radius turns** — Correct entry distance and bank, not with rudder.
9. **Rough controls** — Anticipate sight-line movement and blend pressure.
10. **Uncoordinated aileron/rudder** — Center the ball through roll and turn.
11. **Loss of orientation** — Keep both pylons and entry/exit heading in the scan; level if lost.

## Completion Standards

Determine approximate pivotal altitude; select suitable pylons; enter correctly; maintain pylon sight line with smooth pitch changes, coordinated flight, and **bank ≤40°**; transition straight-and-level; exit on heading; teach and correct effectively.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What determines pivotal altitude?
[[answer: Groundspeed squared; pivotal altitude in feet AGL is approximately groundspeed in knots squared divided by 11.3.]]

### 2. What correction is made when the pylon moves toward the nose?
[[answer: Lower the nose and descend; do not use rudder to move the pylon.]]

### 3. What correction is made when the pylon moves toward the tail?
[[answer: Raise the nose and climb while remaining coordinated.]]

### 4. What is pivotal altitude at 100 knots groundspeed?
[[answer: Approximately 885 feet AGL, commonly rounded to about 900 feet AGL for planning.]]

### 5. What is the maximum bank?
[[answer: 40°.]]

### 6. What makes pylons suitable?
[[answer: Distinct references, appropriate spacing and distance, a straight-and-level transition, open terrain, obstacle clearance, and an emergency landing option.]]

### 7. Why does altitude change around each pylon?
[[answer: Wind changes groundspeed, and pivotal altitude varies with groundspeed squared.]]
