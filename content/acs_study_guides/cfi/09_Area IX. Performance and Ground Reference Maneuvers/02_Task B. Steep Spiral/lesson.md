# Task B. Steep Spiral

**Area IX — Performance and Ground Reference Maneuvers**

## Objective

Determine that the instructor applicant understands steep spirals, applies that knowledge, manages associated risks, demonstrates the maneuver to commercial-pilot standards while providing effective instruction, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

## Purpose

A steep spiral is a power-off gliding maneuver that dissipates altitude while keeping a selected landing area within reach. It develops **wind correction, constant-radius tracking, coordination, airspeed control, orientation, and division of attention** for emergency landing planning.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **20–30 minutes ground**, **20–30 minutes flight**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** C172S POH/checklist, CFI ACS, AFH, model airplane or whiteboard, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain purpose, wind correction, risks, procedure, standards, and errors; demonstrate while teaching.  
**Instructor:** Verify altitude and area selection; demonstrate visual references and engine clearing; simulate one error at a time; debrief to ACS standards.

## Completion Standards

The applicant completes at least **three 360° turns**, maintains a constant radius with bank never exceeding **60°**, maintains specified airspeed **±10 knots**, rolls out **±10°**, finishes no lower than **1,500 feet AGL**, teaches effectively, and corrects common errors.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures

The maneuver is a **gliding series of at least three turns** around a suitable ground point. Clear, select an emergency-capable area, establish the specified glide speed, reduce power to idle, vary bank for wind, clear the engine at least once each turn, count revolutions, and roll out on the selected reference or heading.

## K2 — Relationship to Emergency Landing Procedures

The spiral can keep a landing area continuously visible while excess altitude is dissipated. It is not a substitute for an emergency checklist or landing plan. Before descending, identify **wind, landing direction, key positions, obstacles, and minimum rollout altitude**. Preserve enough altitude to transition to a stabilized approach.

## K3 — Maintaining a Constant Radius

Hold **constant airspeed with pitch** and adjust **bank for the changing groundspeed**. Avoid pulling to tighten the circle; that raises load factor and stall speed. Distance from the point should remain visually constant.

## K4 — Wind, Ground Track, and Reference

The steep spiral has **no prescribed wind-relative entry heading**. Before entry, identify the wind, approach the reference point at the planned radius, and select the rollout heading. Groundspeed is greatest downwind and least upwind. Use the **steepest bank downwind** and **shallowest bank upwind**, continuously blending between them. Never exceed **60° bank**. If wind, altitude, or radius demands more than the limit, discontinue and reposition.

## K5 — Common Errors

- **Not clearing / not scanning:** stop, clear, and restore an outside scan.
- **Poor pitch or airspeed control:** reestablish glide attitude, then trim.
- **No wind correction:** compare radius around the point and vary bank early.
- **Poor coordination:** center the ball; never use rudder to tighten radius.
- **Ineffective trim:** stabilize before trimming; remain ready for changing pressure.
- **Disorientation / instrument fixation:** count turns from a fixed visual heading and keep the scan outside.
- **Missed rollout:** identify the rollout reference before entry and anticipate it.

## Risk Management

### Altitude, Stall/Spin, and CFIT

**Hazard:** Descending, steep-bank, low-speed flight.  
**Recognition:** Decaying speed, excessive back pressure/bank, rising terrain, or lost altitude awareness.  
**Consequence:** Accelerated stall, spin, or CFIT.  
**Mitigation:** Plan from at least **3,000 feet AGL** for this training profile, honor **60° maximum bank**, remain coordinated, announce altitude each turn, and complete no lower than **1,500 feet AGL**.

### Collision and Division of Attention

**Hazard:** Fixation on the point or instruments.  
**Recognition:** Traffic scan pauses or track errors go unnoticed.  
**Consequence:** Collision or disorientation.  
**Mitigation:** Clear first, use an outside-dominant scan, and pause instruction whenever aircraft control or traffic avoidance requires attention.

### Wind and Limitations

**Hazard:** Strong wind can demand excessive bank or move the airplane beyond gliding reach.  
**Recognition:** Radius grows despite approaching **60°**, or the landing area moves aft/out of reach.  
**Consequence:** Loss of control or loss of landing option.  
**Mitigation:** Choose another point, widen/reposition, or discontinue. Never exceed POH/AFM limitations.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** After two turns the wind increases, the downwind radius expands, and correcting it would require more than **60° bank**.  
**Discuss / Decide:** Continue, tighten with rudder, or discontinue?  
**Desired outcome:** Discontinue or reposition. Never skid or exceed the bank limit to salvage the track.

# Fly

## Setup

- **Altitude:** At least **3,000 feet AGL** for the local training profile and sufficient for three turns; completion above **1,500 feet AGL**.
- **Clear:** Two 90° clearing turns; scan above, below, and around the area.
- **Configuration:** Clean; checklist complete; mixture as required.
- **Point / entry:** Suitable emergency landing area/reference; identify wind and approach the point at the planned radius. **No specific wind-relative entry heading is required.** Note the rollout heading.
- **Power / speed:** Establish **90 KIAS**, approximately **1,800 RPM** as needed before entry; then throttle idle.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Enter abeam the point at the planned radius and heading.
2. Reduce throttle to **idle** and pitch for **90 KIAS**.
3. Establish a coordinated turn; vary bank for wind, never exceeding **60°**.
4. Hold constant radius and airspeed with an outside-dominant scan.
5. **Clear the engine once every 360°** per aircraft procedure; reestablish idle and airspeed.
6. Count at least **three complete turns** and announce altitude.
7. Roll out on the specified visual reference/heading **±10°**, above **1,500 feet AGL**.
8. Restore cruise; compare DG/HSI with the compass and adjust as appropriate.

## Learner Practice

Practice point selection, one-turn radius control, three-turn sequencing, then simultaneous instruction. The learner must prioritize **aircraft control, traffic, altitude, then explanation**.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Not clearing the area** — **Recognize:** traffic scan absent. **Correct:** discontinue, clear, and restart.
2. **Inadequate pitch control on entry or rollout** — **Recognize:** speed/attitude changes abruptly. **Correct:** set glide attitude smoothly and transition deliberately.
3. **Not correcting the bank angle to compensate for wind** — **Recognize:** radius expands/contracts. **Correct:** steepen downwind, shallow upwind.
4. **Poor flight control coordination** — **Recognize:** ball displaced or yaw. **Correct:** coordinated aileron/rudder; never tighten with rudder.
5. **Ineffective use of trim** — **Recognize:** heavy pressure or speed wander. **Correct:** stabilize, then trim.
6. **Inadequate airspeed control** — **Recognize:** more than ±10 knots. **Correct:** pitch for speed; avoid radius correction with pitch.
7. **Becoming disoriented** — **Recognize:** lost turn count/heading. **Correct:** level, orient, and restart if needed.
8. **Performing by reference to the flight instruments rather than visual references** — **Recognize:** prolonged head-down time. **Correct:** outside attitude and point primary; instruments verify.
9. **Not scanning for other traffic during the maneuver** — **Recognize:** point fixation. **Correct:** insert traffic scan into every cycle.
10. **Not completing the turn on the designated heading or reference** — **Recognize:** late rollout or lost count. **Correct:** identify and call the rollout reference each revolution.

## Completion Standards

Complete at least **three 360° turns**, hold constant radius with wind correction and **bank ≤60°**, maintain specified airspeed **±10 knots**, roll out **±10°**, finish above **1,500 feet AGL**, and teach/correct effectively.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. Why is a steep spiral useful in an emergency?
[[answer: It dissipates excess altitude while keeping a selected landing area continuously in view and within planning reach.]]

### 2. How does wind change bank?
[[answer: Bank is steepest downwind at highest groundspeed and shallowest upwind at lowest groundspeed, continuously adjusted to hold radius.]]

### 3. What are the bank and completion-altitude limits?
[[answer: Never exceed 60° bank and complete no lower than 1,500 feet AGL.]]

### 4. What controls airspeed and radius?
[[answer: Pitch controls airspeed; bank, varied for wind, controls turn rate and ground-track radius.]]

### 5. Why clear the engine?
[[answer: Extended idle operation can cool or foul the engine; follow the aircraft procedure and clear it at least once per revolution in this training profile.]]

### 6. What if the radius requires more than 60° bank?
[[answer: Discontinue or reposition. Do not skid, pull, or exceed the limit to salvage the maneuver.]]
