# Task E. Ground Reference Maneuvers

**Area IX — Performance and Ground Reference Maneuvers**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands ground reference maneuvers, manages associated risks, demonstrates the evaluator-selected maneuver while providing effective instruction, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

## Purpose

The **rectangular course, S-turns, and turns around a point** teach the pilot to convert wind awareness into a precise ground track while maintaining altitude, airspeed, coordination, traffic scan, and safe low-altitude judgment.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **35–45 minutes ground**, **45–60 minutes flight**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** ACS, AFH, checklist/POH, sectional or satellite image, diagrams, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Assess wind and area, explain all three maneuvers, demonstrate the selected maneuver while teaching, and correct errors.  
**Instructor:** Validate reference/area selection, demonstrate wind correction and scan, guard altitude and coordination, and debrief to ACS standards.

## Completion Standards

At **600–1,000 feet AGL**, select suitable references, plan the correct entry, apply wind correction to hold the required track/radius, remain coordinated, divide attention effectively, and maintain altitude **±100 feet** and airspeed **±10 knots**.

- **AI.IX.E.S7** — Maintain altitude ±100 feet; maintain airspeed ±10 knots.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures

These maneuvers combine the four fundamentals while relating the airplane to fixed ground references. Clear; assess wind; choose a safe area and emergency option; stabilize at **600–1,000 feet AGL** and a suitable speed below $V_A$; enter relative to the wind as specified below; control track; and recover without sacrificing scan.

**Wind-relative entries:**

- **Rectangular course:** Enter **45° to the downwind leg**.
- **S-turns:** Enter **directly downwind and perpendicular to the reference line**.
- **Turns around a point:** Enter **directly downwind, abeam the point at the selected radius**.

## K2 — Wind, Ground Track, and Reference

**Heading** is where the nose points; **ground track** is the path over Earth. Crab into crosswind on straight legs. During turns, vary bank continuously because wind direction relative to the airplane changes throughout the turn.

## K3 — Bank, Groundspeed, Rate, and Radius

At higher groundspeed, more turn rate is needed to preserve a given ground radius; at lower groundspeed, less is needed. Therefore, for constant-radius turns, use the **steepest bank downwind** and **shallowest bank upwind**. Never skid to make the ground track fit.

## K4 — Rectangular Course and Traffic Pattern

The rectangular course models the airport traffic pattern. Fly parallel to field boundaries, crab each straight leg, and adjust turn timing/bank so the corners remain square. Enter **45° to downwind** at an appropriate distance, just as pattern planning requires.

## K5 — Common Errors

The error chain usually begins with poor wind assessment or reference selection, then becomes late correction, excessive bank, altitude/airspeed loss, and fixation. Teach early visual recognition and small continuous correction.

## Risk Management

### Low Altitude / Stall-Spin / CFIT
**Hazard:** Maneuvering near terrain with changing bank. **Recognition:** excessive bank, skid, decaying speed, rising obstacles. **Consequence:** stall/spin or CFIT. **Mitigation:** safe area, **600–1,000 AGL**, coordinated flight, conservative bank, abort early.

### Collision and Fixation
**Hazard:** Looking only at the reference. **Recognition:** lost traffic scan or altitude drift. **Consequence:** collision or loss of control. **Mitigation:** scan **reference → attitude → traffic → instruments → reference**.

### Unsuitable Area
**Hazard:** People, structures, wires, livestock, or no forced-landing option. **Recognition:** hazards under/near the planned track. **Consequence:** danger to persons/property and no emergency margin. **Mitigation:** choose open terrain and reposition or cancel if unsuitable.

## Maneuver Procedures

### Rectangular Course

Enter **45° to the downwind leg**, 600–1,000 AGL. Hold an appropriate distance outside the field. Crab on each leg and adjust turn timing/bank to maintain a rectangle. Relate each leg to departure/upwind, crosswind, downwind, base, and final.

### S-Turns

Enter **directly downwind and perpendicular to the reference line**. Begin the steepest bank immediately after crossing; shallow continuously toward the upwind crossing. Reverse with **wings level directly over the line**, then build bank continuously toward the next downwind crossing. Keep semicircles equal.

### Turns Around a Point

Enter **directly downwind, abeam the point at the selected radius**. Begin with the steepest bank, shallow toward upwind, then steepen toward downwind. Keep the point at a constant distance and complete turns in the specified direction.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** The chosen field has power lines on the downwind side and the wind requires the steepest bank there.  
**Desired outcome:** Reject the area and choose another reference; do not accept obstacle exposure merely because the geometry is convenient.

# Fly

## Setup

- **Clear and assess wind** using available surface cues and weather information.
- Select an **unpopulated area**, prominent references, and nearby emergency landing option.
- Stabilize **600–1,000 feet AGL**, clean, at a suitable speed below $V_A$.
- **Rectangular course entry:** **45° to the downwind leg**.
- **S-turn entry:** **Directly downwind and perpendicular to the reference line**.
- **Turns-around-a-point entry:** **Directly downwind, abeam the point at the selected radius**.
- Brief wind corrections, bank limits, and exit.

## Instructor Demonstration

Demonstrate the evaluator-selected maneuver using an outside-dominant scan. Verbalize **wind direction, groundspeed trend, needed bank/crab, altitude/airspeed, traffic, and abort criteria** without allowing explanation to degrade control.

## Learner Practice

Practice each maneuver first with instructor wind calls, then learner calls, then simultaneous instruction. Require the learner to reject unsuitable references and terminate when safe parameters cannot be maintained.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Failure to clear above, below, and both sides** — Stop and restore continuous scan.
2. **Reference without emergency landing area** — Reposition to open terrain.
3. **Improper wind assessment** — Recheck cues and observe drift before entry.
4. **No stable level entry** — Stabilize altitude, speed, and trim first.
5. **Failure to maintain altitude or airspeed** — Restore outside attitude and power; simplify track correction.
6. **Rough or discontinuous controls** — Anticipate wind changes and blend inputs.
7. **Poor division of attention** — Use a deliberate repeating scan.
8. **Inaccurate turn timing** — Lead corners/crossings based on groundspeed.
9. **Uncoordinated controls** — Center the ball; do not use rudder to force track.
10. **Improper wind-drift correction** — More bank downwind, less upwind; crab straight legs.

## Completion Standards

At **600–1,000 feet AGL**, perform the selected maneuver with correct entry, ground track/radius, coordination and scan; maintain altitude **±100 feet** and airspeed **±10 knots**; teach and correct effectively.

[[answer: Heading is where the nose points; ground track is the actual path over Earth.]]

[[answer: Downwind, where groundspeed is highest and more turn rate is needed to hold radius.]]

### 3. How is a rectangular course related to a traffic pattern?
[[answer: It uses the same rectangular legs, entry planning, wind correction, spacing, and turn timing around a field.]]

### 4. Where do S-turns reverse?
[[answer: Wings level directly over and perpendicular to the reference line.]]

### 5. What are altitude and speed standards?
[[answer: Fly 600–1,000 feet AGL and maintain altitude ±100 feet and airspeed ±10 knots.]]

### 6. When should a reference be rejected?
[[answer: When people, structures, wires, terrain, traffic, or lack of an emergency landing option make the area unsuitable.]]
