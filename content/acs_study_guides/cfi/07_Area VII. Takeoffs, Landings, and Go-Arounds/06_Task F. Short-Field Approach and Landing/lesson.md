# Task F. Short-Field Approach and Landing

**Area VII - Takeoffs, Landings, and Go-Arounds**

## Objective

To determine the applicant understands short-field approach and landing, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate appropriate skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

Short-field approach and landing is a **precision operation**. It combines obstacle clearance, exact airspeed and energy control, a stabilized glidepath, minimum float, a designated touchdown point, and effective braking to land within a limited distance.

For the instructor applicant, the lesson is not simply “land short.” Teach the learner to choose a suitable runway and touchdown point, correct the glidepath early, avoid the back-side-of-the-power-curve trap, and execute a go-around before an approach becomes unrecoverable.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 30 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.8-1.2 hour, adjusted for runway, obstacle, wind, and traffic conditions.
- Current weather, NOTAMs, airport diagram, Chart Supplement, POH/AFM, landing-performance charts, checklist, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and approved Flight Guide visual references.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Calculate landing performance, identify obstacles and touchdown point, brief the approach and go-around gate, establish a stabilized short-field approach, touch down within the standard with minimum float, and apply manufacturer-recommended braking while maintaining directional control.

**Instructor:** Use the Binns sequence of precision/spot landing, obstacle clearance, flaps, airspeed and trim, stabilized approach, glidepath, power-curve awareness, performance, runway illusions, braking, and timely go-around decisions.

## Completion Standards

The learner explains short-field purpose/procedure, stabilized approach and energy management, atmospheric effects, wind correction, performance, and common errors; manages all stated risks; and demonstrates the applicable ACS skills. Touchdown occurs **on or within 100 feet beyond** the specified point, threshold markings, or runway numbers, with no side drift, minimum float, and the longitudinal axis aligned with the runway centerline.

# Teach

## AI.VII.F.K1 - Purpose of and Procedures for Short-Field Approach and Landing

Short-field approach and landing is used when the landing area is limited or obstacles constrain the approach. It is an **accuracy approach**: select the runway, obstacle path, touchdown point, configuration, and go-around gate before the final approach becomes busy.

Select a touchdown point on or within the first usable portion of the runway, but not so close to the threshold that a landing short would be unsafe. Use a prominent runway feature such as threshold/runway markings, the 1,000-foot markers, or a centerline stripe. Establish the airplane at pattern altitude and below V<sub>A</sub> before beginning the maneuver, then complete the landing checklist and radio calls.

Use the POH/AFM short-field procedure. Configure and trim early, establish the published short-field approach speed, and maintain a stabilized final. Use full flaps if the manufacturer specifies them. Maintain a controlled glidepath over obstacles, aim before the desired touchdown point to account for roundout and flare, and avoid floating.

### Cessna 172S POH Short-Field Landing

For the C172S, use the supplied POH procedure and the aircraft-specific landing-performance data:

1. **Airspeed:** **65-75 KIAS with flaps up** during the initial short-field approach setup.
2. **Wing flaps:** **30 degrees, full down**.
3. **Airspeed:** **61 KIAS until flare** in smooth-air conditions. Use a slightly higher speed in turbulence as appropriate to the POH and conditions.
4. **Power:** Reduce to idle after clearing the obstacle while lowering the nose as necessary to maintain approach speed and keep the aiming point stationary.
5. **Touchdown:** Main wheels first, with power off and the airplane aligned.
6. **Brakes:** Apply heavily as required for maximum stopping performance without sliding the tires.
7. **Wing flaps:** Retract after touchdown for maximum brake effectiveness, while holding the control wheel full back.

The **61 KIAS, 30-degree-flap, obstacle-clearance, and braking values** are C172S POH values, not universal short-field numbers. The POH procedure controls if it differs from a generic technique.

After touchdown, use the manufacturer-recommended aerodynamic and wheel braking procedure. Maintain directional control, hold the proper control position, and stop within the available area without locking the brakes or allowing the airplane to depart the runway.

## AI.VII.F.K2 - A Stabilized Approach, Including Energy Management Concepts

A stabilized short-field approach has a controlled glidepath, obstacle clearance, correct alignment, published airspeed, landing configuration, trim, appropriate descent rate, and power sufficient to maintain the path. Establish the stable final before the threshold area; do not use a steep, high-sink correction close to the ground as a substitute for early planning.

When **safety and conditions permit**, use a wider-than-normal pattern and a longer final. The additional distance gives the pilot time to configure and trim the airplane, adjust the descent angle, verify obstacle clearance, and establish a stabilized approach before the runway becomes the immediate problem. A wider pattern is an option for stabilization, not permission to ignore traffic, airspace, noise-abatement, or runway procedures.

At short-field approach speeds, the airplane may be on the **back side of the power curve**. High angle of attack and low airspeed increase induced drag. If the airplane is low, pulling back alone can reduce airspeed and increase sink. Use **power for glidepath/altitude trend** and **pitch/trim for airspeed**. Make corrections early and small; if the approach cannot be stabilized, go around.

If the airplane is high, reduce power and adjust pitch as necessary to steepen the path while protecting airspeed. If low, add power and adjust pitch to shallow the path while protecting airspeed. Do not dive at the aiming point or allow sink rate to become excessive near the surface.

## AI.VII.F.K3 - Effects of Atmospheric Conditions, Including Wind, on Approach and Landing Performance

Landing performance changes with weight, density altitude, temperature, pressure, wind, runway slope, surface condition, braking action, and obstacles. Tailwind increases groundspeed and landing distance; high density altitude increases true airspeed for a given indicated airspeed and reduces performance margin.

Crosswind, gusts, windshear, wake turbulence, wet/icy surfaces, and runway slope can make a calculated landing distance inadequate. Use the POH/AFM charts and a conservative operational margin. Before landing, ask the critical performance question: **Can the airplane land here and stop, and can it go around if the approach fails?**

Excess airspeed is especially consequential. A 10 percent increase in landing speed can increase landing distance by at least 21 percent. Do not carry excess speed into the roundout in the hope of correcting it after touchdown.

## AI.VII.F.K4 - Wind Correction Techniques on Approach and Landing

Crab on final to maintain the intended ground track, then transition to the crosswind correction required to align the longitudinal axis and eliminate side drift at touchdown. Maintain directional control and crosswind correction through rollout.

Do not close the downwind excessively because a narrow runway can create a **runway-width illusion** that makes the runway appear farther away. A too-tight downwind, uncorrected wind drift, or an overshooting crosswind can create a rushed base-to-final turn. Never use uncoordinated rudder to force the nose toward the runway; go around.

## AI.VII.F.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### 1. Overly Steep Approach and High Sink Rate

**Error:** The final approach requires an excessive descent angle or high sink rate to reach the touchdown point.

**Correction:** Correct the glidepath early with small power/pitch changes. If the approach cannot be stabilized without excessive sink, go around and rebuild the approach.

### 2. Unstable Approach

**Error:** Continuing with unstable airspeed, configuration, alignment, glidepath, trim, or power.

**Correction:** Establish stabilization before the threshold area. If it cannot be achieved promptly, go around; do not let the short runway pressure the pilot into continuing.

### 3. Delayed Glidepath Correction

**Error:** Waiting until short final to correct a high or low aim point.

**Correction:** Monitor aim-point movement and glidepath continuously. Make early, small corrections; do not dive, pull, or make a large late power change near the runway.

### 4. Too-Low Airspeed and Hard Landing

**Error:** Airspeed decays below the recommended short-field speed, making the flare ineffective and producing a hard touchdown.

**Correction:** Use pitch/trim to regain the published speed and power to support the path. If too low and too close to the runway, go around rather than increase angle of attack toward a stall.

### 5. Too-High Airspeed and Floating

**Error:** Excess speed produces a long float and loss of landing-distance margin.

**Correction:** Control speed before the flare. Do not force the airplane onto the runway or continue beyond the planned go-around point; execute a timely go-around.

### 6. Premature Power Reduction at Roundout

**Error:** Reducing power to idle too early causes an excessive sink rate and hard landing.

**Correction:** Maintain the power appropriate to the aircraft and approach until the flare/touchdown sequence is assured. Reduce power smoothly according to the POH/AFM and sight picture.

### 7. Touchdown With Excessive Airspeed

**Error:** Touchdown occurs fast, increasing the after-landing roll and braking demand.

**Correction:** Use the published speed, proper configuration, trim, and small power/pitch corrections. If the required touchdown speed or point cannot be achieved, go around.

### 8. Excessive or Unnecessary Braking

**Error:** Heavy or abrupt braking causes skids, tire damage, loss of directional control, or unnecessary stress when the approach was not properly stabilized.

**Correction:** Use aerodynamic braking and manufacturer-recommended wheel braking smoothly. Keep the airplane aligned, apply only the braking needed for the available distance, and avoid locking the wheels.

### 9. Failure to Maintain Directional Control

**Error:** The airplane drifts, yaws, or departs the runway during touchdown and rollout.

**Correction:** Maintain runway-axis alignment with rudder, hold the appropriate crosswind correction, use outside references, and keep both hands/feet available for control through braking.

### 10. Failure to Abort a Poor Approach

**Error:** Continuing an approach that cannot be completed safely within the touchdown and stopping standards.

**Correction:** Brief the go-around point before final. If the approach is unstable, the airplane is not on speed, float passes the first third, or any safety doubt exists, go around immediately.

## Risk Management

### AI.VII.F.R1 - Runway, Approach Path, Touchdown Area, and Performance

Select a runway and touchdown area based on wind, surface, slope, obstacles, pilot capability, aircraft performance/limitations, braking action, and available distance. Use the POH/AFM data and include margin. A short runway with no overrun area demands a conservative go/no-go decision before entering the pattern.

### AI.VII.F.R2 - Crosswind, Windshear, Tailwind, Wake Turbulence, and Surface/Condition

Evaluate crosswind, tailwind, gusts, windshear, wake turbulence, wet/icy contamination, rubber, slope, and braking action. Change runway, delay, divert, or go around when the combined conditions exceed the airplane or pilot margin.

### AI.VII.F.R3 - Go-Around and LAHSO

Brief the go-around and the first-third touchdown gate. Go around for unstable approach, excessive sink, excessive float, low airspeed, high airspeed, runway conflict, uncertain obstacle clearance, or any doubt about stopping safely. Accept LAHSO only when the pilot can safely meet the hold-short requirement after considering actual performance, wind, surface, and runway margin.

### AI.VII.F.R4 - Collision Hazards

Scan final, runway, adjoining areas, parallel/intersecting runways, vehicles, and obstacles. A landing clearance or radio call does not replace see-and-avoid. Continue the scan through touchdown and braking.

### AI.VII.F.R5 - Low Altitude Maneuvering, Stall, Spin, or CFIT

An overly steep approach, low airspeed, high angle of attack, or rushed base-to-final turn can produce a stall/spin or terrain impact. Do not skid to make the runway, pull to stretch a low approach, or dive toward an aiming point. Go around early.

### AI.VII.F.R6 - Distractions, Task Prioritization, Loss of Situational Awareness, or Disorientation

Complete the performance calculation, touchdown/go-around brief, checklist, and radio work before the workload peaks. During final and rollout, prioritize **aircraft control, runway alignment, speed, path, and braking**. Resolve noncritical tasks after the airplane is stopped or clear.

## Skills

### AI.VII.F.S1-S5 - Checklist, Radio, Runway, Scan, and Touchdown Point

Complete the checklist, make radio calls, verify the runway, scan the landing area and adjoining area, and select/aim for a suitable touchdown point. Identify the first-third go-around gate and runway-exit/braking plan before final.

### AI.VII.F.S6-S8 - Stabilized Configuration, Airspeed, Trim, Directional Control, and Wind Correction

Establish the POH/AFM configuration, trim, and short-field approach speed. Maintain published speed, or in its absence no more than 1.3 V<sub>SO</sub>, within **+/-5 knots** with gust factor applied. Maintain the glidepath with pitch/power, align with the runway, and correct wind drift throughout approach and landing.

### AI.VII.F.S9-S11 - Touchdown, Braking, and Directional Control

Make smooth control inputs before, during, and after touchdown. Touch down at the proper attitude on or within 100 feet beyond the specified point with no side drift, minimum float, and longitudinal-axis alignment. Use manufacturer-recommended configuration and braking, combining aerodynamic and wheel braking as appropriate.

### AI.VII.F.S12-S13 - Timely Go-Around and Error Correction

Execute a timely go-around whenever the approach cannot meet the tolerances or becomes unsafe. Analyze the error, identify its cue, apply the correction, and demonstrate the correction on the next approach.

# Fly

## Setup

- Review the POH/AFM short-field approach, flap/configuration, approach speed, landing distance, braking, and go-around procedure.
- Brief runway, obstacles, touchdown point, aiming point, wind, surface/braking, performance margin, first-third go-around gate, LAHSO if offered, and runway exit.
- Complete the landing checklist before final. Verify runway, traffic, landing clearance/status, obstacle path, and final approach.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Establish a normal downwind at pattern altitude and below V<sub>A</sub>. When safety, traffic, and local procedures permit, use a wider-than-normal pattern and longer final to create time for stabilization. Select a touchdown point and aim point; use a runway marking that permits a short landing without accepting a landing short.
2. Configure and trim according to the POH/AFM. For the C172S, begin the short-field approach setup at **65-75 KIAS with flaps up**, select **30 degrees flaps**, and establish **61 KIAS until flare** in smooth air. Use the longer final to adjust and stabilize the descent angle before the runway is close.
3. Fly a stabilized final over the obstacle path. Use small power/pitch corrections early; maintain a controlled, appropriate descent rate and do not create an excessive sink rate close to the runway.
4. Maintain the aim point stationary in the windscreen. As the obstacle is cleared, adjust power/configuration per the POH/AFM while protecting airspeed and the designated touchdown point.
5. After clearing the obstacle, progressively reduce power to idle while lowering the nose as necessary to maintain **61 KIAS** and keep the aiming point stationary. Round out and flare accurately with minimum float. Touch down **main wheels first**, with power off, on or within 100 feet beyond the point, with no side drift and the longitudinal axis aligned.
6. Hold the control wheel full back, retract flaps, and apply **heavy braking as required** without sliding the tires. Maintain directional control and stop within the available distance.
7. If the approach is unstable, speed is wrong, the touchdown point is missed, float reaches the first third, or stopping is doubtful, go around without delay.

## Learner Practice

Have the learner brief the runway, obstacle, aiming/touchdown points, speed, configuration, performance, braking, and go-around gate before each approach. Begin in conditions with comfortable margins, then add manageable crosswind or obstacle scenarios as proficiency permits.

Require the learner to verbalize aim-point movement and power/pitch corrections. Practice at least one planned go-around so the learner treats the maneuver as a normal safety decision rather than a failed landing.

## Instructor Demonstration Technique

Use progressive cues:

- **Downwind:** “Point, performance, configuration, first-third gate.”
- **Final:** “Stable path, speed, trim, obstacle, aim point, hand on throttle.”
- **Roundout:** “Minimum float, proper attitude, no side drift.”
- **Rollout:** “Align, aerodynamic braking, wheel braking per POH, stop safely.”
- **Decision:** “If the approach cannot meet the standard, go around now.”

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### High Sink or Delayed Correction

**Recognize:** The final becomes steep, the sink rate increases, or the aim point moves without an early correction.

**Correct:** Add appropriate power and adjust pitch to protect airspeed and shallow the path. If the approach cannot stabilize, go around.

### Low/High Airspeed

**Recognize:** Too low to flare effectively or too high to prevent float.

**Correct:** Reestablish the published speed with pitch/trim and use power for path. Do not force a hard touchdown or continue a long float.

### Braking and Directional-Control Error

**Recognize:** Abrupt braking, wheel lock, drift, yaw, or runway-edge movement.

**Correct:** Maintain runway alignment, use aerodynamic braking, apply smooth wheel braking per POH/AFM, and keep the outside scan active.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching a short-field approach and landing: performance/runway/obstacle planning; checklist, radio, and scan; stabilized configuration and speed; timely glidepath correction; accurate touchdown on or within 100 feet beyond the point with minimum float and no drift; manufacturer-recommended braking; directional control; and timely go-around when needed.

### 7. How does crosswind affect a short-field landing?

[[answer:
Crab to maintain final ground track, then use the appropriate crosswind correction to align the longitudinal axis and eliminate side drift. Maintain directional control and correction through braking; do not skid base-to-final to save the approach.
]]

### 8. What conditions require a go-around?

[[answer:
Unstable approach, excessive sink, low or high airspeed, delayed correction, excessive float, missed touchdown point, runway/obstacle/traffic conflict, inadequate stopping margin, or any doubt that the approach can be completed safely require a timely go-around.
]]
