# Task E. Short-Field Takeoff and Maximum Performance Climb

**Area VII - Takeoffs, Landings, and Go-Arounds**

## Objective

To determine the applicant understands short-field takeoff and maximum performance climb, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate appropriate skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

A short-field takeoff is a **maximum-performance operation**. It uses all available takeoff area, the manufacturer-approved configuration, precise airspeed control, and a planned obstacle-clearance path to produce the shortest practical ground roll and steepest practical initial climb.

The instructor applicant must teach the learner to use the POH/AFM performance data rather than habit, verify runway and obstacle conditions, hold the airplane with brakes while setting takeoff power when approved, rotate at or near the recommended V<sub>X</sub> attitude, and use ground effect when the airplane naturally lifts off before V<sub>X</sub>. The airplane should accelerate just above the runway to V<sub>X</sub>, climb at V<sub>X</sub> until the obstacle is cleared, then accelerate to V<sub>Y</sub> without premature configuration changes.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 25-30 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.6-1.0 hour, adjusted for runway, obstacle, wind, and traffic conditions.
- Current weather, NOTAMs, airport diagram, Chart Supplement, POH/AFM, performance charts, checklist, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and approved Flight Guide visual references.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Calculate performance, brief runway/obstacle/abort conditions, identify V<sub>R</sub>, V<sub>X</sub>, and V<sub>Y</sub>, configure from the POH/AFM, use all available runway, hold brakes while setting power when approved, and explain the transition from V<sub>X</sub> to V<sub>Y</sub>.

**Instructor:** Use the Binns sequence of short-field purpose, V-speeds, obstacle avoidance, left-turning tendencies, performance, runway safety, and step-by-step maximum-performance technique. Demonstrate the maneuver while maintaining a broad outside scan and explaining the exact decision points.

## Completion Standards

The learner explains the purpose, performance factors, V<sub>X</sub>/V<sub>Y</sub>, configuration, obstacle-clearance profile, left-turning tendencies, and common errors; manages the stated risks; and demonstrates the applicable ACS skills while maintaining the selected obstacle-clearance speed and V<sub>Y</sub> within **+/-5 knots**.

# Teach

## AI.VII.E.K1 - Purpose of and Procedures for Short-Field Takeoff and Maximum Performance Climb

A short-field takeoff is used when available runway is limited or an obstacle requires maximum takeoff and climb performance. Select the runway and departure path first, then use the exact POH/AFM configuration, power, rotation speed, obstacle-clearance speed, and climb procedure.

Complete the checklist and brief before entering the runway. Use **all available takeoff area**, including a permitted back-taxi when necessary. Align on centerline, stop, hold the brakes, apply full takeoff power as specified, verify engine and flight instruments, then release the brakes smoothly. Maintain directional control and remain on the ground until the recommended rotation speed.

### Cessna 172S POH Short-Field Takeoff

For the C172S, use the supplied POH procedure and the aircraft-specific performance data:

1. **Wing flaps:** **10 degrees**.
2. **Brakes:** Apply.
3. **Throttle:** Full open.
4. **Mixture:** Rich; above 3,000 feet, lean to obtain maximum RPM as specified by the POH.
5. **Brakes:** Release.
6. **Elevator:** Slightly tail low.
7. **Climb speed:** **56 KIAS** until all obstacles are cleared.
8. **Wing flaps:** Retract slowly after reaching **60 KIAS**.

The **56 KIAS obstacle-clearance speed** and **10-degree flap setting** are C172S POH values, not universal short-field numbers. If the airplane naturally lifts off before reaching 56 KIAS, keep the wheels just clear and use ground effect to accelerate; do not force the airplane back onto the runway or hold it down with forward elevator pressure.

Rotate at or near the recommended V<sub>X</sub> attitude. Some airplanes naturally lift off before reaching V<sub>X</sub>; when that occurs, **do not force the airplane to remain on the runway with forward elevator pressure**. Allow the airplane to lift off, lower the pitch attitude enough to remain just above the surface in ground effect, and accelerate with the wheels clear to V<sub>X</sub>. This reduces drag without loading the nosewheel or risking wheelbarrowing. Once V<sub>X</sub> is established, climb at V<sub>X</sub> until clear of the obstacle or the applicable 50-foot point. Then lower pitch, accelerate to V<sub>Y</sub>, verify a positive rate, configure per the POH/AFM, and continue V<sub>Y</sub> to a safe maneuvering altitude.

## AI.VII.E.K2 - Effects of Atmospheric Conditions, Including Wind, on Takeoff and Climb Performance

Takeoff performance changes with weight, pressure altitude, temperature, density altitude, wind, runway slope, surface, and obstacles. High density altitude, high temperature, high weight, and tailwind generally increase takeoff distance and reduce climb performance. A headwind can improve ground performance, but crosswind may reduce control margin.

Use the POH/AFM charts exactly as published, including aircraft weight, pressure altitude, temperature, wind correction, surface, and configuration notes. Add an operational margin appropriate to the aircraft, field, and technique; do not treat a chart result as a guarantee when conditions or surface differ from the test basis.

Windshear, gusts, wake turbulence, wet/contaminated surfaces, and runway slope can invalidate a comfortable-looking calculation. Reassess before brake release. If the airplane will not accelerate as expected, reject while there is runway remaining.

## AI.VII.E.K3 - Best Angle of Climb Speed (V<sub>X</sub>) and Best Rate of Climb Speed (V<sub>Y</sub>)

**V<sub>X</sub>** provides the greatest altitude gain per horizontal distance and is used to clear an obstacle. **V<sub>Y</sub>** provides the greatest altitude gain per unit of time and is used after obstacle clearance to reach a safe maneuvering altitude efficiently.

Use the exact values from the airplane's POH/AFM. V-speeds and performance may vary with aircraft model, weight, altitude, and configuration. Precise pitch control matters: premature acceleration to V<sub>Y</sub> can reduce obstacle clearance, while remaining at V<sub>X</sub> too long can reduce rate of climb and increase workload.

## AI.VII.E.K4 - Appropriate Airplane Configuration

Use the manufacturer-approved short-field configuration, which may include a specified flap setting, trim, fuel/mixture/propeller configuration, and power setting. Performance numbers are valid only for the configuration used to produce them.

Trim before entering the runway, confirm flight-control freedom, and position controls for the wind. Hold brakes only as the POH/AFM and aircraft procedure permit. After liftoff, do not retract gear or flaps until a positive rate of climb is verified and the manufacturer guidance/obstacle plan allows it. Retract flaps in the specified sequence to avoid a sudden loss of lift.

## AI.VII.E.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### Performance, Clearance, Runway, and Trim Errors

**Errors:** Failure to review AFM/POH and performance charts; failure to adequately clear the area; failure to utilize all available runway/takeoff area; and failure to have the airplane properly trimmed.

**Correction:** Calculate before taxi, brief runway/obstacles/abort, use the full available takeoff path, clear final and departure areas, and complete the checklist/trim verification before entering the runway.

### Liftoff and Initial-Climb Errors

**Errors:** Premature lift-off followed by a climb before V<sub>X</sub>; holding the airplane on the ground unnecessarily with excessive forward-elevator pressure; inadequate rotation causing excessive speed after lift-off; or inability to attain/maintain V<sub>X</sub>.

**Correction:** Rotate at or near the recommended V<sub>X</sub> attitude. If the airplane lifts off below V<sub>X</sub>, lower the pitch attitude with the wheels just clear, remain in ground effect, and accelerate to V<sub>X</sub>. Never force the airplane back down or hold it on the runway with excessive forward pressure; that increases nosewheel loading, can cause wheelbarrowing, and hinders acceleration.

### Scan and Configuration Errors

**Errors:** Fixation on the airspeed indicator during initial climb, or premature retraction of landing gear and/or flaps.

**Correction:** Set pitch with the natural horizon, verify airspeed briefly, maintain an outside scan for obstacles and traffic, and configure only after positive rate/obstacle clearance and manufacturer guidance permit.

## Risk Management

### AI.VII.E.R1 - Runway Selection, Performance, Available Distance, and Wind

Select a runway based on pilot capability, aircraft limitations, calculated performance, available distance, surface, slope, wind, and obstacles. Verify that the airplane can accelerate, rotate, clear the obstacle at V<sub>X</sub>, and stop safely if the takeoff is rejected. A legal runway may still be an unacceptable operational choice.

### AI.VII.E.R2 - Crosswind, Windshear, Tailwind, Wake Turbulence, and Surface/Condition

Crosswind reduces directional-control margin; tailwind increases ground roll; windshear changes airspeed and climb performance; wake turbulence can roll or sink the airplane; and wet, icy, contaminated, or rough surfaces can change acceleration and braking. Delay, change runway, reduce weight, or cancel when margins are inadequate.

### AI.VII.E.R3 - Rejected Takeoff and Engine Failure After Liftoff

Brief an abort point and the cues that require rejection: abnormal engine indications, inadequate acceleration, directional-control loss, runway conflict, or another unsafe condition. If rejecting, reduce power, maintain directional control, and brake according to the aircraft procedure and remaining runway.

After an engine failure after liftoff, **maintain aircraft control first**. Lower the nose to protect airspeed, coordinate, and land toward a plausible area, normally ahead. A turnback requires prior training, sufficient altitude, and suitable conditions; it is not an instinctive response.

### AI.VII.E.R4 - Collision Hazards

Before entering the runway, scan final, the runway, intersecting runways, parallel runway paths, and the departure area. At a nontowered airport, a radio call does not provide separation and aircraft may be NORDO. Continue the outside scan during the high-workload roll and climb.

### AI.VII.E.R5 - Low Altitude Maneuvering, Stall, Spin, or CFIT

Short-field climb at V<sub>X</sub> is near the airplane's low-speed/high-angle-of-attack region. Maintain coordination, avoid excessive pitch, and do not make unplanned turns toward obstacles or terrain. If performance is inadequate, lower pitch as needed to preserve airspeed and land toward a plausible area.

### AI.VII.E.R6 - Distractions, Task Prioritization, Loss of Situational Awareness, or Disorientation

Complete performance planning, checklist, trim, radio, and departure briefing before entering the runway. During the roll and initial climb, prioritize **aircraft control, runway/obstacle path, and outside scan**. Do not become fixated on the airspeed indicator, EFB, or radio at the expense of V<sub>X</sub>, alignment, or obstacle clearance.

## Skills

### AI.VII.E.S1-S6 - Checklist, Radio, Runway, Wind, Controls, and Available Area

Complete the checklist, make required radio calls, verify the correct runway, determine wind direction, position controls for the wind, clear the area, use maximum available takeoff area, and align precisely on centerline.

### AI.VII.E.S7-S8 - Brake-Held Power and Instrument Verification

Hold the brakes while setting takeoff power only as specified by the POH/AFM. Confirm takeoff power and normal engine/flight-instrument indications before releasing the brakes. Abort if indications or acceleration are abnormal.

### AI.VII.E.S9-S11 - Rotation, V<sub>X</sub>, Obstacle Clearance, and V<sub>Y</sub> Transition

Rotate at or near the recommended V<sub>X</sub> attitude. If the airplane naturally lifts off before V<sub>X</sub>, keep the wheels just clear, reduce pitch to remain in ground effect, and accelerate to V<sub>X</sub> +/-5 knots. Do not force the airplane to remain on the runway with forward pressure. Once V<sub>X</sub> is established, maintain it until the obstacle is cleared or the applicable 50-foot point; then lower pitch and accelerate to V<sub>Y</sub> +/-5 knots.

### AI.VII.E.S12-S15 - Configuration, Directional Control, Wind Drift, and Noise Abatement

Configure after positive rate and per manufacturer guidance. Maintain V<sub>Y</sub> +/-5 knots to a safe maneuvering altitude, remain coordinated, correct wind drift, maintain the assigned/departure path, and comply with noise-abatement procedures.

### AI.VII.E.S16 - Analyze and Correct Common Errors

First restore aircraft control and the outside picture. Then identify whether the error arose from performance planning, runway use, rotation, pitch/airspeed, scan, configuration, or wind correction; state the correction and demonstrate it on the next attempt.

# Fly

## Setup

- Review the POH/AFM short-field procedure, V<sub>R</sub>, V<sub>X</sub>, V<sub>Y</sub>, flap/trim configuration, performance charts, limitations, and rejected-takeoff procedure.
- Brief runway, available distance, obstacle height/location, surface, slope, wind, crosswind/tailwind, wake turbulence, abort point, engine-failure plan, departure/noise-abatement path, and traffic.
- Complete the before-takeoff checklist before entering the runway. Verify clearance/status, runway, final, and departure path.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Use all available runway, including an approved back-taxi when necessary. Align precisely on centerline and stop.
2. For the C172S, select **10 degrees flaps**, hold brakes, apply **full throttle**, set **mixture rich** (or lean above 3,000 feet for maximum RPM per POH), and confirm engine/flight-instrument indications before brake release.
3. Release brakes smoothly, maintain centerline with rudder, correct crosswind drift, and accelerate toward the recommended rotation/V<sub>X</sub> speed.
4. Rotate at or near the manufacturer-recommended V<sub>X</sub> attitude. If the C172S lifts off before **56 KIAS**, lower pitch enough to keep the wheels just clear and remain in ground effect while accelerating to **56 KIAS**. Do not force the airplane back onto the runway or hold it down with forward pressure.
5. Maintain **56 KIAS until all obstacles are cleared**. Then accelerate as specified and, after reaching **60 KIAS**, retract the flaps slowly.
6. After positive rate and according to the POH/AFM, establish the normal climb configuration and continue the applicable climb profile to a safe maneuvering altitude.

## Learner Practice

Have the learner brief performance, runway use, obstacle, rotation, V<sub>X</sub>/V<sub>Y</sub> transition, configuration, abort, and engine-failure plans before each attempt. Begin in conditions with comfortable margins, then introduce a crosswind or simulated obstacle only when control quality supports it.

Require an outside-reference callout during the roll and climb: centerline, tracking reference, obstacle path, pitch attitude, and traffic. Use a verbal or safe simulated rejected-takeoff/engine-failure exercise rather than creating a low-altitude surprise.

## Instructor Demonstration Technique

Use progressive cues:

- **Before runway:** “Performance, runway, obstacle, wind, abort, configuration.”
- **Brake-held setup:** “Centerline, full power, gauges, release.”
- **Initial climb:** “Rotate, V<sub>X</sub>, obstacle path, outside scan, coordinated.”
- **Transition:** “Clear/50 feet, lower pitch, V<sub>Y</sub>, positive rate, configure.”

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### Premature Liftoff or Improper Rotation

**Recognize:** The airplane leaves the runway before the recommended speed and the learner either climbs away below V<sub>X</sub> or forces the airplane back down/holds it on with forward pressure.

**Correct:** Rotate at or near the recommended V<sub>X</sub> attitude. If the airplane naturally lifts off early, lower pitch to remain just above the runway in ground effect and accelerate to V<sub>X</sub>. Do not force it back down: excessive forward pressure loads the nosewheel, can cause wheelbarrowing, and delays acceleration.

### Failure to Maintain V<sub>X</sub>

**Recognize:** The airplane climbs at an incorrect pitch/speed, obstacle clearance is degraded, or the learner fixates on the airspeed indicator.

**Correct:** Use the natural horizon to set pitch, verify the airspeed briefly, maintain the obstacle path, and make small corrections to hold V<sub>X</sub>.

### Premature Configuration Change

**Recognize:** Flaps/gear are retracted before positive rate or obstacle clearance, followed by sink or loss of climb performance.

**Correct:** Maintain the short-field configuration until the POH/AFM and obstacle plan permit change. Retract in the specified sequence and verify the climb before reaching inside.

### Runway/Performance Planning Error

**Recognize:** The learner has not used all available runway, lacks a performance calculation, or has no clear abort point.

**Correct:** Stop and rebrief before entering. Use the POH/AFM charts, maximize usable runway, identify obstacles and stopping options, and reject when acceleration or indications are not normal.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching a short-field takeoff and maximum-performance climb: performance/runway planning; checklist, radio, and runway safety; maximum available area; brake-held power and instrument verification; rotation at the recommended speed; V<sub>X</sub> +/-5 knots to obstacle clearance; V<sub>Y</sub> +/-5 knots afterward; proper configuration, directional control, wind correction, noise abatement, and common-error correction.

### 1. What makes a short-field takeoff a maximum-performance maneuver?

[[answer:
It uses the exact POH/AFM configuration, all available runway, brake-held takeoff power when approved, precise rotation and obstacle-clearance airspeeds, and a planned transition from V<sub>X</sub> to V<sub>Y</sub>.
]]

### 2. What is the purpose of V<sub>X</sub> during this maneuver?

[[answer:
V<sub>X</sub> provides the greatest altitude gain per horizontal distance and is maintained until the obstacle is cleared or the applicable 50-foot point is reached.
]]

### 3. When do you accelerate from V<sub>X</sub> to V<sub>Y</sub>?

[[answer:
After clearing the obstacle, or at 50 feet AGL when simulating an obstacle, lower pitch smoothly and accelerate to V<sub>Y</sub>. Then configure per the POH/AFM after positive rate is verified.
]]

### 4. Why are performance charts and configuration so important?

[[answer:
Weight, pressure altitude, temperature, density altitude, wind, surface, slope, obstacles, and configuration change the takeoff distance and climb performance. POH/AFM charts are valid only for their specified configuration and conditions.
]]

### 5. What are the common errors in this Task?

[[answer:
Failure to review performance data, inadequate clearing, failure to use all runway, improper trim, premature liftoff, holding the airplane down with excessive forward pressure, inadequate rotation, inability to maintain V<sub>X</sub>, airspeed-indicator fixation, and premature gear/flap retraction.
]]

### 6. What requires a rejected takeoff?

[[answer:
Abnormal engine indications, inadequate acceleration, loss of directional control, runway or traffic conflict, or any condition that makes continuation unsafe while stopping remains practical. Reduce power, maintain directional control, and brake according to the procedure.
]]

### 7. What is the engine-failure priority after liftoff?

[[answer:
Maintain aircraft control: lower the nose to protect airspeed, coordinate, and select a plausible landing area, normally ahead. A turnback requires prior training, sufficient altitude, and suitable conditions.
]]

### 8. How do you teach the learner to avoid airspeed fixation?

[[answer:
Use outside pitch and tracking references, hold the selected attitude, then briefly verify the airspeed. Keep scanning the obstacle path, runway alignment, traffic, and engine indications rather than chasing the airspeed indicator.
]]
