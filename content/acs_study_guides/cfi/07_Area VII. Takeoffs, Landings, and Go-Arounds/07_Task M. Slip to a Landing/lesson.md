# Task M. Slip to a Landing

**Area VII - Takeoffs, Landings, and Go-Arounds**

## Objective

To determine the applicant understands a slip to a landing, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate appropriate skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

A **forward slip to a landing** is a precision energy-management tool. It can dissipate altitude and increase descent rate without an immediate increase in airspeed, which is valuable during an engine-out emergency, an approach over obstacles, or an approach that is high when a go-around is not available or appropriate.

A slip is not a substitute for planning or a stabilized approach. Teach the learner to use it deliberately, maintain airspeed and ground track, recognize aircraft limitations, recover smoothly before touchdown, and preserve a go-around option whenever one exists.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 25-30 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.8-1.2 hour, adjusted for conditions and aircraft limitations.
- POH/AFM, checklist, airport diagram, performance data, wind information, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and approved Flight Guide visuals.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain forward versus side slips, select a touchdown point, brief the energy and recovery plan, enter and maintain a forward slip, correlate the slip with wind, transition to a sideslip or normal landing, and identify when to go around.

**Instructor:** Use the Binns and AFH sequence: purpose, slip aerodynamics, drag, airspeed-indicator error, entry, wind correction, recovery, aircraft limitations, and common-error correction. Demonstrate first at altitude, then in an appropriate landing pattern.

## Completion Standards

The learner explains and teaches the purpose, energy management, wind correction, limitations, and risks of a forward/side slip; establishes a stabilized approach, enters and maintains the slip, recovers smoothly, maintains the intended ground track, and lands within the applicable ACS tolerance without side drift and with the longitudinal axis aligned.

### Cessna 172S POH Limitations

The supplied C172S POH material controls the aircraft-specific limitations:

- Avoid **steep slips with flap settings greater than 20 degrees** because of possible elevator oscillation under certain airspeed, sideslip-angle, and center-of-gravity conditions.
- With one tank dry, limit a slip or skid to **30 seconds maximum**.
- Fuel quantity indicators may not be reliable during slips, skids, or unusual attitudes.
- Keep the fuel selector in **BOTH** for takeoff, climb, landing, and maneuvers involving prolonged slips or skids of more than 30 seconds.

Always verify the current POH/AFM for the aircraft flown.

# Teach

## AI.VII.M.K1 - Purpose of and Procedures for Forward or Side Slip to a Landing

A **forward slip** is used to dissipate altitude and increase descent rate without increasing airspeed. The airplane continues along its original ground path while the nose points away from that path. Aileron lowers one wing; opposite rudder prevents the airplane from turning.

A **sideslip** uses similar crossed controls, but the airplane's longitudinal axis remains aligned with the runway or original flightpath while the airplane moves sideways toward the low wing. Sideslips are commonly used for crosswind landings; forward slips are primarily an altitude/energy-management tool.

Plan the maneuver from downwind or base. Complete the checklist, select the runway and touchdown point, reduce power as appropriate, and establish a stabilized approach. Enter the forward slip when normal power/configuration changes are insufficient and a steeper descent is necessary. Recover by smoothly reducing the crossed-control inputs, returning to the normal glide attitude, and transitioning to the appropriate sideslip or normal landing before touchdown.

## AI.VII.M.K2 - Concepts of Energy Management During a Forward or Side Slip Approach

A slip creates substantial drag by presenting the fuselage and other surfaces obliquely to the relative wind and reducing the vertical component of lift. **Power is normally reduced to idle** when the purpose is to lose altitude; adding power opposes the drag and reduces the maneuver's effectiveness.

Control the slip's descent rate primarily with bank angle and control airspeed with pitch/attitude. Keep the nose below the horizon enough to prevent airspeed decay. The airspeed indicator may be erroneous because pitot/static airflow is disturbed, so use attitude, sound, control feel, and aircraft-specific references as well as the instrument.

A forward slip is an **energy-management tool**, not a repair for an approach that should have been stabilized earlier. If a safe normal approach or go-around remains available, use it. In an engine-out or confined-area emergency, a forward slip may provide rapid altitude loss without the unwanted speed increase associated with diving.

## AI.VII.M.K3 - Effects of Atmospheric Conditions, Including Wind, on Approach and Landing Performance

Wind changes the ground track and the amount of slip required. Crosswind, gusts, windshear, tailwind, wake turbulence, density altitude, runway surface, and obstacles affect the approach and stopping margin. A wind shift can move the airplane away from the selected landing area while the nose appears pointed elsewhere.

Airspeed fluctuations greater than approximately **5 knots** during landing may indicate windshear; discontinue an unsafe approach. Wake turbulence from a larger aircraft can sink and drift into the low-altitude approach. Preserve spacing and maintain the option to go around.

Surface condition and braking action determine whether the planned touchdown area is usable. A forward slip can improve the descent path, but it cannot create runway length, remove an obstacle, or make an unsafe surface acceptable.

## AI.VII.M.K4 - Wind Correction Techniques During a Forward or Side Slip

When a crosswind is present, bank toward the wind during the forward slip so the lateral component helps maintain the intended ground track. The amount of aileron changes the ground-track position; the amount of opposite rudder controls the nose direction and slip aggressiveness.

As the airplane approaches the runway, smoothly reduce the forward-slip inputs and transition to the proper crosswind sideslip. Align the longitudinal axis with the runway before touchdown. Do not land with the nose pointed away from the runway or with side drift.

## AI.VII.M.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### Pitch and Airspeed Control

**Errors:** Incorrect pitch adjustment, airspeed decay, or reacting to an erroneous airspeed indication.

**Correction:** Maintain the intended attitude and a nose-below-horizon slip picture. Use sound, control feel, outside references, and aircraft-specific experience to supplement the unreliable indicator.

### Power and Glidepath

**Errors:** Excess power while attempting to lose altitude, poor glidepath control, or using a slip instead of making an earlier energy decision.

**Correction:** Reduce power as appropriate, use bank to control descent rate, and recover or go around when the approach cannot be stabilized.

### Wind, Recovery, and Touchdown Alignment

**Errors:** Slipping in the same direction as the crosswind, late transition to a sideslip, landing with the longitudinal axis misaligned, or landing off centerline.

**Correction:** Correlate slip direction with wind, recover smoothly before touchdown, align the longitudinal axis with the runway, and maintain centerline with the proper crosswind correction.

## Risk Management

### AI.VII.M.R1 - Runway, Approach Path, and Touchdown Area Selection

Select a runway, approach path, and touchdown point based on capability, performance, wind, terrain, obstacles, surface, and available distance. A slip is not permission to accept a landing area that cannot be reached and stopped in safely.

### AI.VII.M.R2 - Crosswind, Windshear, Tailwind, Wake Turbulence, and Surface/Condition

Evaluate crosswind, windshear, tailwind, wake turbulence, surface condition, and braking action. A slip may increase descent rate but can reduce control margin and create misleading visual/instrument cues. Go around when conditions exceed the planned margin.

### AI.VII.M.R3 - Rejected Landing/Go-Around and LAHSO

Brief a go-around before beginning the approach. If the slip cannot be maintained or recovered, the runway alignment is lost, the airspeed/path is uncertain, or the touchdown area is no longer safe, discontinue the approach. Accept LAHSO only when the pilot can safely meet the hold-short requirement.

### AI.VII.M.R4 - Collision Hazards

Maintain an outside scan for traffic, obstacles, wires, and runway conflicts. The nose may not point toward the flightpath, which can distort the visual scan. Do not allow the cross-controlled maneuver to eliminate traffic awareness.

### AI.VII.M.R5 - Low Altitude Maneuvering, Stall, Spin, or CFIT

Avoid steep, rushed, or uncoordinated turns close to the surface. Keep airspeed from decaying, respect rudder authority, and do not use a slip to salvage a base-to-final overshoot. A slip is not a skid.

### AI.VII.M.R6 - Distractions, Task Prioritization, Loss of Situational Awareness, or Disorientation

Complete the checklist and maneuver brief before the workload peaks. Prioritize aircraft control, ground track, airspeed/attitude, runway alignment, and traffic. Resolve radio and noncritical tasks after the slip is stable or after a go-around.

### AI.VII.M.R7 - Forward Slip Operations, Fuel Flowage, Tail Stalls With Flaps, and Airspeed Control

Use the current POH/AFM limitations. For the supplied C172S material: avoid **steep slips with flaps greater than 20 degrees** because of possible elevator oscillation; with one tank dry, limit slip/skid duration to **30 seconds**; do not rely on fuel indicators during slips; and use **BOTH** for takeoff, climb, landing, and maneuvers involving prolonged slips/skids over 30 seconds. Flap configuration and slip duration must follow the aircraft POH.

### AI.VII.M.R8 - Surface Contact With Longitudinal Axis Misaligned

Recover before touchdown. Landing while the nose points away from the runway or while the airplane has side drift can impose damaging side loads and cause loss of control. Use rudder for alignment and aileron for crosswind correction.

### AI.VII.M.R9 - Unstable Approach

A forward slip should produce a controlled, explainable path. If bank, rudder, pitch, airspeed, ground track, or recovery timing becomes uncertain, discontinue the maneuver and go around when possible.

## Skills

### AI.VII.M.S1-S6 - Planning, Checklist, Radio, Downwind, Touchdown, and Configuration

Complete the checklist and radio calls; plan the flightpath; select the touchdown point; establish downwind parallel to the runway; and configure according to the POH/AFM. Begin below V<sub>A</sub> and use a normal pattern entry.

### AI.VII.M.S7-S9 - Slip Direction, Touchdown Alignment, and Ground Track

Correlate slip direction with the crosswind. Enter the forward slip with aileron and opposite rudder, maintain the intended ground track, and recover smoothly into a sideslip or normal landing before touchdown. Touch down at proper attitude, without side drift, with the longitudinal axis aligned.

### AI.VII.M.S10 - Analyze and Correct Common Errors

First stabilize the airplane and recover the slip if required. Then identify whether the error arose from pitch/airspeed, power, wind correlation, ground track, recovery timing, alignment, or task saturation, and demonstrate the correction.

# Fly

## Setup

- Review the POH/AFM slip limitations, flap limitations, fuel-selector requirements, airspeed references, and emergency/normal landing procedures.
- Brief runway, touchdown point, wind, slip direction, obstacle path, recovery altitude, go-around, traffic, surface, and fuel state.
- Complete the landing checklist before the maneuver. Use a safe altitude for initial slip demonstrations and a suitable pattern for the landing demonstration.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. At a safe altitude, demonstrate a coordinated flight condition, then enter a forward slip with power reduced/idle, aileron toward the low wing, opposite rudder, and the nose below the horizon.
2. Show that **bank angle controls descent rate**, rudder controls the nose direction/slip aggressiveness, and pitch/attitude protects airspeed. Demonstrate the practical rudder-authority limit.
3. Recover by smoothly leveling the wings and releasing rudder while readjusting pitch to the normal glide attitude. Do not release rudder abruptly.
4. In the pattern, establish downwind at pattern altitude, select a touchdown point, reduce power, and position the airplane on final above the normal glidepath.
5. Use the forward slip only as needed to steepen the path while maintaining the intended ground track. Bank toward the wind when a crosswind exists.
6. Recover in time for a normal landing or crosswind sideslip, align the longitudinal axis, and touch down within the applicable standard. Go around if recovery or alignment is uncertain.

## Learner Practice

Begin with altitude demonstrations of entry, steady-state slip, control effects, and recovery. Progress to a pattern demonstration only after the learner can maintain airspeed/attitude and ground track without fixation on the unreliable airspeed indicator.

Use a planned high approach or simulated emergency/obstacle scenario. Do not use a forward slip to rescue a poor base-to-final turn when a go-around is available. Practice the recovery before touchdown and require the learner to state the C172S flap/fuel limitations before applying the maneuver.

## Instructor Demonstration Technique

Use progressive cues:

- **Entry:** “Power idle, low wing, opposite rudder, nose below horizon.”
- **Maintain:** “Bank controls sink, rudder controls nose, attitude protects airspeed, track remains toward landing area.”
- **Recover:** “Wings level and rudder neutral together, normal glide attitude, align.”
- **Decision:** “If the slip or recovery is not stable, go around.”

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### Erroneous Airspeed Reliance

**Recognize:** The learner chases a fluctuating or falsely low indicator and allows attitude/airspeed control to degrade.

**Correct:** Use outside attitude, sound, control feel, and known trim condition; maintain the nose below the horizon and recover if control is uncertain.

### Excess Power or Wrong Slip Direction

**Recognize:** Descent rate is inadequate, or the wind pushes the airplane off the intended ground track.

**Correct:** Reduce power as appropriate, correlate the low wing with the wind, and adjust bank while preserving rudder authority and runway track.

### Late Recovery or Misaligned Touchdown

**Recognize:** The airplane reaches the flare still pointed away from the runway or with side drift.

**Correct:** Recover earlier, align with rudder, establish the crosswind sideslip as needed, and go around if alignment cannot be achieved safely.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching a slip to a landing: planning, checklist/radio, touchdown selection, correct configuration, forward-slip entry and maintenance, wind correlation, attitude/airspeed control, ground-track maintenance, smooth recovery, aligned touchdown, applicable POH limitations, and common-error correction.

[[answer:

### 4. What C172S limitations must be briefed?

[[answer:

]]

### 5. How should a forward slip be recovered before touchdown?

[[answer:
Smoothly level the wings and release rudder pressure together while readjusting pitch to the normal glide attitude. Then align the longitudinal axis with the runway and transition to the appropriate crosswind sideslip. Do not release rudder abruptly.

### 6. When should a pilot go around?

[[answer:
When the slip cannot be maintained or recovered, airspeed/attitude is uncertain, the ground track or runway alignment is lost, the touchdown area is no longer safe, or any other condition prevents a controlled landing.
]]
