# Task O. Power-Off 180° Accuracy Approach and Landing

**Area VII - Takeoffs, Landings, and Go-Arounds**

## Objective

To determine the applicant understands a power-off 180° accuracy approach and landing, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate appropriate skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

The **power-off 180** simulates an engine failure from the downwind key position to a preselected touchdown point. It develops judgment in glide distance, wind, configuration, approach geometry, and energy management while removing the throttle as a glidepath tool.

The maneuver is not a fixed landmark exercise. The key position is a starting reference from which the pilot continuously evaluates altitude, distance, wind, and landing options. A safe approach and landing take priority over forcing the airplane onto the selected spot.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 30 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.8-1.2 hour, adjusted for conditions and emergency-training safeguards.
- POH/AFM, checklist, airport diagram, wind information, performance data, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and approved Flight Guide visuals.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Select a runway and touchdown point, establish the key positions, close the throttle at the downwind key, trim for best glide, manage geometry/configuration/slips, and land within the standard or execute a go-around when safe.

**Instructor:** Use the Binns and AFH sequence of purpose, key positions, best glide, wind effects, glidepath tools, touchdown point, stabilization, steep-spiral relationship, safety, and common-error correction.

## Completion Standards

The learner explains the purpose, energy management, key positions, wind effects, configuration tools, forward-slip relationship, and common errors; manages the stated risks; and demonstrates an idle-power approach from the downwind key to a touchdown on or within **200 feet beyond** the specified point, without sacrificing a safe approach or landing.

# Teach

## AI.VII.O.K1 - Purpose of and Procedures for Proper 180° Accuracy Approach and Landing

The **180° power-off approach** is executed by gliding with idle power from a point on downwind to a preselected landing spot. It extends the 90° power-off approach by requiring the pilot to judge the glide through a 90° turn to base and then continue to final.

Begin at normal pattern altitude, below V<sub>A</sub>, and at a normal downwind distance. Complete the landing checklist and select a touchdown point with margin. At or before the downwind key, close the throttle, establish best glide, and plan the base and final geometry. The approach may be steeper and tighter than a normal powered landing.

After the downwind-to-base turn, use altitude and wind to position the base key. Continue the approach as a 90° power-off approach: maintain best glide, configure only when the glide is assured, use geometry or drag tools as needed, and never sacrifice a safe landing to reach the exact point.

## AI.VII.O.K2 - A Stabilized Approach, Including Energy Management Concepts

With power at idle, **pitch controls airspeed** and the airplane's geometry controls much of the glidepath. Best glide provides the greatest distance in still air for a given altitude loss. Flying substantially faster or slower shortens the glide.

The maneuver should be planned with enough energy to reach the runway and a reserve that can be dissipated with flaps, a forward slip, or geometry. Do not stretch an undershoot by raising the nose; the airplane will slow, sink, and may stall. Do not dive at an overshoot; use early configuration/geometry adjustments or go around.

A stabilized power-off approach is a smooth, coordinated glide with predictable airspeed, alignment, descent angle, and touchdown point. S-turns, slips, and configuration changes are tools, not substitutes for a safe stabilized approach.

## AI.VII.O.K3 - Effects of Atmospheric Conditions, Including Wind, on Approach and Landing

Wind changes ground distance, key-position geometry, base-leg placement, and the selected touchdown point. A headwind reduces glide distance over the ground and generally requires a higher best-glide speed to maximize distance into the wind. A strong tailwind can extend the downwind and make the glide too long.

Density altitude, weight, temperature, runway slope, surface, braking action, windshear, gusts, and wake turbulence affect the approach and landing. Select the runway and touchdown area before closing power. If the conditions make the glide or stopping margin unacceptable, discontinue the maneuver and use a safer landing option.

## AI.VII.O.K4 - Wind Correction Techniques on Approach and Landing

Use crab and coordinated wind correction on downwind, base, and final to maintain the intended ground track. A strong headwind on base may require a closer base; a tailwind may require an earlier turn or a different geometry.

If a forward slip is used, correlate the low wing with the wind, keep the nose below the horizon to protect airspeed, and recover to a sideslip or normal landing before touchdown. Never use a skid to increase gliding distance or tighten a turn.

## AI.VII.O.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### Key, Configuration, and Stabilization Errors

**Errors:** Failure to identify key positions, improper or premature configuration, or failure to establish and maintain a stabilized approach.

**Correction:** Use the keys as planning references, protect best glide, delay drag until the glide is assured, and continuously evaluate altitude/distance rather than chasing fixed landmarks.

### Wind, Geometry, and Slip Errors

**Errors:** Downwind/base geometry ignores wind, a skid is used instead of a forward slip, or the pilot tries to stretch the glide.

**Correction:** Move the base and adjust geometry early. Use a coordinated forward slip only when appropriate. Lower the nose to protect airspeed and go around when the runway cannot be reached safely.

### Touchdown and Rollout Errors

**Errors:** Improper roundout/touchdown, failure to hold back elevator, poor directional control, or improper braking.

**Correction:** Preserve the outside sight picture, touch down in the proper attitude, maintain directional control, and use aerodynamic/wheel braking as appropriate.

## Risk Management

### AI.VII.O.R1 - Runway, Approach Path, and Touchdown Area Selection

Select a runway and touchdown area based on wind, terrain, obstacles, surface, aircraft capability, performance, and available distance. A power-off accuracy maneuver never justifies selecting a landing area that cannot be safely reached and stopped in.

### AI.VII.O.R2 - Crosswind, Windshear, Tailwind, Wake Turbulence, and Surface/Condition

Evaluate wind, gusts, windshear, tailwind, wake turbulence, density altitude, runway slope, and surface/braking. These conditions can change the glide ratio and stopping margin after power is removed.

### AI.VII.O.R3 - Rejected Landing/Go-Around and LAHSO

Brief a go-around before closing power. If the airplane cannot reach the runway, airspeed is unsafe, the approach is unstable, or the touchdown/stop is doubtful, use the available go-around option or select the safest reachable landing area. Do not force a spot landing.

### AI.VII.O.R4 - Collision Hazards

Scan for traffic, obstacles, wires, vehicles, and runway conflicts throughout the glide. A power-off maneuver increases workload and reduces options; visual traffic awareness remains primary.

### AI.VII.O.R5 - Low-Altitude Maneuvering, Stall, Spin, or CFIT

Avoid skids, steep unplanned turns, excessive bank, and pulling to stretch the glide. Maintain a margin above stall, coordinate turns, and use a go-around or safer landing area before options disappear.

### AI.VII.O.R6 - Distractions and Situational Awareness

Complete the landing checklist and brief before power reduction. Prioritize airspeed, altitude, wind, runway, traffic, and landing options over radio or cockpit tasks.

### AI.VII.O.R7 - Forward Slip Operations

Use forward slips only within the aircraft's POH/AFM limitations. Keep airspeed under control, use the correct wind direction, avoid skids, and recognize that pitot/static indications may be unreliable in a slip.

## Skills

### AI.VII.O.S1-S4 - Checklist, Radio, Planning, and Touchdown Point

Complete the checklist and radio calls, plan the gliding flightpath, select a touchdown point, and account for wind, terrain, obstructions, surface, and aircraft limitations.

### AI.VII.O.S5-S6 - Downwind Position and Configuration

Position parallel to the runway on downwind, below V<sub>A</sub>, and configure the airplane in accordance with the POH/AFM while protecting the glide.

### AI.VII.O.S7-S8 - Slip Correlation, Touchdown, and Alignment

Correlate crosswind with any forward slip and transition to a sideslip before touchdown. Touch down at the proper attitude, within 200 feet beyond the point, without side drift, and with longitudinal-axis alignment.

### AI.VII.O.S9 - Analyze and Correct Common Errors

Identify whether the error came from key-position judgment, wind, airspeed, geometry, configuration, slip use, touchdown, or braking, then apply the correction without sacrificing safety.

# Fly

## Setup

- Review the POH/AFM best-glide speed, configuration, flap/gear limits, emergency checklist, and landing performance.
- Brief runway, touchdown point, downwind key, base key, wind, geometry, obstacles, slip/S-turn options, go-around, traffic, and surface.
- Complete the landing checklist before closing power. Use a safe training altitude and clear area for initial demonstrations.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Establish downwind at normal pattern altitude, below V<sub>A</sub>, parallel to the runway, and at the planned distance.
2. At or before abeam the selected touchdown point, close the throttle, establish best glide, trim, and maintain the initial glide attitude.
3. Turn base with the planned medium or slightly steeper bank. Position base using altitude, wind, and distance; do not chase a fixed landmark.
4. Continue to evaluate the glide. Use geometry, delayed/early flaps, or a forward slip as appropriate and permitted. Never skid or stretch the glide.
5. Establish final aligned with the runway, maintain best glide/appropriate approach speed, select an aim point before the touchdown point, and preserve a stabilized glide.
6. Round out, flare, touch down at the proper attitude, and use appropriate aerodynamic/wheel braking. Go around if the glide or landing cannot be completed safely.

## Learner Practice

Begin with altitude and verbal demonstrations of glide distance, wind, key positions, and configuration. Progress to pattern practice with a planned touchdown point and instructor-controlled power reduction.

Require the learner to verbalize whether the glide is short, long, or workable and to choose a correction before the runway becomes close. Practice a go-around rather than forcing an inaccurate or unsafe landing.

## Instructor Demonstration Technique

Use progressive cues:

- **Downwind key:** “Power idle, best glide, trim, assess.”
- **Base key:** “Altitude, wind, geometry; do not chase the landmark.”
- **Final:** “Aim point, best glide, configure only when assured.”
- **Correction:** “Geometry, flaps, slip; never skid or stretch.”
- **Decision:** “Safe landing doubtful: go around.”

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### Short or Long Glide

**Recognize:** The aim point moves away or closer, or the base geometry will not reach the runway.

**Correct:** Adjust geometry early; use appropriate configuration or forward slip to dissipate excess altitude. Never raise the nose to stretch an undershoot.

### Fixed-Key or Wind Error

**Recognize:** The learner treats a landmark as mandatory despite different altitude or wind.

**Correct:** Use the key as a reference, not a destination. Replan base and final using the actual energy state.

### Skid, Stall Margin, or Late Drag

**Recognize:** Uncoordinated turn, excessive bank, low airspeed, late flap/slip, or stall warning.

**Correct:** Lower the nose, regain coordination, and go around if safe recovery is doubtful.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching a power-off 180: checklist/radio, runway and touchdown selection, downwind key, idle-power best-glide transition, wind/geometry management, safe configuration/slip use, stabilized final, touchdown within 200 feet beyond the point, and common-error correction without sacrificing safety.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What is the purpose of the power-off 180?

[[answer:
It simulates an engine failure from downwind and develops judgment in glide distance, wind, energy, geometry, and landing-point selection.
]]

### 2. What happens at the downwind key?

[[answer:
At or before abeam the selected touchdown point, power is closed, the airplane is trimmed for best glide, and the pilot begins continuous evaluation of altitude, wind, distance, and base/final geometry.
]]

### 3. Why is the base key not a fixed landmark?

[[answer:
Altitude, wind, airplane performance, and distance vary. The base key is a planning reference, not a mandatory tree or landmark. The pilot must reposition base to make the glide work.
]]

### 4. How is airspeed controlled with power off?

[[answer:
Pitch controls airspeed. Raising the nose when already slow steepens the descent and risks a stall; lowering the nose increases airspeed and may shallow the glide. Use geometry and drag devices to manage excess altitude, never by stretching the glide.
]]

### 5. What tools can manage a long or short glide?

[[answer:
Approach geometry, airspeed adjustments within a safe margin, delayed or added flaps, and a forward slip when appropriate. Do not skid, and do not use an unsafe correction merely to reach the selected point.
]]

### 6. When should the pilot go around?

[[answer:
Whenever the runway cannot be reached safely, airspeed/stall margin is unacceptable, the approach is unstable, traffic or obstacles conflict, or the selected touchdown point would require an unsafe landing. A safe landing away from the exact point is better than forcing it.
]]
