# Task N. Go-Around-Rejected Landing

**Area VII - Takeoffs, Landings, and Go-Arounds**

## Objective

To determine the applicant understands go-around/rejected-landing procedures, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate appropriate skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

A **go-around is a normal maneuver** used when approach or landing parameters deviate from expectations or when continuing becomes hazardous. ATC requirements, runway hazards, traffic conflict, windshear, wake turbulence, mechanical failure, unstable approach, bounce, porpoise, or loss of directional control are all valid reasons to discontinue a landing.

The maneuver becomes dangerous through **delay, overpitch, improper configuration, loss of directional control, or failure to maintain an outside scan**. Teach the learner to decide early, apply power immediately, control pitch and airspeed, configure deliberately, remain clear of traffic and obstacles, and communicate only after the airplane is safely climbing.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 25-30 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.5-0.8 hour, adjusted for traffic and conditions.
- POH/AFM, checklist, airport diagram, weather, runway information, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and approved Flight Guide visuals.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Identify go-around triggers, state the pitch/power/configuration sequence, explain ground-effect and trim-stall risks, perform the C172S balked-landing procedure, maintain track and outside scan, and complete the climb checklist after the airplane is safe.

**Instructor:** Use the AFH and Binns sequence of decision, immediate power, pitch/trim control, configuration, obstacle/traffic avoidance, transition to normal climb, and communication. Demonstrate planned and scenario-based go-arounds from different points in the approach.

## Completion Standards

The learner explains go-around purpose, stabilization, energy management, atmospheric effects, wind correction, and common errors; identifies the stated risks; and makes a timely decision, applies power, establishes V<sub>X</sub>/V<sub>Y</sub> as appropriate, configures after positive rate, maintains directional control and traffic/obstacle clearance, and communicates when safe.

# Teach

## AI.VII.N.K1 - Purpose of and Procedures for Go-Around or Rejected Landing

A go-around is a **normal maneuver** used when landing parameters deviate from expectations or continuation is hazardous. Decide early and commit: **apply maximum allowable power immediately**, establish the correct climb pitch, maintain directional control, configure after positive rate, and communicate when safe.

Triggers include ATC instruction, runway or obstacle hazard, traffic conflict, overtaking another airplane, windshear, wake turbulence, mechanical failure, unstable approach, excessive float, bounce/porpoise, loss of alignment, or any doubt that the landing can be completed safely. A go-around may begin before touchdown or after a bounce/initial touchdown when continuing would be less safe.

### Cessna 172S POH Balked Landing

Use the supplied C172S POH procedure:

1. **Throttle:** Full open.
2. **Wing flaps:** Retract to **20 degrees**.
3. **Climb speed:** **60 KIAS**.
4. **Wing flaps:** **10 degrees** until obstacles are cleared.
5. **Wing flaps:** Retract after reaching a safe altitude and **65 KIAS**.

The POH procedure controls. The instructor must still maintain aircraft control, avoid overpitch, account for obstacles and traffic, and delay communication until safe.

## AI.VII.N.K2 - A Stabilized Approach, Including Energy Management Concepts

The best go-around is often prevented by recognizing an unstable approach early. Stabilization includes predictable path, alignment, airspeed, configuration, trim, descent rate, power, and a clear runway/traffic picture. Do not continue an approach that requires large or late corrections.

Energy management determines whether the airplane is high, low, fast, slow, or configured incorrectly. A high-energy approach may require an early go-around rather than a steep descent. A low-energy approach may require power, but adding power while pulling into a high angle of attack can worsen the situation. When the landing cannot be safely stabilized, go around.

## AI.VII.N.K3 - Effects of Atmospheric Conditions, Including Wind and Density Altitude

Wind, density altitude, temperature, tailwind, windshear, gusts, wake turbulence, runway slope, and surface condition affect go-around acceleration and climb performance. High density altitude and high temperature reduce available climb performance; tailwind and windshear can worsen the departure path.

Close to the ground, **ground effect reduces induced drag** and can create the appearance of extra climb performance. That performance is borrowed: when the airplane climbs out of ground effect, induced drag increases. Do not pitch for a climb that the airplane cannot sustain at safe airspeed.

## AI.VII.N.K4 - Wind Correction Techniques on Takeoff/Departure and Approach/Landing

Maintain the runway or assigned departure track during the initial climb unless traffic, obstacles, or ATC require maneuvering to the side. Use rudder for directional control and correct for wind drift with coordinated control inputs.

During a go-around from final, maintain the appropriate wind correction and avoid turning toward conflicting traffic or obstacles. If the runway is occupied or the go-around is initiated after accepting LAHSO, maneuver to the side of the runway/landing area as necessary and safe.

## AI.VII.N.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### Failure to Recognize or Decide

**Errors:** Failure to recognize the need, indecision, or delay after the landing becomes unsafe.

**Correction:** Brief go-around triggers before final and decide immediately when one occurs. Once committed, follow through; do not reverse the decision because the runway appears close.

### Power, Pitch, and Trim Errors

**Errors:** Delayed or abrupt power, improper pitch, failure to counter torque/P-factor, or failure to manage nose-up landing trim.

**Correction:** Apply power promptly but smoothly, use forward pressure as required, establish takeoff-like pitch, coordinate with rudder, and trim nose-down when control is established.

### Configuration and Track Errors

**Errors:** Improper flap/gear retraction, loss of recommended airspeed, failure to maintain track, or failure to remain clear of obstacles/traffic.

**Correction:** Verify positive rate before configuration changes, follow the POH, maintain V<sub>X</sub>/V<sub>Y</sub> as appropriate, and keep the outside scan active.

## Risk Management

### AI.VII.N.R1-R2 - Recognition and Low-Altitude Delay

The lower the airplane, the less time remains to recover from delay. Treat a go-around as an expected option and initiate it before the airplane reaches an unrecoverable energy or runway state.

### AI.VII.N.R3 - Power Application

Apply maximum allowable power without delay, but avoid an abrupt application that produces excessive yaw or loss of control. Anticipate torque, slipstream, and P-factor.

### AI.VII.N.R4 - Configuration

Do not retract flaps or gear before positive rate and manufacturer guidance. In the C172S balked landing, retract flaps to 20 degrees initially, climb at 60 KIAS, use 10 degrees until obstacles clear, then retract after safe altitude and 65 KIAS.

### AI.VII.N.R5 - Collision Hazards

Scan for runway traffic, departing aircraft, parallel/intersecting runway traffic, and pattern traffic. Maneuver to the side when necessary and safe; communicate after control is established.

### AI.VII.N.R6 - Low-Altitude Maneuvering, Stall, Spin, or CFIT

Avoid overpitch, skidding, abrupt turns, and premature climb out of ground effect. Maintain airspeed and coordination; if the stall warning sounds, lower the nose immediately.

### AI.VII.N.R7 - Distractions and Situational Awareness

Fly first. Do not become head-down with checklists, radio, trim, or flap controls. Establish a safe climb and outside picture before communicating or completing the climb checklist.

### AI.VII.N.R8 - LAHSO Go-Around

If a go-around is required after accepting LAHSO, follow the go-around procedure and do not attempt to force a landing to honor the hold-short point. Clear conflicting traffic and communicate when safe.

### AI.VII.N.R9 - Runway Incursion

An occupied runway, vehicle, aircraft, or unexpected hazard requires immediate discontinuation. Continue to scan after touchdown and during the climb; a go-around does not remove runway-incursion risk.

## Skills

### AI.VII.N.S1-S3 - Checklist, Radio, and Timely Decision

Complete the appropriate checklist when workload permits, make radio calls when safe, and make a timely decision to discontinue the landing whenever safety is in doubt.

### AI.VII.N.S4-S5 - Power, Pitch, Airspeed, and Configuration

Apply takeoff power immediately, establish V<sub>X</sub> or V<sub>Y</sub> as appropriate within +/-5 knots, and configure after positive rate or according to the POH.

### AI.VII.N.S6-S8 - Traffic, Climb, Track, and Wind Correction

Move to the side of the runway/landing area when necessary to avoid traffic, maintain V<sub>Y</sub> +/-5 knots to a safe maneuvering altitude, and maintain directional control and wind correction.

### AI.VII.N.S9 - Analyze and Correct Common Errors

Identify whether the error came from decision timing, power, pitch/trim, configuration, track, scan, or communication. State the correction and demonstrate the go-around again.

# Fly

## Setup

- Review the POH/AFM go-around/balked-landing procedure, flap limits, climb speeds, trim, obstacle path, and runway/traffic procedures.
- Brief triggers, power, pitch, flap/gear sequence, obstacle/traffic path, communication, and return-to-pattern plan.
- Complete the landing checklist before final and keep the go-around option mentally active.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Establish a normal stabilized approach and identify a decision point.
2. Announce the go-around decision, apply full power immediately, and counter the pitch-up with forward pressure as necessary.
3. Establish the climb attitude and V<sub>X</sub> or V<sub>Y</sub> as appropriate. Maintain coordination, runway/departure track, and outside scan.
4. After positive rate, configure per the POH. For the C172S balked landing: flaps 20 degrees, climb 60 KIAS, flaps 10 degrees until obstacles clear, then retract after safe altitude and 65 KIAS.
5. Maneuver to the side if necessary to clear traffic or runway conflicts. Communicate once the airplane is safely climbing.
6. Complete the climb checklist after the danger has passed and rejoin or continue as instructed.

## Learner Practice

Practice planned go-arounds from final, low final, and after a simulated runway conflict. Use a safe altitude and instructor-controlled scenarios for trim-stall awareness. Require the learner to verbalize the C172S flap/speed sequence and explain why configuration changes wait for positive rate.

## Instructor Demonstration Technique

Use progressive cues:

- **Decision:** “Unsafe or unstable: go around now.”
- **Power:** “Full power, forward pressure, right rudder.”
- **Climb:** “Pitch, airspeed, track, outside scan.”
- **Configure:** “Positive rate: flaps per POH.”
- **Communicate:** “Only after safe climb and traffic picture.”

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### Delay or Indecision

**Recognize:** The learner continues an unstable approach or waits to see whether the landing will work.

**Correct:** Decide at the first clear cue and apply power immediately.

### Overpitch and Trim-Stall Risk

**Recognize:** The nose rises sharply when power is applied, airspeed decays, or stall warning occurs.

**Correct:** Apply forward pressure, establish a takeoff-like pitch attitude, coordinate with rudder, and trim nose-down as needed.

### Configuration or Track Error

**Recognize:** Flaps retract prematurely, airspeed is not maintained, or the airplane drifts toward obstacles/traffic.

**Correct:** Positive rate first, follow the POH sequence, maintain outside references, and maneuver to the safe side when necessary.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching a go-around/rejected landing: timely recognition and decision, immediate power, controlled pitch/trim, appropriate V<sub>X</sub>/V<sub>Y</sub>, POH configuration, obstacle/traffic clearance, directional control, communication after safe climb, and correction of common errors.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. When is a go-around a normal and appropriate maneuver?

[[answer:
Whenever landing parameters deviate from expectations or continuation becomes hazardous: unstable approach, runway hazard, traffic, ATC instruction, windshear, wake turbulence, mechanical issue, bounce, porpoise, or loss of control.
]]

### 2. What is the immediate action after deciding to go around?

[[answer:
Apply maximum allowable power immediately, control pitch and airspeed, counter torque/P-factor with rudder, and establish the appropriate climb attitude.
]]

### 3. Why can a go-around produce a strong pitch-up?

[[answer:
Landing trim is often nose-up. Increasing power changes tail downwash and thrust effects, producing a strong pitch-up that can lead to a power-on or elevator-trim stall unless forward pressure and nose-down trim are applied.
]]

### 4. State the supplied C172S balked-landing procedure.

[[answer:
Full throttle; retract flaps to 20 degrees; climb at 60 KIAS; retract flaps to 10 degrees until obstacles are cleared; then retract after reaching a safe altitude and 65 KIAS.
]]

### 5. Why should the airplane not be pitched into a climb prematurely near the ground?

[[answer:
Ground effect reduces induced drag and can make performance appear better than it is. When the airplane climbs out of ground effect, that borrowed performance disappears. Maintain safe airspeed and climb capability before pitching away from the surface.
]]

### 6. What configuration changes wait for positive rate?

[[answer:
Gear and flap changes follow the POH/AFM, but the general principle is to establish a positive climb and safe control before retracting drag-producing configuration. The C172S balked-landing sequence is the controlling example for this airplane.
]]
