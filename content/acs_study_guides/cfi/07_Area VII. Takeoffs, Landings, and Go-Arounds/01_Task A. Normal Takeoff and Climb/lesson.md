# Task A. Normal Takeoff and Climb

**Area VII - Takeoffs, Landings, and Go-Arounds**

## Objective

To determine the applicant understands normal takeoff and climb, can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate appropriate skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

Every flight begins with a takeoff, but normal does not mean casual. A safe takeoff combines a correct performance decision, runway-incursion discipline, a controlled acceleration, prompt recognition of abnormal indications, wind correction, and an immediate plan for the first seconds after liftoff.

The instructor applicant must teach the learner to use the POH/AFM rather than remembered numbers, recognize the increasing left-turning tendency at high power and low speed, maintain runway and departure-path alignment, and reject a takeoff while the problem remains on the runway.

## Schedule / Equipment

- **Ground instruction:** 25-30 minutes.
- **Flight instruction:** 0.6-1.0 hour, adjusted for wind, runway, and traffic conditions.
- Current weather, NOTAMs, airport diagram, Chart Supplement, aircraft POH/AFM, checklist, performance data, and kneeboard.
- Whiteboard/model airplane and the approved Flight Guide visual references.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Complete the performance and departure brief, identify V-speeds from the airplane's POH/AFM, describe the four left-turning tendencies, conduct the checklist and runway verification, and fly the assigned takeoff/climb while maintaining directional control and scan.

**Instructor:** Use the Binns sequence of normal/crosswind technique, runway safety, V-speeds, left-turning tendencies, performance, and abnormal-operation planning. Demonstrate, then coach the learner through a normal and, when conditions permit, crosswind takeoff.

## Completion Standards

The learner explains normal and crosswind takeoff technique, atmospheric-performance effects, V<sub>X</sub>/V<sub>Y</sub>, configuration, and common errors; manages all stated risks; and demonstrates the applicable ACS skills while maintaining the manufacturer's recommended climb speed or V<sub>Y</sub> within **+/-5 knots** to a safe maneuvering altitude.

# Teach

## AI.VII.A.K1 - Procedures for Normal and Crosswind Takeoff

### Normal Takeoff

Complete the pre-takeoff checklist and departure brief before taxiing onto the runway. Verify the correct runway, clear the approach and departure paths, align precisely on centerline, and select a far runway reference for directional control.

Advance power **smoothly and continuously** to takeoff power; confirm expected engine and flight-instrument indications before rotation. Use rudder as required to maintain the centerline, make a smooth rotation at the POH/AFM-recommended airspeed, and establish the pitch attitude that accelerates to V<sub>Y</sub>. Maintain coordinated flight, correct for wind drift, and trim after the climb is established.

### Crosswind Takeoff

Crosswind technique begins with wind awareness before entering the runway. Apply aileron **into the wind** at the start of the roll and use rudder to keep the longitudinal axis aligned with the centerline. As speed increases, adjust aileron input for the actual lateral control needed; do not use a fixed control position without looking outside.

In a significant crosswind, retain positive control and allow the airplane to accelerate before liftoff. After liftoff, transition from the takeoff sideslip to a wings-level crab as necessary to maintain the extended runway centerline. The aircraft-specific POH/AFM and demonstrated crosswind capability guide the decision.

## AI.VII.A.K2 - Effects of Atmospheric Conditions, Including Wind, on Takeoff and Climb Performance

Wind changes ground performance, controllability, and the departure track. A headwind reduces the ground speed needed for a given airspeed and generally reduces takeoff roll; a tailwind increases the takeoff roll and may reduce the usable performance margin. Crosswinds require control input and must be evaluated against pilot capability, aircraft limitations, and actual gusts.

High density altitude reduces engine and propeller performance, increases takeoff distance, and reduces climb performance. Weight, runway slope, surface condition, temperature, pressure altitude, obstacles, and contaminated or soft surfaces must be considered with the POH/AFM performance data.

Windshear, gusts, and turbulence can change airspeed and performance near the surface. In gusty conditions, use the applicable POH/AFM technique and ensure adequate control before liftoff; do not rotate merely because the normal speed appears. Reevaluate a departure that cannot safely clear obstacles or meet performance expectations.

## AI.VII.A.K3 - Best Angle of Climb Speed (V<sub>X</sub>) and Best Rate of Climb Speed (V<sub>Y</sub>)

**V<sub>X</sub>** is the speed for the greatest gain in altitude over a given horizontal distance. It is used when obstacle clearance is the controlling need. **V<sub>Y</sub>** is the speed for the greatest gain in altitude in the least time and is the normal target after a standard takeoff unless another procedure applies.

The applicant must obtain the actual values from the **POH/AFM**. V-speeds may change with weight, altitude, configuration, or aircraft model. Teach the relationship, then use the manufacturer's published number and procedure.

## AI.VII.A.K4 - Appropriate Airplane Configuration

Use the configuration specified by the POH/AFM for the actual runway, wind, obstacle, and surface condition. Confirm trim, flap setting, fuel selector, mixture, propeller control, cowl flaps, lights, doors/windows, and any required system configuration through the checklist.

After a normal liftoff, establish a positive rate and follow manufacturer guidance for gear/flap retraction. Do not retract configuration early merely to make the airplane climb; first establish control, airspeed, and a safe flightpath. Continue takeoff power until the manufacturer procedure and safe altitude permit a change.

## AI.VII.A.K5 - Common Errors Related to This Task

### Runway or Checklist Complacency

**Error:** Entering the runway without a positive runway/clearance/traffic verification, or treating the checklist as a routine ritual.

**Correction:** Stop at the hold-short line, use the airport diagram, verify the runway heading/signage, complete the checklist, confirm clearance or nontowered status, and visually clear final and the runway.

### Abrupt Power, Directional Drift, or Improper Liftoff

**Error:** Advancing power abruptly, failing to anticipate left yaw, steering with brakes, lifting off early, or forcing the airplane into the air.

**Correction:** Add power smoothly, use rudder and outside references to remain on centerline, keep toes off the brakes, and rotate only at the recommended speed with the correct attitude.

### Airspeed or Instrument Fixation

**Error:** Chasing the airspeed indication while allowing pitch, bank, centerline, or traffic scan to degrade.

**Correction:** Set the pitch attitude with outside references, hold it, then verify the resulting airspeed. Maintain a broad scan through liftoff and climb.

### Inadequate Wind or Left-Turning-Tendency Correction

**Error:** Allowing drift, side-skipping, sideslip, or yaw because the learner uses rote control inputs rather than responding to the airplane.

**Correction:** Use the aileron/rudder inputs needed for the actual wind and left-turning forces. Keep the aircraft aligned with the runway and its extended centerline, then adjust the crab as wind changes.

## Risk Management

### AI.VII.A.R1 - Runway Selection, Performance, Available Distance, and Wind

Select a runway only after evaluating wind, crosswind/tailwind component, aircraft performance and limitations, runway length/width/slope/surface, obstacles, and pilot capability. A legal runway can still be a poor operational choice. Use calculated POH/AFM data with a margin appropriate to the conditions.

### AI.VII.A.R2 - Crosswind, Windshear, Tailwind, Wake Turbulence, and Surface Condition

Crosswind can produce side loads and loss of directional control; tailwind consumes runway and reduces margin. Windshear and gusts can cause unexpected performance changes near liftoff. Soft, wet, contaminated, or uneven surfaces affect acceleration, controllability, and stopping distance.

Avoid wake turbulence by delaying or changing the departure path as appropriate. Behind a larger/heavier airplane, rotate before its rotation point when operationally appropriate and remain above its departure path, while accounting for wind drift.

### AI.VII.A.R3 - Rejected Takeoff and Engine Failure After Liftoff

Brief an **abort point** and the events that require rejection: inadequate acceleration, abnormal engine indication, control problem, runway incursion, traffic conflict, or another unsafe condition while stopping remains practical. If rejected, reduce power, maintain directional control, and use braking consistent with the aircraft procedure and available runway.

Before takeoff, identify the first landing options and the departure path. After an engine failure immediately after liftoff, **maintain control first**: lower the nose to avoid a stall, coordinate, and select a plausible landing area, normally ahead. Do not attempt a turnback unless trained, planned, and supported by sufficient altitude and conditions.

### AI.VII.A.R4 - Collision Hazards

Clear final and the runway before entering. Look for aircraft that may not be transmitting, aircraft departing or landing on intersecting/parallel runways, and traffic hidden by the nose or wing. Continue the scan after liftoff; the first few hundred feet contain high convergence and limited recovery altitude.

### AI.VII.A.R5 - Low Altitude Maneuvering, Stall, Spin, or CFIT

The aircraft is near stall angle of attack after a power loss in the climb. The correct immediate response is a controlled reduction in pitch to preserve airspeed, not a reflexive pull. Maintain a safe flightpath, respect obstacles, and avoid steep or unplanned low-altitude turns.

### AI.VII.A.R6 - Distractions, Task Prioritization, Loss of Situational Awareness, or Disorientation

The takeoff roll is not the time to troubleshoot a radio, change an EFB page, or complete a forgotten item. Complete the checklist and brief before entering; once moving, **aviate first**. If an abnormal indication or distraction compromises the takeoff, reject while there is runway and control margin.

### AI.VII.A.R7 - Runway Incursion

Runway safety is a positive verification process: airport diagram, signs/markings, clearance or CTAF intention, runway number/heading, final/runway scan, and hold-short discipline. At a towered airport, cross or enter a runway only with the applicable authorization; a clearance to taxi does not authorize takeoff.

## Skills

### AI.VII.A.S1-S6 - Checklist, Radio, Runway, Wind, Controls, and Positioning

Use the checklist and departure brief before entering the runway. Make the appropriate radio call, verify the assigned/correct runway or takeoff path, determine wind from available reports and indicators, position controls for the wind, clear the area, and align precisely on centerline.

### AI.VII.A.S8 - Smooth Power and Instrument Check

Advance the throttle smoothly to takeoff power while holding centerline with rudder. Confirm expected engine and flight-instrument indications before rotation. If acceleration, power, or indications are abnormal, reject the takeoff while it remains the safer option.

### AI.VII.A.S11-S15 - Rotate, Climb, Configure, and Maintain Directional Control

Rotate at the recommended speed, establish the pitch attitude that accelerates to V<sub>Y</sub>, configure per the POH/AFM, and hold V<sub>Y</sub> within +/-5 knots to a safe maneuvering altitude. Maintain coordination, directional control, and wind-drift correction throughout the roll and climb; trim to reduce sustained control pressure.

### AI.VII.A.S16 - Noise Abatement and Standard Downwind Departure

Review published noise-abatement procedures before takeoff and comply unless safety, ATC, or an emergency requires otherwise. For **closed traffic or a standard downwind departure**, remain on the departure/upwind path beyond the departure end, then make the crosswind turn at the published/local pattern point or as instructed. When remaining in the pattern, the PHAK pattern guidance is to begin the crosswind turn within 300 feet of TPA; establish the downwind leg at TPA with the wind correction necessary to maintain its ground track.

Do not make an early turn toward downwind to meet an expectation. The assigned ATC departure, published procedure, obstacle environment, runway/traffic conditions, and safety control the actual path.

### AI.VII.A.S17 - Analyze and Correct Common Errors

First regain aircraft control and directional awareness. Then identify the error, state the cue that should have revealed it, apply the correction, and use the next takeoff to verify improvement.

# Fly

## Setup

- Review POH/AFM normal/crosswind takeoff procedures, V<sub>R</sub>, V<sub>X</sub>, V<sub>Y</sub>, takeoff distance, climb performance, and limitations for the actual airplane.
- Brief runway, wind, crosswind/tailwind component, obstacles, surface, traffic, wake turbulence, noise abatement, abort point, engine-failure plan, and intended departure or closed-traffic/downwind path.
- Complete the checklist before the hold-short line. Verify runway, clearance/status, final approach, runway, and departure path.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Taxi to the hold-short line; stop, verify the correct runway and traffic picture, then enter only when authorized/clear.
2. Align with centerline and select a distant tracking reference. Apply wind-correction controls for the actual wind.
3. Smoothly apply takeoff power. Confirm engine indications and acceleration, use right rudder as needed for left-turning tendencies, and keep the aircraft on centerline without brake steering.
4. At V<sub>R</sub>, rotate smoothly to the normal liftoff attitude. Do not force the airplane into the air.
5. Establish pitch to accelerate to V<sub>Y</sub>, maintain coordination and runway/extended-centerline ground track, and trim.
6. For closed traffic, continue straight ahead beyond the departure end; turn crosswind at the appropriate pattern point and establish a wind-corrected downwind at TPA. For a departure, follow the assigned or published departure path and noise-abatement procedure.

## Learner Practice

Have the learner give the complete takeoff brief before each attempt. Begin with normal conditions, then introduce a crosswind only within demonstrated capability and aircraft limits. Require the learner to verbalize the expected left yaw and the corrective use of rudder before advancing power.

Use a simulated abnormal indication early in the takeoff roll only when conditions permit a safe reject demonstration. For engine-failure-after-liftoff teaching, use a ground brief and appropriate safe-altitude simulation; do not create a low-altitude surprise.

## Instructor Demonstration Technique

Demonstrate one takeoff with progressive verbalization:

- **Before runway:** “Performance, runway, traffic, abort, engine failure, departure.”
- **On the roll:** “Centerline, smooth power, gauges, right rudder, wind correction, outside reference.”
- **At liftoff:** “Rotate at V<sub>R</sub>, attitude, accelerate to V<sub>Y</sub>, coordinated, trim, track.”
- **Departure:** “Remain straight ahead; turn only when the planned/assigned path and traffic picture support it.”

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

### Runway Incursion or Wrong-Runway Setup

**Recognize:** Clearance, runway sign/heading, diagram, or outside picture does not agree.

**Correct:** Stop before entering or discontinue the takeoff if safe. Resolve the discrepancy with ATC or by rechecking the airport information; do not proceed on assumption.

### Left of Centerline / Overcorrection

**Recognize:** Increasing right-rudder pressure is needed, centerline drifts, or the learner reacts with abrupt oscillating inputs.

**Correct:** Look far down the runway, make measured rudder corrections, and anticipate rather than chase the changing left-turning tendency. Keep feet on rudder, not brakes.

### Premature Liftoff or Excessive Pitch

**Recognize:** The airplane is forced off before recommended speed, control feel is weak, or pitch rapidly increases after liftoff.

**Correct:** Maintain the proper liftoff attitude, allow acceleration to the published speed, then use a small pitch correction and trim to establish V<sub>Y</sub>. Do not pull to escape ground effect.

### Failing to Fly the Assigned/Planned Departure

**Recognize:** An early downwind turn, a turn toward obstacles/traffic, or a missed noise-abatement/ATC direction.

**Correct:** Brief the path before takeoff. Maintain runway alignment through the departure end, follow the assigned or published procedure, and turn for crosswind/downwind only when the proper pattern point and traffic picture permit.

## Completion Standards

The applicant demonstrates while teaching the normal/crosswind takeoff and climb: proper planning and runway safety, checklist and radio use, wind/control positioning, smooth power and instrument check, rotation at the recommended speed, V<sub>Y</sub> +/-5 knots, directional control and drift correction, appropriate departure/noise-abatement procedure, and correction of common errors.

Both require directional control with rudder and the proper liftoff attitude. In a crosswind, begin with aileron into the wind, adjust it as aerodynamic effectiveness increases, use rudder to keep aligned with the runway, and establish a crab after liftoff to maintain the departure ground track.
]]

### 5. What conditions may require a rejected takeoff?

[[answer:
Examples include inadequate acceleration, abnormal engine indications, directional-control problems, runway incursions, traffic conflicts, or another unsafe condition while there is adequate runway to stop. Reduce power, maintain directional control, and use the aircraft procedure.
]]

### 6. What is your first priority after an engine failure immediately after liftoff?

[[answer:
Maintain aircraft control. Lower the nose as needed to prevent a stall, establish coordinated flight, and land toward a plausible area, normally ahead. A turnback requires prior training, planning, adequate altitude, and suitable conditions.
]]

### 7. Describe the standard closed-traffic/downwind departure plan.

[[answer:
Maintain runway alignment through the departure end, comply with the published or assigned path, and when remaining in the pattern turn crosswind within 300 feet of TPA when clear and appropriate. Establish a wind-corrected downwind at TPA. Do not make an early turn merely to reach downwind.
]]

### 8. How does runway safety continue after takeoff?

[[answer:
Use a continuous outside scan for aircraft, obstacles, and runway/parallel-runway conflicts; maintain the planned departure path; respect ATC and noise-abatement procedures; and avoid drifting into another runway's path. The takeoff clearance or CTAF call does not remove the pilot's see-and-avoid responsibility.
]]
