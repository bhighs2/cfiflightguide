# Task A. Communications, Light Signals, and Runway Lighting Systems

**Area VI — Airport and Seaplane Base Operations**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands communications, ATC light signals, and runway lighting systems and can apply that knowledge, manage associated risks, demonstrate the required skills, and provide effective instruction.

## Purpose

Whether at a busy towered airport or a quiet non-towered one, radio communication is essential to every flight — and when the radios fail, the light gun and the runway status lights still speak. A flight instructor must communicate with correct phraseology, read back what must be read back, recognize the light signals, and teach a learner to do the same and to manage lost communications.

This is a ground-focused Task. The ACS skill elements require the applicant to **select and activate frequencies, transmit with standard phraseology, acknowledge and comply with ATC, and analyze and correct common errors**.

## Schedule / Equipment

- Ground: 20 minutes
- Student Q&A: 10 minutes
- Airport diagrams, a VFR sectional chart, and the Chart Supplement
- Whiteboard / markers (optional)

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** explain the types of ATC and self-announce frequencies and their purpose, proper phraseology and the phonetic alphabet and required readbacks, self-announcing procedures, how to determine the correct frequency, lost-communication and light-signal procedures, and the runway status lights — then select and activate frequencies, transmit with proper phraseology, and comply with ATC instructions.

**Instructor:** deliver the ground lesson, have the learner make example radio calls and read back clearances, and require the learner to recognize the light signals and the lost-communication procedures.

## Completion Standards

The learner can explain and apply:

- The **types of ATC and self-announce frequencies** and their purpose.
- **Proper phraseology, the phonetic alphabet, and required readbacks**, and proper self-announcing procedures.
- How to **determine the correct frequency** on the ground and in the air.
- **Lost procedures, lost-communication procedures, ATC light-gun signals, and the runway status lights.**
- Before each flight: **select and activate the appropriate frequencies**, communicate with proper phraseology, and comply with ATC instructions.

## AI.VI.A.K1 — Obtaining the Appropriate Radio Frequencies

Know where to find the correct frequency. **On the ground**, use the **Chart Supplement**, the **airport diagram**, or the **VFR sectional** — the tower, ground, and ATIS frequencies are published. **In the air**, the important frequencies are printed on the **VFR sectional**. The common frequencies:

- **ATIS** — a recorded broadcast of the weather and airport information, coded by letter ("Information November").
- **Ground** — for taxi; **Tower** — for takeoff and landing.
- **Approach/Departure** — for radar service around the airport; **Center** — en route.
- **Guard (121.5)** — the emergency frequency, monitored.
- **FSS** — for flight service, briefings, and flight plans.
- **CTAF / UNICOM** — the common traffic advisory frequency at non-towered airports, and UNICOM for airport services.

## AI.VI.A.K2 — Radio Communication Procedures and Phraseology

Use the **standard phraseology** of the AIM and the Pilot/Controller Glossary. **Listen before transmitting — think, then speak.** Use the **phonetic alphabet** for call signs and alphanumeric identifiers (the tail number N12345 is "November One-Two-Three-Four-Five"; a runway 25 is "Two Five"). Read back what must be read back — hold-short instructions, runway assignments, clearances, and altitude/heading assignments.

<figure class="lesson-reference-figure">
    <img src="/knowledge/Phonetic_Alphabet.png" alt="The ICAO phonetic alphabet" loading="lazy">
    <figcaption>The phonetic alphabet — use it for call signs and alphanumeric identifiers.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Radio-call examples (from the Flight Guide [Sample Radio Calls](/knowledge/SampleRadioCalls.pdf)):**

- **Towered — taxi:** "Hooks Ground, Cessna 12345, at the south ramp, with Information November, ready to taxi."
- **Towered — takeoff:** "Hooks Tower, Cessna 12345, holding short Runway 35, ready for departure."
- **Non-towered (CTAF):** "Apopka Traffic, Cessna 12345, five miles west at 1,500 feet, entering a left downwind for Runway 33, Apopka Traffic."

The CTAF/self-announce format: **[frequency name], [call sign], [position], [intentions], [frequency name again]**.

## AI.VI.A.K3 — ATC Light-Signal Recognition

When radio communication is lost at a towered airport, the tower can direct the aircraft with a **light gun**. Recognize the signals (§ 91.125) — for aircraft **in flight** and **on the ground**:

- **Steady green** — in flight: cleared to land; on the ground: cleared for takeoff.
- **Flashing green** — in flight: return for landing (to be followed by steady green); on the ground: cleared to taxi.
- **Steady red** — in flight: give way to other aircraft and continue circling; on the ground: stop.
- **Flashing red** — in flight: airport unsafe, do not land; on the ground: taxi clear of the runway in use.
- **Flashing white** — on the ground: return to the starting point.
- **Alternating red and green** — exercise extreme caution (both).

## AI.VI.A.K4 — Appropriate Use of the Transponder

The **transponder** makes the aircraft visible to ATC radar. Use it appropriately: **squawk the assigned code**, **Mode C (altitude reporting)** where required, **1200 for VFR** when not on an assigned code, **7700 for an emergency**, **7600 for lost communications**, and **7500 for hijack**. Respond to "squawk ident" when ATC requests it. The transponder and ADS-B are required in the controlled airspace covered in Task G.

## AI.VI.A.K5 — Lost Communication Procedures

If communications are lost, **fly the airplane first** and navigate; then troubleshoot the radio (volume, squelch, frequency, the intercom, the headset). If communications cannot be restored: **squawk 7600**, and there is always an ATC facility listening on **121.5 (Guard)** — call "in the blind." At a towered airport, approach the traffic pattern carefully and **watch for the light-gun signals** from the tower. Under IFR, the lost-communication procedures of § 91.185 apply.

## AI.VI.A.K6 — Equipment Issues That Could Cause Loss of Communication

Communications can be lost for many reasons: an **electrical-system failure**, **incorrect radio or intercom settings**, a **failed headset or stuck mic**, or simply **dialing in the wrong frequency**. Before assuming a failure, check the simple things — the volume, the squelch, the frequency, the audio selector, and the headset connection. Know the equipment and how to isolate the issue.

## AI.VI.A.K7 — Radar Assistance

ATC radar can assist a VFR aircraft — **flight following**, traffic advisories, and, when needed, **vectors and DF steers** to a position or an airport. If lost or in difficulty, **don't hesitate to declare an emergency** — radar assistance and 121.5 are there for exactly that. (Radar services are covered in depth in Task H.)

## AI.VI.A.K8 — Runway Status Lighting Systems

**Runway Status Lights (RWSL)** are a fully automated system that tells pilots and surface-vehicle operators when it is **unsafe to enter, cross, take off from, or land on a runway** — independent of the human controllers. The system processes surveillance and activates:

- **Runway Entrance Lights (REL)** — red lights at the taxiway/runway intersection when it is unsafe to enter or cross.
- **Takeoff Hold Lights (THL)** — red lights ahead when it is unsafe to take off.

## AI.VI.A.K9 — Common Errors Related to This Task

- **Using the improper frequency:** confirm the frequency on the sectional, diagram, or Chart Supplement before transmitting.
- **Improper procedure and phraseology:** at a non-towered airport, state the call sign, position, runway, and intentions; read back the required items at a towered airport.
- **Not acknowledging or complying with ATC instructions:** acknowledge and comply, or say "unable."
- **Not understanding or complying with the light signals:** know the in-flight and on-the-ground meanings.
- **Hesitating to declare an emergency:** if in doubt, declare it — radar assistance and Guard are there to help.

## Risk Management

### AI.VI.A.R1 — Communication

Poor communication — a wrong frequency, a clipped transmission, a misunderstood clearance, a missed readback — is a runway-incursion and collision risk. Mitigate by listening first, using standard phraseology, reading back what must be read back, confirming the frequency, and asking when uncertain. (The communication figures in this lesson illustrate the barriers to and the monitoring of clear communication.)

### AI.VI.A.R2 — Deciding If and When to Declare an Emergency

Pilots hesitate to declare an emergency out of pride or fear of consequence — and pay for the delay. Mitigate by treating a genuine doubt, a lost radio in IMC, or a deteriorating situation as a reason to declare: the system is there to help, and an early declaration preserves options.

## Skills — Teaching the Task

The ACS skill elements require the applicant to **select the frequencies, transmit with standard phraseology, acknowledge and comply, and analyze the common errors**:

- **AI.VI.A.S1 — Select and activate the appropriate frequencies:** the learner finds and sets the correct frequencies on the ground and in the air.
- **AI.VI.A.S2 — Transmit with standard phraseology:** the learner makes radio calls using the AIM/Pilot-Controller Glossary phraseology — the towered and CTAF formats, the phonetic alphabet, and the required readbacks.
- **AI.VI.A.S3 — Acknowledge and comply:** the learner acknowledges radio communications and complies with ATC instructions, or says "unable."
- **AI.VI.A.S4 — Analyze and correct the common errors:** the learner recognizes and corrects the common communication errors in a scenario.
