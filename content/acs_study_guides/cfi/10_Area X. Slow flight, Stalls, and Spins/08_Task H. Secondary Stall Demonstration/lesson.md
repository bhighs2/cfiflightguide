# Task H. Secondary Stall Demonstration

**Area X — Slow Flight, Stalls, and Spins**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands secondary stalls, applies that knowledge, manages associated risks, demonstrates the required skills, and provides effective instruction.

## Purpose

A secondary stall occurs when a pilot incompletely recovers from an initial stall or raises the nose too aggressively before sufficient airspeed and control effectiveness return. This demonstration teaches why recovery must include a complete AOA reduction and a patient, smooth return to the flightpath.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **20–30 minutes ground**, **15–20 minutes demonstration**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** C172S POH/checklist, ACS, AFH, AC 61-67C, stall-recovery diagram, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain the incomplete-recovery error chain; demonstrate the primary stall, deliberate second critical-AOA exceedance, and final recovery while teaching avoidance.  
**Instructor:** Brief the complete sequence and altitude gates; guard yaw, pitch rate, and repeated stall; take control immediately if limits are approached.

## Completion Standards

Clear; complete above **3,000 feet AGL**; enter a stall in the specified configuration; exceed critical AOA a second time during recovery; recognize and recover promptly; and explain the conditions and errors that produce a secondary stall.

# Teach

## K1 — Aerodynamics

A secondary stall is aerodynamically another exceedance of **critical AOA** during recovery from a preceding stall. If the pilot reduces AOA only briefly or pulls abruptly while the airplane remains slow, the upward pitching motion and renewed load can exceed critical AOA again, sometimes more aggressively than the first stall.

## K2 — Flight Situations

- Abrupt pullout after power-off or power-on stall recovery
- Attempt to minimize altitude loss before speed/control effectiveness return
- Pullout from a spin or steep dive
- Power-only recovery without sufficient AOA reduction
- Startle response or ground-proximity fixation

## K3 — Recognition

Recognize the return of horn, buffet, mushy controls, uncommanded pitch/roll, or renewed loss of lift during the recovery. The second break may feel sharper because pitch rate and load are already increasing.

## K4 — Entry and Minimum Altitude

Complete above **3,000 feet AGL** and plan entry at least **3,500 feet AGL**. Enter a primary stall in the evaluator-specified configuration. Begin a deliberately incomplete recovery, then increase pitch too soon so critical AOA is exceeded a second time.

## K5 — Recovery

Recover from the secondary stall exactly as from any stall: **reduce AOA sufficiently and maintain the input until warning ends**, remove yaw, level wings after airflow is restored, add/confirm power, configure, accelerate, and return smoothly. More forward elevator may be required than during the incomplete first attempt, but avoid low/negative G.

## K6 — Common Errors

Key errors are wrong configuration, insufficient altitude, poor demonstration/recognition/recovery, and failure to explain the hazards of incomplete primary-stall recovery. Additional errors include poor coordination, insufficient AOA reduction, abrupt pullout, and failure to regain situational awareness.

## Risk Management

### Repeated Stall / Spin
**Hazard:** A second critical-AOA exceedance before speed returns. **Recognition:** warning/buffet returns during pullout. **Consequence:** deeper stall, spin, and greater altitude loss. **Mitigation:** complete AOA reduction, coordinate, accelerate, and raise the nose smoothly.

### Environment / Collision / Limitations
**Hazard:** Turbulence or high density altitude reduces margin; fixation interrupts clearing. **Mitigation:** suitable conditions, high entry altitude, continuous scan, configuration/airspeed limits, and prompt termination.

### Startle / Task Prioritization
**Hazard:** Ground-proximity instinct produces an abrupt pull. **Mitigation:** brief that **stall recovery may require altitude**; airplane control and AOA take priority over holding altitude.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** After a stall break the pilot lowers the nose only until the horn stops, then immediately pulls hard to avoid altitude loss; the horn and buffet return.  
**Desired outcome:** Identify a secondary stall, reduce AOA again and hold it reduced, coordinate, accelerate, then return smoothly to the flightpath.

# Fly

## Setup

- Clear; start ≥**3,500 feet AGL** and complete >**3,000 feet AGL**.
- Autopilot/wing leveler OFF; positive control transfer briefed.
- Use evaluator-specified configuration; observe $V_{FE}$ if flaps are used.
- Brief the **primary stall → incomplete recovery → secondary stall → complete recovery** sequence.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Enter the primary stall in the specified configuration.
2. Call the first cue and allow the primary stall to occur.
3. Reduce AOA only enough to begin recovery, then deliberately raise the nose too soon.
4. Exceed critical AOA a second time and call **“secondary stall.”**
5. Promptly apply sufficient nose-down elevator to eliminate warning; maintain coordination.
6. Level wings after airflow is restored; add/confirm power.
7. Configure incrementally and accelerate to appropriate $V_X$/$V_Y$.
8. Return to assigned flightpath and explain the prevention lesson.

## Learner Practice

Instructor demonstrates first. Applicant practice is closely guarded and limited to the planned sequence. Require verbal distinction between the **intentionally incomplete first recovery** and the **complete final recovery**.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Failure to establish selected configuration** — Stabilize and verify before the primary stall.
2. **Performing below minimum safe altitude** — Discontinue; protect the 3,000-foot floor.
3. **Inadequate recognition/recovery demonstration** — Call both stalls and show clearly different incomplete versus complete recovery.
4. **Failure to emphasize poor primary-recovery hazards** — Connect early pullout to renewed critical AOA, spin risk, and extra altitude loss.
5. **Uncoordinated primary or secondary stall** — Remove yaw; prevent spin entry.
6. **Insufficient final AOA reduction** — Hold nose-down input until warning is eliminated.
7. **Excessive forward elevator / negative G** — Use enough input to recover, not an abrupt push.
8. **Aileron or power before AOA reduction** — Unload first; then roll and manage power.
9. **Another premature pullout** — Accelerate and regain control effectiveness before increasing load.
10. **Failure to return to desired flightpath** — Reorient, scan, configure, and stabilize after recovery.

## Completion Standards

Demonstrate a primary stall, deliberate incomplete recovery, second critical-AOA exceedance, and prompt complete recovery above **3,000 feet AGL** while maintaining coordination, limitations, effective instruction, and error analysis.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What is a secondary stall?
[[answer: A second exceedance of critical AOA during an incomplete or overly aggressive recovery from a preceding stall.]]

### 2. Why may it be more aggressive?
[[answer: The airplane may still be slow while pitch rate and load are increasing rapidly, carrying the wing farther beyond critical AOA.]]

### 3. What is the minimum completion altitude?
[[answer: 3,000 feet AGL.]]

### 4. How is the final recovery different from the deliberately incomplete recovery?
[[answer: The final recovery reduces AOA sufficiently and keeps it reduced until warning ends, restores coordination and roll control, then accelerates before increasing load.]]

### 5. What operational error commonly causes a secondary stall?
[[answer: Pulling abruptly to avoid altitude loss before the airplane has regained adequate airspeed and control effectiveness.]]

### 6. What prevents the maneuver from becoming a spin?
[[answer: Maintaining coordination and promptly removing yaw while reducing AOA; terminate immediately if rotation develops.]]
