# Task A. Maneuvering During Slow Flight

**Area X — Slow Flight, Stalls, and Spins**

## Objective

Determine that the instructor applicant understands **maneuvering during slow flight in cruise configuration**, applies that knowledge, manages associated risks, demonstrates the required skills while providing effective instruction, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

## Purpose

Slow flight develops precise control at **low airspeed and high angle of attack (AOA)**, where control response is degraded, induced drag is high, yaw effects are pronounced, and the margin to a stall warning is small. These characteristics occur during **takeoff, climb, approach, landing, and go-around** operations.

The maneuver teaches the applicant to control **airspeed with pitch**, control **flightpath with power**, remain coordinated, divide attention, recognize the limits of stall-warning systems, and respond immediately to any stall indication.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested Schedule:** **25–35 minutes ground**, **25–35 minutes flight**, and **10 minutes debrief**.

**Equipment:** C172S POH and checklist, CFI ACS, Airplane Flying Handbook, Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, Binns lesson, model airplane or whiteboard, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner Actions**

- Review assigned references and C172S limitations.
- Explain slow-flight aerodynamics, warning cues, risks, and procedures.
- Establish and maneuver in slow flight while teaching.
- Maintain an outside-dominant scan, coordination, and ACS tolerances.
- Recognize and correct common errors.

**Instructor Actions**

- Verify clearing, altitude, airspace, configuration, and speed limitations.
- Demonstrate a smooth entry, stabilized maneuver, warning response, and recovery.
- Teach outside references, power/pitch coordination, rudder, trim, and division of attention.
- Introduce only one error or distraction at a time when safe.
- Debrief both aircraft control and instructional effectiveness.

## Completion Standards

The applicant completes the maneuver no lower than **1,500 feet AGL**, establishes the evaluator-specified configuration and an airspeed at which any further **increase in AOA**, **increase in load factor**, or **reduction in power** would produce a stall warning, and performs coordinated straight-and-level flight, turns, climbs, and descents **without a stall warning**.

The applicant maintains **altitude ±50 feet**, **heading ±10°**, **airspeed +5/-0 knots**, and **bank ±5°**, provides effective instruction, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

### Objective

**Teach the learner to establish, maneuver, and recover from slow flight while maintaining precise aircraft control at high angle of attack and low airspeed, remaining coordinated, staying clear of a stall warning, and understanding how pitch, power, load factor, configuration, weight, CG, and environmental conditions affect the airplane near the stall.**

### Purpose

Slow flight teaches precise aircraft control where the airplane is least forgiving:

- low airspeed,
- high angle of attack,
- high induced drag,
- reduced control effectiveness,
- pronounced yaw,
- and a small margin to a stall warning.

The teaching story is:

**clear → slow while holding altitude → establish the high-AOA condition → add the power needed to hold flightpath → trim → maneuver gently → stay coordinated → protect the stall margin → recover smoothly.**

The control relationship to teach is:

**Pitch controls airspeed.**  
**Power controls flightpath.**  
**Rudder maintains coordination.**

### Current ACS Target

The current CFI ACS does **not** define slow flight by one memorized airspeed.

The target is an airspeed at which any further:

- **increase in angle of attack,**
- **increase in load factor,** or
- **reduction in power**

would result in a **stall warning**.

The maneuver itself is flown **without the warning active**.

So the goal is not to sit on the horn.

The goal is to fly just above that point with a small but definite stall margin. fileciteturn81file0L64-L68

---

### Applicable FARs

There is no FAR that specifically prescribes the slow-flight maneuver, but several operating rules remain directly relevant:

- **14 CFR §91.103** — know the airplane, weather, area, and conditions before the maneuver.
- **14 CFR §91.113** — see-and-avoid / collision avoidance remains continuous.
- **14 CFR §91.119** — minimum safe altitudes still apply; select an altitude that leaves adequate margin for maneuvering and recovery.

For the CFI ACS, the maneuver must be completed **no lower than 1,500 feet AGL** in ASEL. fileciteturn81file0L64-L66

---

### C172S Airspeed and Flap References

From the C172S POH airspeed indicator markings:

- **White arc: 40–85 KIAS**
    - lower limit: maximum-weight **V<sub>SO</sub>**
    - upper limit: maximum speed permitted with flaps fully extended
- **Green arc: 48–129 KIAS**
    - lower limit: maximum-weight **V<sub>S1</sub>** in the referenced clean condition
    - upper limit: maximum structural cruising speed
- **Yellow arc: 129–163 KIAS**
    - caution range; smooth air only
- **Red line: 163 KIAS**
    - maximum speed for all operations

C172S flap limits used in the local slow-flight procedure:

- **Flaps 10° below 110 KIAS**
- **Flaps beyond 10° only below 85 KIAS**

Those speeds are limitations, not slow-flight target speeds.

---

### Important ACS vs. Local Training Procedure Distinction

The current CFI ACS evaluates slow flight in the **configuration specified by the evaluator** and the source lesson identifies **cruise configuration** as the normal Task A setup. fileciteturn81file0L64-L68

Your local training procedure uses progressive flap extension to full flaps and is useful for teaching configuration effects.

So teach both ideas clearly:

**ACS target:** establish the warning-free slow-flight condition in the evaluator-specified configuration.

**Local C172 training profile:** use the flap sequence below when practicing configured slow flight.

Do not confuse a local 50-knot training target with the ACS definition of the maneuver.

---

### Why the Airplane Feels Different

As the airplane slows while holding altitude, angle of attack must increase.

At higher angle of attack:

- induced drag increases,
- more power is required to maintain altitude,
- controls become less effective,
- larger control deflections may be required,
- left-turning tendencies become more noticeable,
- and the margin to critical AOA becomes smaller.

The pilot should feel the airplane becoming:

- softer on the controls,
- more yaw-sensitive,
- more power-dependent,
- and less tolerant of abrupt control inputs.

---

### Region of Reverse Command

Slow flight operates on or near the **back side of the power curve**.

In this region:

**slower flight requires more power to maintain the same flightpath.**

That is why the teaching relationship becomes so useful:

**Pitch → airspeed**  
**Power → flightpath**

They are still coupled, but this is the cleanest way to teach the maneuver.

If altitude starts decreasing at the correct airspeed:

**add power.**

If airspeed is too low:

**reduce AOA / lower pitch slightly.**

Do not simply keep pulling to save altitude.

---

### Angle of Attack, Load Factor, and Bank

A wing stalls because it exceeds its **critical angle of attack**, not because it reaches one universal airspeed.

In slow flight, the airplane is already operating close to that critical AOA.

If we increase bank while trying to maintain altitude:

- load factor increases,
- required lift increases,
- AOA increases,
- stall speed increases,
- and the margin to the warning decreases.

That is why the local training profile limits turns to **20° bank or less**.

That 20° value is a conservative training technique, not an ACS tolerance.

---

### Weight and CG

The exact warning speed is not fixed.

It changes with:

- weight,
- CG,
- configuration,
- load factor,
- power,
- and atmospheric conditions.

A heavier airplane generally reaches the stall warning at a higher indicated airspeed.

Forward CG generally requires more tail-down force and more wing lift.

Aft CG reduces longitudinal stability and can make stall/spin recovery less favorable.

That is why the ACS uses the **actual warning-free condition**, not one universal number.

---

### Power and Right Rudder

Slow flight combines:

- high power,
- low airspeed,
- and high AOA.

That makes the left-turning tendencies more pronounced.

Expect to use **more right rudder** than in cruise.

As power increases, right-rudder demand usually increases.

As power decreases, right-rudder demand usually decreases.

The airplane must remain coordinated because an uncoordinated stall near critical AOA can rapidly develop into a spin.

---

### Entry — Local C172 Training Procedure

Start with:

1. **Clearing turn at or above 1,500 feet AGL** with sufficient altitude to complete the maneuver above the ACS floor.
2. Establish a clean, stabilized condition below the applicable maneuvering speed.
3. **Power → approximately 1500 RPM.**
4. Below **110 KIAS**, select **flaps 10°**.
5. As airspeed decreases, **increase pitch to maintain altitude**.
6. **Trim** as the airplane stabilizes.
7. Once inside the **white arc**, extend the remaining flaps progressively to full flaps if practicing the local configured profile.
8. Approaching approximately **50 KIAS**, add power as needed — about **2000 RPM** is a training reference, not a fixed requirement.
9. Stabilize the airplane:
    - altitude steady,
    - airspeed steady,
    - warning off,
    - coordinated,
    - trimmed.

The important point is:

**Do not chase 50 knots.**

Use 50 KIAS only as the local training reference.

The ACS target is the actual warning-free condition. fileciteturn81file0L41-L43

---

### Straight-and-Level Slow Flight

Once stabilized:

- maintain the outside pitch picture,
- use **pitch for airspeed**,
- use **power for altitude / flightpath**,
- use **rudder for coordination**,
- and keep the stall warning off.

Use an outside-dominant scan:

**attitude → traffic → coordination → performance → attitude.**

Do not stare at the airspeed indicator.

The instruments confirm what the outside picture is producing.

---

### Turns

Perform turns using **20° bank or less** under the local training profile.

As bank increases:

- load factor increases,
- AOA requirement increases,
- stall margin decreases.

So anticipate the turn with:

- gentle bank,
- coordinated rudder,
- small power increase if needed,
- and enough pitch adjustment to preserve the target airspeed.

The ACS requires the specified bank to be maintained within **±5°**. fileciteturn81file0L66-L68

Do not pull harder to maintain altitude if that brings on the stall warning.

Use power and reduce AOA as needed.

---

### Climbs

To climb in slow flight:

- add power,
- add right rudder,
- maintain the slow-flight airspeed with pitch,
- and accept the climb performance the airplane can actually provide.

Do not demand an aggressive climb by increasing AOA.

At high density altitude or high weight, the airplane may have little excess power.

If climb performance is inadequate:

**protect airspeed and AOA first.**

---

### Descents

To descend in slow flight:

- reduce power gradually,
- adjust pitch to preserve the target airspeed,
- maintain coordination,
- and monitor the stall margin.

Because a reduction in power is itself one of the changes that can produce a stall warning at the ACS target condition, be ready to reduce AOA immediately if the warning activates.

---

### Stall Warning — Immediate Response

This is the key safety rule.

If the horn, buffet, or another valid warning occurs:

**reduce angle of attack immediately until the warning stops.**

Then:

- maintain coordination,
- use power as needed,
- and reestablish the desired flightpath.

Do not hold altitude or assigned pitch at the expense of stall avoidance.

The maneuver is supposed to remain **warning-free**. fileciteturn81file0L41-L43

---

### Recovery — Local C172 Training Procedure

From the configured local training profile:

1. **Apply FULL POWER.**
2. **Flaps → 20°.**
3. Reduce pitch as needed to **maintain altitude and reduce AOA**.
4. Apply right rudder as required.
5. **Trim** as control pressures change.
6. Accelerating through **55 KIAS → flaps 10°.**
7. **Trim** as required.
8. Accelerating through **60 KIAS → flaps 0°.**
9. **Trim** as required.
10. Accelerate to normal cruise or the specified airspeed.
11. Reduce power as necessary.
12. Complete the cruise / after-maneuver flow.

The important recovery principle is:

**power + reduced AOA + coordination + progressive cleanup.**

Do not dump all the flaps at once.

---

### Environmental Effects

Turbulence, wind shear, microbursts, and high density altitude all reduce the margin available in slow flight.

#### Turbulence

A sudden gust can change:

- AOA,
- load factor,
- airspeed,
- and stall margin.

If conditions are too rough to maintain a warning-free margin:

**discontinue the maneuver.**

#### High Density Altitude

High density altitude reduces:

- engine power,
- propeller performance,
- and climb capability.

If the airplane cannot hold altitude at the target condition, do not keep increasing AOA.

Use a safer airspeed and terminate or modify the maneuver.

---

### Collision Avoidance

Slow flight creates high cockpit workload.

Under **§91.113**, traffic avoidance remains continuous.

Clear before entry and keep the scan outside.

Do not let:

- the airspeed indicator,
- trim,
- flap lever,
- or explanation of the maneuver

take your eyes away from the traffic environment for too long.

---

### Common Errors

#### Failure to Clear

**Error:** Starting the maneuver without an adequate traffic scan.

**Correction:** Clear first and continue scanning throughout.

#### Altitude Loss During Deceleration

**Error:** Not increasing pitch enough as power is reduced.

**Correction:** Increase back pressure smoothly while slowing.

#### Climb / Rapid Airspeed Decay

**Error:** Excessive back pressure during entry.

**Correction:** Reduce AOA slightly and reestablish the proper sight picture.

#### Insufficient Right Rudder

**Error:** Nose yaws left as power and AOA increase.

**Correction:** Anticipate with coordinated right rudder.

#### Instrument Fixation

**Error:** Trying to fly the maneuver from the ASI or altimeter.

**Correction:** Fly the outside picture; use instruments only to verify.

#### Poor Flap Management

**Error:** Extending outside the flap limits or retracting too quickly.

**Correction:** Respect **110 KIAS for 10°** and **85 KIAS beyond 10°**; clean up progressively.

#### Poor Power Management

**Error:** Pulling to correct sink instead of adding power.

**Correction:** Use power for flightpath and pitch for airspeed.

#### Poor Trim

**Error:** Holding excessive pressure or trimming before the airplane is stable.

**Correction:** Stabilize attitude and power first, then trim.

#### Stall Warning Allowed to Continue

**Error:** Treating the horn as normal slow flight.

**Correction:** **Reduce AOA immediately.**

---

### Instructor Demonstration

Teach it in this order:

1. Clear the area and select an altitude allowing completion above **1,500 feet AGL**.
2. Explain that the ACS target is **warning-free slow flight**, not one fixed number.
3. Reduce power to about **1500 RPM**.
4. Below **110 KIAS → flaps 10°** if using the local configured profile.
5. Increase pitch to maintain altitude as speed decreases.
6. Trim.
7. Inside the white arc, progressively extend full flaps if practicing the configured profile.
8. Approaching approximately **50 KIAS**, add power as required — roughly **2000 RPM** is only a reference.
9. Stabilize with:
    - warning off,
    - coordination,
    - altitude,
    - airspeed,
    - and trim.
10. Demonstrate straight-and-level flight.
11. Demonstrate turns **20° bank or less**.
12. Demonstrate climbs and descents.
13. Throughout:
    - pitch = airspeed,
    - power = flightpath,
    - rudder = coordination.
14. At any stall warning:
    - **reduce AOA immediately.**
15. Recover:
    - full power,
    - flaps 20°,
    - reduce pitch / hold altitude,
    - right rudder,
    - 55 KIAS → flaps 10°,
    - 60 KIAS → flaps 0°,
    - accelerate,
    - cruise power,
    - trim.

A concise teaching flow is:

**“Clear. Power back. Hold altitude and slow. Configure. Trim. Add power before the warning. Pitch for airspeed, power for altitude, rudder for coordination. Maneuver gently. Any warning — reduce AOA. Recover with power, reduce AOA, clean up progressively, accelerate, trim.”**

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures for Proper Slow Flight

Slow flight develops the ability to operate at **low airspeed and high AOA** while preserving precise, coordinated control. The current CFI Task evaluates the maneuver in **cruise configuration**, although understanding configuration effects remains essential because comparable conditions occur during takeoff, approach, landing, and go-around.

### Establishing Slow Flight

1. **Clear the area** and confirm the maneuver can be completed above **1,500 feet AGL**.
2. Stabilize in **straight-and-level cruise configuration** at a speed below the applicable $V_A$.
3. Reduce power smoothly and increase back-elevator pressure enough to **maintain altitude** while airspeed decreases.
4. Approaching the target, add power to arrest further deceleration and maintain altitude.
5. Establish a speed at which any further **AOA increase**, **load-factor increase**, or **power reduction** would cause a stall warning, but maintain the maneuver **without activating the warning**.
6. Stabilize the outside attitude, coordination, power, and trim.

### Maneuvering

Perform **straight-and-level flight, turns, climbs, and descents** as assigned. Use **pitch primarily for airspeed**, **power primarily for flightpath**, coordinated rudder, and smooth bank changes. During turns, anticipate the added lift and power needed to maintain altitude without increasing AOA into a warning.

### Recovery

Apply power, reduce AOA with forward elevator pressure, maintain coordination, level the wings as necessary, accelerate, return to the desired flightpath, and retrim. If configured with flaps at the evaluator's direction, retract them incrementally within POH limitations while anticipating the associated AOA and trim changes.

ACS Target |

## K2 — Aerodynamics in Various Configurations

### Angle of Attack, Airspeed, and Load Factor

A wing stalls when it exceeds its **critical AOA**, not when it reaches one universal airspeed. At slow-flight speed, only a small AOA margin remains. Increasing bank while maintaining altitude increases load factor and required lift, so the airplane reaches the warning sooner.

<figure class="lesson-reference-figure">
	<img src="/knowledge/Critical_AOA.png" alt="Airfoil airflow at low, high, and critical angles of attack" loading="lazy">
	<figcaption>As AOA approaches critical, airflow separation increases and the margin to a stall disappears.</figcaption>
</figure>

### Region of Reverse Command

Below $L/D_{MAX}$, induced drag increases rapidly as speed decreases. The airplane exhibits **speed instability**: a disturbance that reduces speed can lead to further deceleration unless the pilot reduces AOA or adds power. In this region, **pitch is the more effective airspeed control** and **power is the more effective flightpath control**.

<figure class="lesson-reference-figure">
	<img src="/knowledge/Power_Curve.png" alt="Power required curve showing the region of reverse command at low airspeed" loading="lazy">
	<figcaption>On the back side of the power curve, slower flight requires more power because induced drag rises sharply.</figcaption>
</figure>

### Power, Attitude, and Yaw Effects

Slow flight requires relatively high power at high AOA. **Torque, spiraling slipstream, and P-factor** create pronounced left yaw, so increasing power generally requires increasing **right rudder**. Reduced airflow makes controls less effective and requires larger but still smooth inputs.

### Weight and Center of Gravity

- **Higher weight** requires more lift and a higher AOA at a given speed, increasing the warning/stall speed.
- **Forward CG** requires greater tail-down force and wing lift, generally increasing stall speed and control force.
- **Aft CG** reduces longitudinal stability and can make stall/spin recovery more difficult.

### Configuration Effects

Flaps change wing camber, lift, drag, pitch, trim, and stall behavior. Configuration changes must occur within the POH limits and must be anticipated with coordinated pitch, power, and trim. Abrupt flap retraction in slow flight can reduce lift and increase required AOA enough to cause a stall.

### C172S Speed References and Limits

| Item | C172S reference / limit | Instructional use |
|---|---:|---|
| **$V_A$ at 2,550 lb** | **105 KIAS** | Maximum entry/control reference at maximum gross weight |
| **$V_A$ at 2,200 lb** | **98 KIAS** | Use lower $V_A$ as weight decreases |
| **$V_A$ at 1,900 lb** | **90 KIAS** | Use lower $V_A$ as weight decreases |
| **$V_{FE}$, 10° flaps** | **110 KIAS** | Do not exceed with 10° flaps |
| **$V_{FE}$, 10°–30° flaps** | **85 KIAS** | Do not exceed beyond 10° flaps |
| **Lower green arc, $V_{S1}$ reference** | **48 KIAS** | Maximum-weight, forward-CG, flaps-up reference; not a fixed target |
| **Lower white arc, $V_{S0}$ reference** | **40 KIAS** | Maximum-weight landing-configuration reference; not a fixed target |

For planning, the AFH suggests approximately **5–10 knots above the 1G stall speed** for the actual flight condition. In the C172S cruise configuration at the POH reference condition, that corresponds roughly to **53–58 KIAS**, but the controlling target is the **actual warning-free condition**, not the calculated number.

## K3 — Common Errors Related to Slow Flight

### Entry Pitch Errors

**Error:** Too little back-elevator causes altitude loss; too much causes a climb and rapid speed decay.  
**Correction:** Establish the outside pitch picture progressively and use small corrections while power is reduced.

### Coordination, Scan, and Division of Attention

**Error:** Insufficient right rudder, instrument fixation, or inability to divide attention.  
**Correction:** Use an outside-dominant scan and a repeating cycle: **attitude → traffic → coordination → performance → attitude**.

### Configuration, Power, and Trim Errors

**Error:** Failing to anticipate flap effects, poor power management, or improper trim.  
**Correction:** Respect $V_{FE}$, change configuration incrementally, stabilize attitude/power first, then trim.

### Stall-Warning Error

**Error:** Continuing after a horn, buffet, or other warning.  
**Correction:** **Reduce AOA immediately** enough to remove the warning; adjust power and flightpath as required. Do not normalize flight with a continuous warning.

## Risk Management

### Inadvertent Slow Flight or Stall Warning

**Hazard:** Airspeed decays unnoticed or AOA/load increases while close to critical AOA.  
**Recognition:** Increasing pitch, reduced control effectiveness, speed decay, horn, buffet, or inability to hold altitude.  
**Consequence:** Inadvertent stall, spin, or loss of control.  
**Mitigation:** Maintain a warning-free margin, monitor trends, keep a hand available for power, and reduce AOA promptly at any warning.

### Stall-Warning Range and Limitations

**Hazard:** Treating one warning device as infallible.  
**Recognition:** Warning behavior varies with configuration, load factor, contamination, turbulence, and airplane condition.  
**Consequence:** Delayed recognition or false confidence.  
**Mitigation:** Integrate **sight, sound, feel, control response, buffet, and installed warning devices**. Respond to the earliest valid cue.

### Uncoordinated Flight

**Hazard:** High power and AOA produce strong yaw while stall margin is small.  
**Recognition:** Ball displaced, yaw, wing drop, or excess rudder/aileron.  
**Consequence:** Asymmetric stall or spin entry.  
**Mitigation:** Anticipate right rudder with power, keep the ball centered, and use shallow coordinated banks.

### Environmental Performance

**Hazard:** Turbulence, microbursts, wind shear, or high density altitude reduces margin or power available.  
**Recognition:** Uncommanded AOA/speed change, high power with poor performance, or unstable altitude.  
**Consequence:** Warning activation, stall, or inability to climb.  
**Mitigation:** Increase the margin, avoid hazardous conditions, discontinue an unstable maneuver, and do not demand climb performance the airplane cannot provide.

### Collision, Distraction, and Situational Awareness

**Hazard:** High workload and instrument fixation interrupt traffic scan or orientation.  
**Recognition:** Prolonged head-down time, missed traffic, heading drift, or explanation continuing while control degrades.  
**Consequence:** Collision, airspace deviation, or loss of control.  
**Mitigation:** Clear first, maintain an outside-dominant scan, pause instruction to aviate, and terminate when traffic or orientation requires it.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** While climbing in slow flight, turbulence produces a brief stall-horn chirp and the learner continues pulling to hold the assigned pitch attitude.

**Discuss / Decide:** Which parameter has priority, and should the learner hold pitch, add power, or reduce AOA first?

**Desired Learner Outcome:** **Reduce AOA immediately** enough to eliminate the warning, maintain coordination, then use available power and a safe attitude to reestablish the desired flightpath. The assigned attitude never overrides stall avoidance.

# Fly

## Setup

- **Clear:** Perform clearing turns and scan the area into which the maneuver will proceed.
- **Altitude:** Select an altitude that permits completion no lower than **1,500 feet AGL**.
- **Configuration:** **Cruise configuration** unless otherwise specified by the evaluator; checklist complete.
- **Entry speed:** Below the applicable weight-adjusted $V_A$: **105 KIAS at 2,550 lb**, **98 KIAS at 2,200 lb**, or **90 KIAS at 1,900 lb**.
- **References:** Select outside pitch, heading, and bank references; note traffic, terrain, and airspace.
- **Brief:** Target is **warning-free slow flight**, not a fixed speed; any warning requires immediate AOA reduction.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Reduce power smoothly while applying enough back-elevator to **hold altitude and heading**.
2. Allow speed to decrease; listen and feel for changing airflow and control response.
3. Approaching the target, add power to stop deceleration and maintain altitude.
4. Establish a speed where one further adverse change would cause a warning, but keep the warning **off**. AFH planning target: approximately **5–10 knots above the actual 1G stall speed**.
5. Maintain coordination with appropriate right rudder; stabilize attitude and power, then trim.
6. Demonstrate **straight-and-level flight** within tolerances.
7. Demonstrate **shallow/medium turns** at the specified bank; add power as needed for altitude and prevent warning activation.
8. Demonstrate a **climb** using available excess power while maintaining target airspeed and coordination.
9. Demonstrate a **descent** with coordinated power reduction and pitch adjustment while preserving warning margin.
10. If the evaluator specifies flaps, obey **110 KIAS at 10°** and **85 KIAS beyond 10°**, extend/retract incrementally, and anticipate pitch, power, AOA, and trim changes.
11. Recover: add power, reduce AOA, maintain coordination, level as necessary, retract configuration incrementally if used, accelerate, and retrim.

## Learner Practice

Build the maneuver in stages: entry and stabilization; straight-and-level; turns; climb; descent; recovery; then simultaneous instruction. Require verbal callouts for **warning margin, coordination, power available, traffic, and altitude floor**.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Failure to adequately clear the area** — **Recognize:** no clearing or traffic scan. **Correct:** discontinue, clear, and restart.
2. **Inadequate back-elevator pressure as power is reduced, resulting in altitude loss** — **Recognize:** nose/altitude falls during deceleration. **Correct:** increase back pressure progressively to hold the outside pitch picture.
3. **Excessive back-elevator pressure as power is reduced, resulting in a climb followed by rapid reduction in airspeed** — **Recognize:** altitude rises and speed decays rapidly. **Correct:** reduce AOA smoothly and reestablish the target attitude.
4. **Insufficient right rudder to compensate for left yaw** — **Recognize:** ball left or nose yawing left. **Correct:** apply coordinated right rudder as power/AOA increase.
5. **Fixation on the flight instruments** — **Recognize:** traffic scan and outside attitude disappear. **Correct:** return to outside references and use brief instrument verification.
6. **Failure to anticipate changes in AOA as flaps are extended or retracted** — **Recognize:** balloon, sink, speed change, or warning. **Correct:** change flaps incrementally within limits and coordinate pitch/power.
7. **Inadequate power management** — **Recognize:** altitude or speed trend continues. **Correct:** use prompt, measured power for flightpath while pitch protects airspeed.
8. **Inability to adequately divide attention between airplane control and orientation** — **Recognize:** heading, altitude, traffic, or area awareness is lost. **Correct:** use a deliberate outside-dominant scan and pause instruction.
9. **Failure to properly trim the airplane** — **Recognize:** excessive sustained pressure and imprecise control. **Correct:** establish attitude and power first, then trim away pressure.
10. **Failure to respond to a stall warning** — **Recognize:** horn, buffet, or other warning continues. **Correct:** reduce AOA immediately until the warning stops; adjust power and flightpath afterward.

## Completion Standards

The applicant clears; completes above **1,500 feet AGL**; establishes warning-free slow flight in the specified configuration; performs coordinated straight-and-level flight, turns, climbs, and descents; maintains **altitude ±50 feet**, **heading ±10°**, **airspeed +5/-0 knots**, and **bank ±5°**; recovers smoothly; and teaches and corrects effectively.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. How does the ACS define the target slow-flight condition?

[[answer:
It is an airspeed at which any further increase in AOA, increase in load factor, or reduction in power would result in a stall warning. The maneuver itself must remain warning-free.
]]

### 2. Why is the target not one fixed indicated airspeed?

[[answer:
The warning and stall speeds vary with weight, CG, configuration, load factor, power, turbulence, and airplane condition. The AFH's 5–10-knot margin is a planning target; the actual warning-free condition controls.
]]

### 3. What controls airspeed and flightpath on the back side of the power curve?

[[answer:
Pitch is the more effective control of airspeed, and power is the more effective control of flightpath. Both must be coordinated rather than treated independently.
]]

### 4. What are the C172S flap limits?

[[answer:
Maximum flap-extended speed is 110 KIAS at 10° flaps and 85 KIAS from 10° through 30° flaps.
]]

### 5. Why does slow flight require substantial right rudder?

[[answer:
High power and high AOA strengthen torque, spiraling slipstream, and P-factor while reduced airflow decreases control effectiveness.
]]

### 6. What is the correct response to a stall warning during slow flight?

[[answer:
Reduce AOA immediately enough to remove the warning, maintain coordination, then adjust power and flightpath as necessary. Do not continue normal maneuvering with the warning active.
]]

### 7. What are the CFI ACS tolerances?

[[answer:
Altitude ±50 feet, heading ±10°, airspeed +5/-0 knots, and specified bank ±5°.
]]

### 8. Why can turbulence or high density altitude make the maneuver less safe?

[[answer:
Turbulence can cause sudden AOA and load-factor changes, while high density altitude reduces power available and climb performance. Both reduce the margin for correction.
]]
