# Task F. Cross-Controlled Stall Demonstration

**Area X — Slow Flight, Stalls, and Spins**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands cross-controlled stalls, applies that knowledge, manages associated risks, demonstrates the required skills, and provides effective instruction.

## Purpose

This demonstration exposes the lethal **overshot base-to-final skid**: inside rudder increases yaw, opposite aileron resists bank, and back elevator raises AOA until the inside wing stalls first. The training objective is **recognition and avoidance**, especially the decision to go around rather than force alignment.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **20–30 minutes ground**, **15–20 minutes demonstration**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** C172S POH/checklist, CFI ACS, AFH, AC 61-67C, model airplane, coordination diagram, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain the base-to-final error chain, recognize cross-control cues, demonstrate and recover at altitude, and teach avoidance.  
**Instructor:** Verify airplane approval/limitations, altitude, clearing, and control transfer; closely guard yaw, bank, and incipient-spin development.

## Completion Standards

Clear; complete above **3,000 feet AGL**; close the throttle; establish normal glide and trim; enter a medium-bank turn; apply excess rudder, opposite aileron, and elevator as specified; call the first cue; recover at the evaluator-directed point; and teach the conditions and avoidance strategy.

# Teach

## K1 — Aerodynamics

In a skidding turn, yaw and opposite aileron create unequal AOA and drag. The inside wing travels the shorter path and may be partially blanked; when critical AOA is exceeded, it tends to stall first. The resulting rapid roll and yaw toward the inside of the turn can become an **incipient spin**.

## K2 — Flight Situations

- Overshooting final and using **inside/bottom rudder** to force the nose toward the runway
- Holding bank shallow with **opposite aileron** while pulling to prevent descent
- Improperly correcting a wing drop during another stall
- Maneuvering slowly with distraction, fixation, or poorly coordinated controls

[[highlight:
Pattern Decision |
The recovery for an overshot final is a **go-around**, not a skidding turn. This demonstration belongs at altitude and is never practiced in the traffic pattern.
]]

## K3 — Recognition

Recognize excessive rudder pressure, ball displaced toward the outside of the turn, opposite aileron, increasing back pressure, rapid bank increase, stall horn/buffet, and an abrupt inside-wing drop. The developing motion may be faster and more disorienting than a coordinated stall.

## K4 — Entry and Minimum Altitude

Complete above **3,000 feet AGL**; a local planning entry of at least **3,500 feet AGL** provides margin. Clear above, below, and around the airplane. With the C172S fixed gear down, use **clean configuration** to avoid flap overspeed if an incipient spin develops; close throttle, establish **68 KIAS normal glide**, trim, and select a simulated runway.

## K5 — Recovery

Immediately **reduce AOA**, remove the excessive pro-spin rudder and apply the appropriate anti-yaw/anti-spin rudder as required, then level the wings after the stall breaks and add power. If rotation develops, transition without delay to the airplane-approved spin-recovery procedure.

## K6 — Common Errors

The critical failures are improper setup, failure to create and explain the cross-controlled condition accurately, inadequate recognition/recovery, and failure to emphasize that prevention and go-around are the real operational defenses.

## Risk Management

### Spin Entry
**Hazard:** Intentional skid at high AOA. **Recognition:** yaw, opposite aileron, wing drop, rotation. **Consequence:** rapid spin entry. **Mitigation:** qualified instructor, positive control transfer, high altitude, first-cue recovery unless specifically directed otherwise.

### Environment / Collision / Limitations
**Hazard:** Turbulence, poor visibility, traffic, or flap overspeed during recovery. **Mitigation:** suitable conditions, complete clearing, clean configuration, continuous altitude calls, and immediate termination when margins erode.

### Distraction / Startle
**Hazard:** Dramatic wing drop diverts attention from recovery order. **Mitigation:** brief the expected cues and call **AOA—RUDDER—WINGS—POWER** before entry.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** A tailwind on base causes an overshoot. The learner applies inside rudder and opposite aileron to avoid a steeper bank.  
**Desired outcome:** Recognize the skid immediately, neutralize/coordinate, reduce AOA as needed, and **go around**.

# Fly

## Setup

- Clear above, below, and around; complete above **3,000 feet AGL**; plan entry at least **3,500 feet AGL**.
- Positive control transfer and incipient-spin recovery briefed; autopilot OFF.
- C172S fixed gear down; **flaps UP**; throttle closed.
- Establish and trim **68 KIAS** glide; select a simulated runway/reference.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Fly a simulated descending base leg and intentionally overshoot final.
2. Roll toward final at a **medium bank**.
3. Apply **excess inside rudder** while holding bank with **opposite aileron**.
4. Add back elevator to keep the nose from lowering.
5. Identify skid, yaw, control cross, horn/buffet, and wing-drop tendency.
6. Call the first stall cue; continue only if specifically directed and safe.
7. **Reduce AOA immediately** and remove pro-spin rudder/apply anti-yaw rudder as required.
8. After the stall breaks, level wings, add power, configure, and climb at appropriate $V_X$/$V_Y$.
9. Return to assigned altitude, heading, and airspeed; explain that a go-around prevents the real event.

## Learner Practice

Use instructor demonstration first, then guarded applicant demonstration. The applicant must explain the error chain before touching the controls and state the abort criteria and recovery floor.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Failure to establish the selected configuration** — Verify gear status, flaps up, throttle closed, and glide speed.
2. **Failure to establish a representative cross-controlled condition** — Clearly demonstrate inside rudder, opposite aileron, and increasing elevator while explaining the skid.
3. **Improper recognition or recovery** — Call the first cue; reduce AOA and remove pro-spin yaw immediately.
4. **Failure to emphasize the hazard in gliding/reduced-speed flight** — Tie every demonstration to overshot-final prevention and go-around.
5. **Inadequate clearing or altitude** — Discontinue and reposition; never continue below the floor.
6. **Aggressive aileron before AOA reduction** — Unload first; avoid deepening the inside-wing stall.
7. **Power before reducing AOA/yaw** — Restore airflow and stop pro-spin yaw first.
8. **Failure to regain orientation and assigned flightpath** — Stabilize, scan, and return only after recovery is complete.

## Completion Standards

Demonstrate the cross-controlled condition and its cues, recover as directed above **3,000 feet AGL**, remain within aircraft limitations, explain the base-to-final hazard and go-around decision, and analyze/correct errors.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What creates the cross-controlled stall?
[[answer: Excess rudder into a turn, opposite aileron holding bank, and increasing elevator raise AOA while the airplane skids; the inside wing tends to stall first.]]

### 2. Where does it most commonly occur unintentionally?
[[answer: During an overshot base-to-final turn when the pilot tries to force alignment with inside rudder instead of going around.]]

### 3. What is the minimum completion altitude?
[[answer: 3,000 feet AGL. A higher planning entry, such as 3,500 feet AGL, preserves recovery margin.]]

### 4. What is the recovery priority?
[[answer: Reduce AOA, remove pro-spin yaw/apply appropriate anti-yaw rudder, level after the stall breaks, then add power and return to flight.]]

### 5. Why use flaps up in the C172S demonstration?
[[answer: Clean configuration reduces the risk of exceeding flap limitations if the airplane enters a rapid nose-low or rotating condition.]]

### 6. What is the correct real-world response to an overshot final?
[[answer: Maintain coordinated flight and go around when alignment cannot be safely achieved. Never skid the turn.]]
