# Task I. Spin Awareness and Spins

**Area X — Slow Flight, Stalls, and Spins**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands spins, applies that knowledge, manages associated risks, demonstrates appropriate skills, and provides effective instruction.

## Purpose

Spin training develops recognition and prevention of the **stalled-and-yawing condition** that produces autorotation and, when an intentional spin is authorized and requested, proficiency in the airplane-specific entry and recovery procedure.

At evaluator discretion, a properly certified **logbook record of instructional competency in spin entries, spins, and recoveries** may be accepted instead of the in-flight Task.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **40–60 minutes ground**, **flight only in an approved airplane with a qualified spin instructor**, and **15–20 minutes debrief**.

**Equipment:** Current C172S POH/AFM and supplements, weight-and-balance data, checklist, CFI ACS, AFH, AC 61-67C, secured cabin, airsickness supplies, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Determine aircraft/loading approval, explain spin phases and human factors, identify spin direction, perform the POH entry/recovery if requested, and teach prevention.  
**Instructor:** Verify qualifications, aircraft condition/category/loading, weather, altitude, clearing, occupant readiness, control reach, and positive control transfer; terminate for any uncertainty.

## Completion Standards

The applicant explains all ACS elements, clears, selects an entry altitude allowing completion no lower than **4,000 feet AGL**, and, if requested, enters and recovers from an intentional spin using the approved airplane procedure while providing effective instruction and correcting common errors.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures

The primary goal is **spin prevention**: recognize and correct the stall-plus-yaw condition before autorotation develops. Intentional spins are performed only in an approved airplane, approved loading, suitable conditions, with qualified instruction, adequate altitude, and the exact POH procedure.

## K2 — Aerodynamics

A spin is an aggravated stall with autorotation. Both wings may be stalled, but the descending/inside wing is at higher AOA and produces more drag and less lift. Weight, aft CG, power, configuration, control position, and yaw affect entry, rotation, and recovery. An aft CG reduces stability and rudder/elevator recovery effectiveness.

## K3 — Causes and Phases

**Stall + yaw = spin.** Yaw may come from rudder misuse, adverse yaw, P-factor, torque, slipstream, wake turbulence, or asymmetric loading.

1. **Entry:** Critical AOA is exceeded with yaw/pro-spin controls.
2. **Incipient:** Rotation begins and accelerates; forces are not yet balanced, typically during the first several turns.
3. **Developed:** Rotation rate, airspeed, and descent stabilize.
4. **Recovery:** Anti-spin controls stop rotation and reduce AOA; a steep dive remains to be recovered smoothly.

## K4 — Spin Recovery

Use the **airplane-specific POH**. For the C172S:

1. **Throttle — IDLE; ailerons — NEUTRAL.**
2. **Full rudder opposite rotation — APPLY AND HOLD.**
3. Just after rudder reaches the stop, move the control wheel **briskly forward far enough to break the stall**.
4. **Hold** those inputs until rotation stops.
5. As rotation stops, **neutralize rudder** and recover smoothly from the resulting dive.

[[mnemonic:
PARE — Memory Aid, POH Controls |
**P**ower idle · **A**ilerons neutral · **R**udder full opposite · **E**levator briskly forward. Then neutralize rudder when rotation stops and recover smoothly. The POH remains controlling.
]]

## K5 — Human Factors

Anxiety, startle, disorientation, fixation, nausea, and airsickness can prevent correct control inputs. Brief every action, transfer of control, expected sight/sensation, stopping point, and recovery floor. Confirm occupant readiness and secure airsickness bags before flight.

## K6 — Determining Spin Approval

Check the **POH/AFM limitations, airworthiness category, type certificate, supplements, weight and balance, configuration, and installed equipment**. For the C172S:

- Intentional spins are approved **only in utility category**.
- Maximum utility-category takeoff/landing weight is **2,200 lb**.
- Utility CG must be within the published utility envelope.
- **Rear seat must not be occupied** and **baggage compartment must be empty**.
- Cabin and loose equipment must be secured; occupants must reach full controls.
- **Intentional spins with flaps extended are prohibited**.
- A qualified instructor familiar with C172S spin characteristics is required.

## K7 — Unintentional Spin Situations

- Skidded overshot base-to-final turn
- Uncoordinated power-on stall/departure
- Improper wing-drop correction with aileron
- Secondary stall during recovery
- Distraction during slow, high-AOA maneuvering
- Wake turbulence or abrupt control input near critical AOA

## K8 — Intentional Entry and Minimum Altitude

The ACS requires completion no lower than **4,000 feet AGL**. The C172S POH recommends entries high enough to recover at **4,000 feet AGL or more** and allows at least **1,000 feet** for a one-turn spin and recovery. A conservative local planning altitude is **6,000 feet AGL**, adjusted upward for planned turns and conditions.

Use **clean configuration** and slow deceleration. Near the stall, smoothly move elevator full aft and apply full rudder in the desired direction so full rudder and full aft elevator occur nearly together. As rotation begins, set power idle and neutralize ailerons.

## K9 — Maintaining a Stabilized Spin

Hold the POH-prescribed pro-spin elevator and rudder inputs until the planned recovery point. Maintain ailerons neutral and power idle. Do not extend the spin merely to force stabilization; C172S training uses **one or two turns**, and extended spins may transition toward a spiral.

## K10 — Conditions and Avoidance

Avoid the prerequisite combination: **do not stall while yawing**. Keep turns coordinated, unload at warning, go around after an overshoot, manage trim/power, and do not use aileron against a dropped wing before reducing AOA.

## K11 — Orientation and Reliable Instruments

Use outside rotation cues and maintain altitude/turn count. Gyroscopic instruments may tumble or become unreliable. If disorientation prevents visual determination, the C172S POH permits use of the **symbolic airplane in the turn coordinator** to determine rotation direction. The inclinometer ball alone can be misleading in a spin.

## K12 — Recovery and Minimum Altitude

Initiate PARE/POH recovery early enough to complete no lower than **4,000 feet AGL**. After rotation stops, neutralize rudder and recover from the dive smoothly. Avoid an accelerated or secondary stall and monitor rapidly increasing airspeed/G loading. If the condition becomes a spiral dive, unload slightly, reduce power, roll level, then recover pitch smoothly.

## K13 — Common Errors

Common errors include wrong configuration/loading, incomplete stall at entry, failure to close throttle, missed incipient-spin cues, incorrect controls, disorientation, confusing a spiral with a spin, excessive recovery speed/G, excessive altitude loss, and attempting spins in an unapproved airplane.

## Risk Management

### Approval / Loading / Limitations
**Hazard:** Spin attempted outside approval or utility loading. **Consequence:** unpredictable or unrecoverable behavior/structural damage. **Mitigation:** independently verify POH, W&B, category, seats, baggage, flaps, and equipment before flight.

### Inadvertent Spin / Recovery
**Hazard:** Stall with yaw or incorrect anti-spin input. **Recognition:** low stable airspeed with autorotation and high descent. **Mitigation:** prevent stall/yaw; if spinning, use exact POH controls without delay.

### Environment / Collision / Altitude
**Hazard:** Cloud, poor horizon, turbulence, traffic, or insufficient altitude. **Mitigation:** suitable VMC, broad clearing above/below, conservative entry height, continuous altitude calls, hard 4,000-foot floor.

### Human Factors
**Hazard:** Fear, startle, airsickness, fixation, or disorientation. **Mitigation:** detailed prebrief, consent/readiness check, concise callouts, positive control transfer, and immediate termination when needed.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** During a spin, rotation appears to accelerate and airspeed begins increasing rapidly after several turns.  
**Desired outcome:** Recognize transition toward a **spiral dive**, not hold spin inputs. Recover promptly using the applicable spiral recovery while respecting airspeed and G limits.

# Fly

## Setup

- **Eligibility:** Intentional-spin-approved airplane and qualified instructor; evaluator request or accepted spin-competency logbook record.
- **C172S category/loading:** Utility category; **≤2,200 lb**; utility CG; rear seat empty; baggage empty; flaps up; loose items secured.
- **Occupants:** Belts/harnesses secure, controls fully reachable, airsickness readiness confirmed.
- **Area/weather:** VMC, clear of traffic/clouds, visual horizon, suitable emergency area.
- **Altitude:** Plan approximately **6,000 feet AGL**; complete no lower than **4,000 feet AGL**.
- **Brief:** Direction, number of turns, control transfer, aborts, PARE/POH sequence, spin-versus-spiral cues.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Clear above, below, and around; configure **clean**; reduce power and decelerate.
2. Smoothly approach the power-off stall.
3. Near the break, apply full rudder in the desired spin direction while moving elevator full aft.
4. As rotation begins, **power idle** and **ailerons neutral**.
5. Hold pro-spin rudder/elevator only to the planned recovery point; monitor direction, turns, airspeed, and altitude.
6. Recover using C172S POH: **idle/neutral → full opposite rudder → brisk forward elevator → hold until rotation stops**.
7. Neutralize rudder as rotation stops.
8. Recover smoothly from the dive without accelerated/secondary stall or overspeed.
9. Establish climb and return to assigned flightpath.

## Learner Practice

Conduct only with qualified spin instruction in an approved airplane. Begin with recognition and incipient recovery; limit C172S training spins to **one or two turns**. The applicant must teach throughout without delaying altitude or recovery calls.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Improper configuration before entry** — Verify utility loading, flaps up, power/configuration, and secured cabin.
2. **Failure to achieve/maintain a full stall during entry** — Coordinate timing of full aft elevator and pro-spin rudder per POH.
3. **Failure to close throttle when spin entry is achieved** — Set idle as rotation begins.
4. **Failure to recognize imminent unintentional spin** — Identify stall plus yaw and recover before autorotation.
5. **Improper controls during entry, rotation, or recovery** — Use exact POH sequence; ailerons neutral.
6. **Disorientation** — Use outside cues and turn coordinator symbolic airplane if necessary; maintain count/altitude calls.
7. **Failure to distinguish spiral from spin** — Spin: low/stable speed; spiral: increasing speed and G.
8. **Excessive speed or accelerated stall during recovery** — Smooth dive recovery; avoid abrupt pull.
9. **Failure to recover with minimum altitude loss** — Prompt correct inputs, but never rush into excessive G.
10. **Attempting to spin an unapproved airplane** — Stop; verify category, type approval, POH, W&B, and configuration first.

## Completion Standards

Explain all knowledge/risk elements; verify approval/loading; clear; enter and recover if requested; complete above **4,000 feet AGL** using the C172S POH procedure; maintain orientation and limitations; and analyze/correct common errors.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What two conditions are required for a spin?
[[answer: A stall at or beyond critical AOA and yaw that creates an asymmetric stall and autorotation.]]

### 2. What are the four phases?
[[answer: Entry, incipient, developed, and recovery.]]

### 3. Is every C172S approved for the planned spin as loaded?
[[answer: No. Intentional spins require utility-category operation, no rear-seat occupant, empty baggage compartment, weight at or below 2,200 lb, utility CG, flaps up, and compliance with the specific POH/supplements and aircraft condition.]]

### 4. What is the C172S recovery procedure?
[[answer: Throttle idle and ailerons neutral; full rudder opposite rotation; just after rudder reaches the stop, briskly move the wheel forward enough to break the stall; hold until rotation stops; neutralize rudder; recover smoothly from the dive.]]

### 5. Which instrument identifies direction if visual orientation is lost?
[[answer: The C172S POH permits reference to the symbolic airplane in the turn coordinator. Gyroscopic instruments may be unreliable, and the ball alone may be misleading.]]

### 6. How do spin and spiral indications differ?
[[answer: A developed spin has stalled wings and relatively low/stable airspeed; a spiral dive has flying wings with rapidly increasing airspeed and G loading.]]

### 7. What is the altitude standard?
[[answer: The ACS requires completion no lower than 4,000 feet AGL. The C172S POH recommends recovery at 4,000 feet AGL or more and at least 1,000 feet allowance for a one-turn spin/recovery.]]

### 8. When may the flight demonstration be omitted on the practical test?
[[answer: At evaluator discretion, a properly certified logbook record of instructional competency in spin entries, spins, and recoveries may be accepted in lieu of the Task.]]
