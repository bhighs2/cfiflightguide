# Task D. Power-On Stalls

**Area X — Slow Flight, Stalls, and Spins**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands power-on stalls, applies that knowledge, manages associated risks, demonstrates the required skills while providing effective instruction, and analyzes and corrects common errors.

## Purpose

A power-on stall simulates an inadvertent stall during **takeoff, departure, climb, obstacle clearance, or go-around**. It develops recognition of high-power/high-AOA cues, control of strong yaw effects, and immediate AOA-first recovery without a secondary stall.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **25–35 minutes ground**, **25–35 minutes flight**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** C172S POH/checklist, CFI ACS, AFH, AC 61-67C, approved recovery diagrams, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain departure-stall causes, aerodynamics, cues, prevention, recovery, and C172S speeds; demonstrate straight and turning stalls while teaching.  
**Instructor:** Brief recovery point and altitude floor; guard coordination, pitch rate, and secondary-stall risk; evaluate instruction and control.

## Completion Standards

Configure as specified, use **at least 65% power**, maintain coordination and heading **±10°** or bank **≤20°, ±10°**, call the first cue, recover at the evaluator-directed point, configure per manufacturer, accelerate to $V_X$ or $V_Y$, and return to assigned conditions above **1,500 feet AGL**.

- **AI.X.D.K3** — Stall characteristics as they relate to airplane design, and recognition impending stall and full stall indications using sight, sound, or feel.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures

Slow to a normal lift-off/departure speed in the assigned **takeoff, departure, or cruise configuration**. Apply at least **65% power**, establish the departure attitude, and smoothly increase AOA. Maintain coordination and heading or shallow bank, call the first warning, then recover at the evaluator-directed impending/full-stall point.

## K2 — Aerodynamics

High power and high AOA amplify **torque, spiraling slipstream, and P-factor**, requiring increasing right rudder. Load factor in a turn raises stall speed; weight, CG, configuration, pitch attitude, and abrupt control inputs change the speed and manner of the stall. The wing still stalls only when **critical AOA** is exceeded.

## K3 — Recognition

Recognize changing airflow sound, increasing control displacement, reduced effectiveness, stall horn, buffet, yaw, and possible wing drop. The C172S horn normally sounds **5–10 knots above stall**, but use integrated sight, sound, and feel rather than relying on one device.

## K4 — Causes and Prevention

Typical causes include **excessive rotation**, steep climb, failure to lower the nose after takeoff, improper go-around trim/control, distraction, excessive bank, and inadequate rudder. Prevent with correct rotation speed/attitude, coordinated climb, prompt pitch correction, trim management, and obstacle/go-around discipline.

## K5 — Recovery Fundamentals

**Disconnect automation → reduce AOA until warning stops → level wings/coordinate → confirm or add power as appropriate → configure → accelerate to $V_X$ or $V_Y$ → return to flightpath.** Because power is already high, the essential action is still AOA reduction; power alone cannot recover a stalled wing.

## K6 — Common Errors

Diagnose entry configuration and pitch rate, cue recognition, coordination/right-rudder use, recovery order, secondary stall, excessive altitude/speed loss, and failure to regain the assigned flightpath.

## Risk Management

### Departure Stall / Spin
**Hazard:** High AOA, high power, low speed, and yaw. **Recognition:** excessive pitch, horn/buffet, ball displaced, wing drop. **Consequence:** stall/spin after takeoff. **Mitigation:** coordinated controls, smooth pitch, immediate AOA reduction, and correct climb attitude.

### Warning and Recovery
**Hazard:** Delayed cue recognition or power-first recovery. **Recognition:** warning persists while pitch remains high. **Consequence:** prolonged stall or secondary stall. **Mitigation:** call the first cue and reduce AOA before attempting the climb.

### Environment / Collision / Distraction
**Hazard:** Turbulence, wind shear, density altitude, traffic, or instructional fixation. **Recognition:** abrupt AOA change, poor climb, lost scan. **Consequence:** loss of control or collision. **Mitigation:** suitable conditions, adequate altitude, continuous clearing, and aviate-first discipline.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** During a go-around with nose-up trim, full power produces a rapid pitch-up and left yaw.  
**Desired outcome:** Apply forward elevator and right rudder, manage trim/configuration, preserve AOA margin, and establish the correct climb rather than allowing a power-on stall.

# Fly

## Setup

- Clear; complete recovery above **1,500 feet AGL**.
- Autopilot/wing leveler **OFF**; recovery point and control transfer briefed.
- Use assigned **takeoff/departure/cruise configuration**; C172S takeoff flaps **0°–10°**.
- Slow to normal lift-off reference: **55 KIAS** for the C172S normal takeoff.
- Set **no less than 65% power**; use maximum allowable power when appropriate.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Establish assigned configuration at **55 KIAS** or evaluator-assigned departure speed.
2. Apply at least **65% power** and smoothly establish/increase departure pitch.
3. Maintain heading **±10°** or bank **≤20°, ±10°**; increase right rudder as needed.
4. Call the first horn/buffet/control cue.
5. Recover at first indication or continue to full stall only as directed.
6. **Reduce AOA** until warning is eliminated.
7. **Level wings and coordinate**; confirm/add maximum allowable power.
8. Configure as recommended and accelerate to **$V_X$ 62 KIAS SL / 67 KIAS at 10,000 ft** or **$V_Y$ 74 KIAS SL / 72 KIAS at 10,000 ft**.
9. Return to assigned altitude, heading, and airspeed.

## Learner Practice

Practice impending before full stalls and straight before turning entries. Require callouts for **power, pitch, right rudder, first cue, AOA reduction, warning eliminated, climb speed, and flightpath**.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Failure to adequately clear the area** — Stop, clear, and restart.
2. **Over-reliance on airspeed/slip-skid indications while excluding other cues after recovery** — Restore outside scan and integrate sight, sound, and feel.
3. **Inadvertent accelerated stall by pulling too fast during entry** — Unload; reenter with smooth pitch increase.
4. **Inability to recognize an impending stall** — Recover and review horn, buffet, sound, pressure, and control response.
5. **Failure to prevent a full stall during an impending-stall exercise** — Reduce AOA immediately at first cue.
6. **Failure to maintain constant bank during turning stalls** — Use smooth coordinated control; remain ≤20°.
7. **Failure to maintain rudder coordination** — Increase right rudder with power/AOA; remove yaw.
8. **Recovering before critical AOA during a required full stall** — Call first cue, continue only as directed and safe.
9. **Not disconnecting autopilot/wing leveler** — Disconnect and establish manual control before AOA reduction.
10. **Recovery without prioritizing pitch/AOA** — Reduce AOA first.
11. **Not holding nose-down input until warning is eliminated** — Maintain sufficient forward pressure.
12. **Leveling wings before reducing AOA** — Unload first, then roll.
13. **Recovering with power before reducing AOA** — AOA first; power cannot unstall the wing.
14. **Failure to level wings after warning ends** — Roll level with coordinated controls.
15. **Secondary stall during recovery** — Reduce AOA again; accelerate before increasing load.
16. **Excessive forward elevator / low or negative G** — Use only enough input to eliminate warning.
17. **Excessive airspeed buildup** — Use measured AOA reduction and timely flightpath recovery.
18. **Lost situational awareness / failure to return or follow ATC** — Reorient, comply, and resume teaching only when stable.

## Completion Standards

Use the assigned configuration and **≥65% power**, maintain coordination and heading/bank standards, call the first cue, recover as directed using AOA first, accelerate to $V_X$/$V_Y$, finish above **1,500 feet AGL**, and return to assigned conditions while teaching effectively.

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What does a power-on stall simulate?
[[answer: An inadvertent stall during takeoff, departure, climb, obstacle clearance, or go-around.]]

### 2. Why is coordination especially important?
[[answer: High power and high AOA strengthen left-turning tendencies; yaw at the stall can produce asymmetric wing stall and spin entry.]]

### 3. What power is required by the ACS?
[[answer: No less than 65 percent power.]]

### 4. What are the heading and bank standards?
[[answer: Heading ±10° in straight flight, or specified bank not exceeding 20° and within ±10° in turning flight.]]

### 5. What is the first recovery action?
[[answer: Reduce AOA sufficiently to eliminate the stall warning; power alone cannot unstall the wing.]]

### 6. What C172S speeds complete the recovery?
[[answer: Accelerate to the appropriate climb speed: VX 62 KIAS at sea level, increasing to 67 KIAS at 10,000 feet; or VY 74 KIAS at sea level, decreasing to 72 KIAS at 10,000 feet.]]
