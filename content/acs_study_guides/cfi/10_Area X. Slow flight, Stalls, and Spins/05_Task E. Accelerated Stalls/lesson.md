# Task E. Accelerated Stalls

**Area X — Slow Flight, Stalls, and Spins**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands **power-on and power-off accelerated stalls**, applies that knowledge, manages associated risks, demonstrates the required skills, and provides effective instruction.

## Purpose

An accelerated stall demonstrates that a wing can reach **critical AOA at an airspeed above its 1G stall speed** whenever load factor increases. It develops immediate recognition and recovery for abrupt pullups, steep turns, botched stall/spin recoveries, and overshot base-to-final turns.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **25–35 minutes ground**, **20–30 minutes flight**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** C172S POH/checklist, ACS, AFH, AC 61-67C, V-g/load-factor diagrams, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain load factor and $V_A$; demonstrate a coordinated 45° entry and immediate first-cue recovery while teaching.  
**Instructor:** Verify speed/configuration/altitude; prevent yaw, excessive G, full stall, or spin; evaluate recognition and recovery.

## Completion Standards

Complete above **3,000 feet AGL**, configure as specified, remain at or below applicable $V_A$, establish a coordinated **45° bank**, increase back pressure smoothly and firmly to an **impending stall**, call the first cue, recover immediately per the POH/AFM, accelerate to $V_X$ or $V_Y$, and return to assigned conditions.

# Teach

## K1 — Purpose and Procedures

At or below $V_A$, establish a coordinated **45° bank** and smoothly but firmly increase back pressure until the **first stall indication**. Call the cue and recover immediately. This is not a full-stall exercise.

## K2 — Aerodynamics

Load factor increases when the pilot changes the flightpath. In a level 45° turn the wing supports approximately **1.4 G**, requiring greater lift and AOA; therefore critical AOA is reached at a higher indicated speed. Configuration, power, weight, CG, attitude, and yaw affect the entry and stall behavior.

**Never demonstrate an accelerated stall with flaps extended.** Flaps-down structural load limits are lower, and the AFH directs accelerated-stall practice with flaps retracted.

## K3 — Recognition

An accelerated stall may have a **sharper buffet and more abrupt break** than a 1G stall. Recognize horn, buffet, rapidly increasing control force, yaw, or wing drop by sight, sound, and feel. Recover at the first cue before a full stall develops.

## K4 — Causes and Prevention

Common causes are abrupt pullups, steep/loaded turns, aggressive dive recovery, overshot base-to-final corrections, and premature pullout from a stall or spin. Prevent with smooth loading, coordinated flight, weight-adjusted $V_A$, conservative low-altitude maneuvering, and early go-around decisions.

## K5 — Recovery Fundamentals

**Reduce AOA immediately**, maintain coordination, then level the wings and adjust power/configuration as appropriate. If a wing drops while stalled, unload first and oppose yaw with rudder; do not deepen the stall with aggressive aileron. Accelerate before increasing load to avoid a secondary stall.

## K6 — Common Errors

The principal task-specific failures are wrong configuration/speed, poor recognition or recovery demonstration, and inadequate teaching of the hazards. The full intentional-stall error set is analyzed in Fly.

## Risk Management

### Accelerated Stall / Spin
**Hazard:** High load factor and yaw near critical AOA. **Recognition:** sharp buffet, horn, wing drop, skid. **Consequence:** abrupt stall or spin. **Mitigation:** coordinated 45° entry, smooth loading, and first-cue recovery.

### $V_A$ and Structural Limits
**Hazard:** Entering above the weight-adjusted maneuvering speed or with flaps extended. **Recognition:** wrong speed/configuration. **Consequence:** structural overload before stall. **Mitigation:** calculate $V_A$ for weight; use clean configuration; never exceed limitations.

### Environment / Collision / Distraction
**Hazard:** Turbulence adds load; density altitude reduces recovery performance; fixation interrupts scan. **Mitigation:** use suitable conditions, clear continuously, complete above 3,000 AGL, and terminate unstable entries.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** During a steep descending recovery, the pilot abruptly pulls to avoid losing altitude and feels a sharp buffet.  
**Desired outcome:** Recognize an accelerated stall onset, reduce AOA immediately, coordinate and level, then recover the flightpath smoothly without another high-G pull.

# Fly

## Setup

- Clear; complete the maneuver above **3,000 feet AGL**.
- Autopilot/wing leveler **OFF**; clean configuration unless evaluator specifies otherwise; **flaps retracted** for the accelerated-stall demonstration.
- Establish speed **at or below weight-adjusted $V_A$: 105 KIAS at 2,550 lb, 98 KIAS at 2,200 lb, 90 KIAS at 1,900 lb**.
- Set power appropriate to configuration and brief **first-indication recovery only**.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Establish a coordinated, level **45° bank**.
2. Smoothly and firmly increase elevator back pressure while maintaining coordination.
3. Call the **first horn, buffet, or other valid cue**.
4. Immediately **reduce AOA** to eliminate the warning.
5. Level the wings with coordinated controls after unloading; adjust power as required.
6. Configure per manufacturer and accelerate to **$V_X$ 62 KIAS SL / 67 KIAS at 10,000 ft** or **$V_Y$ 74 KIAS SL / 72 KIAS at 10,000 ft**.
7. Return to assigned altitude, heading, and airspeed.

## Learner Practice

Practice the 45° setup and smooth loading separately before completing the maneuver. Require callouts for **$V_A$, bank, coordination, first cue, unload, wings level, and recovery speed**.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Failure to adequately clear the area** — Stop, clear, and restart.
2. **Over-reliance on instruments while excluding other cues after recovery** — Restore outside scan and integrate sight, sound, and feel.
3. **Pulling too fast and producing an unintended abrupt stall** — Unload; reenter with smooth, firm pressure.
4. **Failure to recognize an impending stall** — Review cues and recover at the first indication.
5. **Failure to prevent a full stall during the impending-stall maneuver** — Reduce AOA immediately at first cue.
6. **Failure to maintain 45° bank** — Stabilize the coordinated turn before loading; avoid chasing bank while near stall.
7. **Failure to maintain rudder coordination** — Remove yaw; terminate a skidding entry.
8. **Continuing beyond the first cue** — Recover immediately; this Task is impending-stall only.
9. **Autopilot/wing leveler not disconnected** — Establish manual control before entry.
10. **Recovery without prioritizing AOA** — Unload first.
11. **Releasing nose-down input before warning ends** — Maintain reduced AOA until airflow is restored.
12. **Leveling wings before reducing AOA** — Unload first, then roll.
13. **Adding power before reducing AOA** — AOA first; power cannot unstall the wing.
14. **Failure to level after AOA reduction** — Roll level with coordinated controls.
15. **Secondary stall during recovery** — Unload again; accelerate before pulling.
16. **Excessive forward elevator / low or negative G** — Use only enough input to eliminate warning.
17. **Excessive airspeed buildup** — Recover flightpath smoothly after warning ends.
18. **Lost situational awareness / failure to return or follow ATC** — Reorient, comply, and stabilize before teaching resumes.

## Completion Standards

Complete above **3,000 feet AGL**, remain at or below applicable $V_A$, hold a coordinated **45° bank**, recover at the first cue per POH/AFM, accelerate to $V_X$/$V_Y$, and return to assigned conditions while teaching effectively.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. Why can a stall occur above the published 1G stall speed?
[[answer: Increased load factor requires more lift and AOA, so critical AOA is reached at a higher indicated speed.]]

### 2. What bank and recovery point does the ACS require?
[[answer: A coordinated 45° bank and recovery at the first indication of a stall.]]

### 3. Why must entry remain at or below weight-adjusted VA?
[[answer: Above VA the airplane may exceed structural load limits before the wing stalls under a full control input. VA decreases with weight.]]

### 4. Why are flaps retracted?
[[answer: Accelerated stalls should not be practiced with flaps extended because the flaps-down design load limit is lower.]]

### 5. What is the first recovery action?
[[answer: Reduce AOA immediately; then restore coordinated wings-level flight and adjust power/configuration.]]

### 6. Where do inadvertent accelerated stalls occur?
[[answer: Abrupt pullups, steep turns, overshot base-to-final corrections, dive recoveries, and premature pullouts from stalls or spins.]]
