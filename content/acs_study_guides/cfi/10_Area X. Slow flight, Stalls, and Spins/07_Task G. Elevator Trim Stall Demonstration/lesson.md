# Task G. Elevator Trim Stall Demonstration

**Area X — Slow Flight, Stalls, and Spins**

## Objective

Determine that the applicant understands elevator trim stalls, applies that knowledge, manages associated risks, demonstrates the required skills, and provides effective instruction.

## Purpose

This demonstration shows how **nose-up landing trim**, sudden go-around power, propeller slipstream, thrust-line effects, and high workload can produce an uncommanded pitch-up toward a power-on stall. The operational lesson is to anticipate control force, establish go-around pitch immediately, coordinate, configure, and trim only after control is secure.

## Schedule / Equipment

**Suggested:** **20–30 minutes ground**, **15–20 minutes demonstration**, **10 minutes debrief**.  
**Equipment:** C172S POH/checklist, CFI ACS, AFH, AC 61-67C, elevator-trim diagram, and maneuver card.

## Learner Actions / Instructor Actions

**Learner:** Explain trim-stall aerodynamics and go-around risk; demonstrate recognition/recovery while teaching avoidance.  
**Instructor:** Brief altitude, control force, recovery point, and positive control transfer; guard pitch, yaw, and flap retraction.

## Completion Standards

Clear; complete above **3,000 feet AGL**; establish landing configuration, normal glide, and trim; apply maximum allowable power as in a go-around; acknowledge the first cue; recover as directed; adjust trim; return to the desired flightpath; and teach avoidance and errors.

# Teach

## K1 — Aerodynamics

In a low-power approach, the airplane is trimmed nose-up for slow flight. Applying maximum power changes pitching moment and increases propeller airflow/downwash over the tail. The existing trim force, power effect, and high AOA can drive the nose sharply upward unless the pilot applies forward elevator.

## K2 — Flight Situations

- Go-around from a trimmed approach
- Power-off stall recovery with landing trim
- Abrupt power application during slow flight
- Distraction during configuration change
- Delayed forward pressure or failure to manage trim

## K3 — Recognition

Recognize increasing forward-control force, rapid nose rise beyond normal climb attitude, increasing right-rudder demand, speed decay, horn/buffet, and inability to hold the intended go-around pitch.

## K4 — Entry and Minimum Altitude

Complete above **3,000 feet AGL**; plan at least **3,500 feet AGL**. Retard throttle, configure for landing within $V_{FE}$, establish **60–70 KIAS** normal approach with flaps 30° or assigned glide speed, and trim for the glide. Apply maximum allowable power as in a go-around.

## K5 — Recovery

Apply sufficient **forward elevator** to reduce AOA and establish a safe climb attitude, maintain right rudder/coordination, retract flaps incrementally, and adjust trim nose-down only while maintaining positive control. Recover at the evaluator-specified first indication or full stall, then accelerate to $V_X$/$V_Y$.

## K6 — Common Errors

Key errors are inadequate instruction about torque/trim hazards, insufficient altitude, incorrect configuration, and poor recognition/recovery. Related errors include power before AOA control, overreliance on trim, abrupt flap retraction, loss of coordination, and secondary stall.

## Risk Management

### Go-Around Pitch-Up / Stall
**Hazard:** Maximum power with nose-up landing trim. **Recognition:** rapid pitch-up, heavy forward force, speed decay, horn. **Consequence:** power-on stall/spin. **Mitigation:** anticipate forward pressure and right rudder; set climb attitude before trimming.

### Trim Misuse / Aircraft Limitations
**Hazard:** Attempting to recover primarily with trim or retracting flaps abruptly. **Consequence:** delayed AOA reduction, excessive force, sink, or secondary stall. **Mitigation:** fly with elevator first, then trim; configure incrementally within $V_{FE}$.

### Environment / Collision / Distraction
**Hazard:** Turbulence, high density altitude, traffic, or checklist fixation during high workload. **Mitigation:** adequate altitude, suitable conditions, continuous scan, and aviate-first prioritization.

## Scenario Application

**Situation:** A balked landing occurs with full flaps and substantial nose-up trim. The pilot applies full power but reaches for trim while the nose rises rapidly.  
**Desired outcome:** Apply forward elevator and right rudder first, set go-around attitude, retract flaps incrementally, then trim while accelerating.

# Fly

## Setup

- Clear; plan entry ≥**3,500 feet AGL** and complete >**3,000 feet AGL**.
- Autopilot OFF; positive control transfer and recovery point briefed.
- Retard throttle; configure for landing within **$V_{FE}$ 110 KIAS at 10° / 85 KIAS beyond 10°**.
- Establish and trim a **60–70 KIAS**, flaps-30° approach/glide or evaluator-assigned condition.

## Instructor Demonstration

1. Stabilize the trimmed landing glide and identify the go-around scenario.
2. Advance throttle smoothly to **maximum allowable power**.
3. Demonstrate the developing nose-up force while guarding the controls; do not allow unsafe pitch/yaw.
4. Call the first horn, buffet, or excessive-pitch cue.
5. Recover at the evaluator-directed point: **forward elevator / reduce AOA**, right rudder, wings level.
6. Establish go-around pitch; retract flaps incrementally per procedure.
7. Adjust nose-down trim while maintaining elevator control.
8. Accelerate to **$V_X$ 62 KIAS SL / 67 KIAS at 10,000 ft** or **$V_Y$ 74 KIAS SL / 72 KIAS at 10,000 ft**.
9. Return to desired flightpath and explain prevention.

## Learner Practice

Use instructor demonstration followed by closely guarded applicant practice. Require the applicant to call **power—forward pressure—right rudder—pitch—configure—trim—climb**.

## Analyze and Correct Common Errors

1. **Failure to emphasize torque and up-trim hazards** — Connect high power, nose-up trim, pitch-up, yaw, and spin risk.
2. **Performing below minimum safe altitude** — Discontinue; preserve the 3,000-foot completion floor.
3. **Failure to establish landing configuration and trimmed glide** — Stabilize before applying power.
4. **Inadequate recognition/recovery demonstration** — Guard the pitch-up, call the cue, and reduce AOA promptly.
5. **Trying to recover with trim instead of elevator** — Apply forward elevator first; trim only relieves controlled pressure.
6. **Insufficient right rudder** — Anticipate left yaw with maximum power.
7. **Abrupt flap retraction** — Retract incrementally after control and climb are established.
8. **Secondary stall or failure to regain flightpath** — Accelerate before increasing AOA; complete the go-around sequence.

## Completion Standards

Demonstrate the landing-trim go-around pitch-up, identify the first cue, recover as directed above **3,000 feet AGL**, control yaw/AOA, configure and trim correctly, return to the desired flightpath, and teach avoidance.

# Review

## Oral / Checkride Review

### 1. What creates the pitch-up tendency?
[[answer: Nose-up landing trim combined with changed thrust-line pitching moment and increased propeller airflow/downwash over the tail when go-around power is applied.]]

### 2. What is the first corrective control?
[[answer: Forward elevator to control pitch and reduce AOA; trim follows after the airplane is under control.]]

### 3. Why is right rudder important?
[[answer: Maximum power and high AOA increase left-turning tendencies; uncorrected yaw can turn the pitch-up into an uncoordinated stall/spin.]]

### 4. What is the minimum completion altitude?
[[answer: 3,000 feet AGL.]]

### 5. What C172S speed limits apply?
[[answer: VFE is 110 KIAS at 10° and 85 KIAS beyond 10°; the normal flaps-30° approach range is 60–70 KIAS.]]

### 6. What is the prevention sequence during a real go-around?
[[answer: Apply power while controlling pitch and yaw, establish the go-around attitude, retract flaps incrementally, accelerate, then trim.]]
